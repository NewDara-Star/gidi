# Lagos, one local government at a time: research for Gidi

13 October 2026. Twenty researchers, one per local government area (LGA), each asked what makes the place itself: buildings, landmarks, roads, markets, transport, sound and smell, people, food, history, danger, environment, sacred things, pop culture, what Gidi can borrow and what to avoid. The full reports are below the summary, unedited.

**How far to trust this.** Every researcher cited only pages it opened. A shared search limit ran out partway through, so most reports are thin on food, nightlife, pop culture and sound, and they mark those parts *[unverified]* or *[unsourced]*. Treat those as prompts for Daramola's own memory, not facts. One correction a researcher made: the Mile 12 clash was March 2016, not 2017.

Maps: `lagos-lgas-core.png` (inner Lagos, coloured by zone) and `lagos-lgas-state.png` (the whole state). Boundaries © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL).

## The 20, in one line each

**Island side**
- **Lagos Island:** the royal and trading heart. The Oba's palace, Afro-Brazilian houses rotting next to bank towers, the biggest cloth markets, porters carrying everything on their heads because trucks can't get in.
- **Eti-Osa** (Ikoyi, VI, Lekki, Ajah): the richest ground in Nigeria, built on marsh and on the ruins of evicted fishing villages (Maroko 1990, Otodo-Gbame 2017). Gated estates, a toll bridge, the toll gate shooting of 2020.
- **Ibeju-Lekki:** farms and fishing villages becoming "the new Lagos". A mega-refinery, a deep-sea port, endless estates, and a land war where every plot has two owners.
- **Epe:** an old lagoon fish town where the toll road ends. Fish market at dawn, barges, canoes, a mostly Muslim town split into two old halves.

**Inner mainland**
- **Lagos Mainland** (Yaba, Ebute-Metta, Makoko): colonial railway town, markets, the tech district, a sawmill on the water and a fishing village on stilts under the long bridge.
- **Surulere:** middle-class, football, stage and music history. Ojuelegba junction, the decaying national stadium, union succession fights.
- **Mushin:** dense working-class Yoruba district. Africa's biggest spare-parts market, "ticketing" turf, street fights that grow into blackout riots.
- **Shomolu** (Bariga, Pedro, Akoka): thousands of print shops in people's homes, forged documents at 4 a.m., a canal that divides two gangs, stilt houses under the bridge, the birthplace of a street-rap label.
- **Oshodi-Isolo:** the place every route passes through. A market and motor-park crossroads bulldozed into a giant bus terminal, with the "under bridge" world still beneath it.
- **Apapa:** the port. A colonial garden suburb strangled by trucks and tankers, 58 extortion points on one road, a lawless night market under a bridge.
- **Ajeromi-Ifelodun** (Ajegunle): "Jungle City". Reclaimed marsh between the port and two expressways, about 81,000 people per km², the birthplace of a whole music scene.

**Outer mainland**
- **Ikeja:** the state capital. Government at Alausa, Africa's biggest phone market, the airport, the army barracks, a live-music shrine, an all-night strip.
- **Agege:** railway trading town. Sweet bread hawked at dawn, the cattle market and abattoir, a big northern community by the tracks.
- **Ifako-Ijaiye:** border suburb. A 1915 colonial waterworks, gridlock, floods, and pipeline fires from fuel theft.
- **Alimosho:** the biggest and loudest commuter sprawl, about 4 million people. Market roundabouts, a landfill, riots when the task force seizes bikes.
- **Kosofe** (Mile 12, Ketu, Ojota, Magodo): the main road in from the north. The food market where northern trucks unload, a protest park, gated estates, creek villages reachable only by boat.
- **Amuwo-Odofin** (Festac, Mile 2, Trade Fair): a 1977 government showpiece estate gone to seed, the internet-scam reputation, tanker queues, a prison, pipelines under the houses.
- **Ojo:** the far-west trading corridor. A self-governing electronics market with its own court and container jail, a barracks, a university, a broken expressway.
- **Badagry:** the frontier. Slave-route history, coconut beaches, and a checkpoint-heavy road to the Benin border where rice and fuel are smuggled every day.
- **Ikorodu:** the huge north-east town on the lagoon. Older royal and masquerade traditions than the city around it, a million commuters down one road, ferries, and a 2016–17 wave of night killings by a gang.

## What runs through all of them (and what Gidi should take)

1. **The okada ban is real geography, and it's the best system we have.** From 2022, okada were banned in the inner areas (Lagos Island, Eti-Osa, Ikeja, Surulere, Lagos Mainland, Apapa, then Mushin, Shomolu, Oshodi-Isolo, Kosofe). The outer areas mostly still allow them (the researchers found Ojo, Amuwo-Odofin, Badagry, Epe, Alimosho and Agege outside the confirmed lists; Ikorodu, Ifako-Ijaiye and Ibeju-Lekki are uncertain). The state crushed thousands of seized bikes in public. **For Gidi:** the inner city is where the money is and where Tunde's bike can be taken; the outer city is okada heartland. Every run crosses that line.
2. **Each district has its own market, and that tells us what the oja is.** Cloth (Lagos Island), phones (Ikeja), electronics and second-hand goods (Ojo), car parts (Mushin, Amuwo-Odofin), food trucks from the north (Kosofe), fish (Epe), cattle and meat (Agege), clothes bales at dawn (Alimosho), print and forged papers (Shomolu), fuel (Ifako-Ijaiye, Amuwo-Odofin), rice over the border (Badagry), containers (Apapa). A delivery game set in Gidi can give every district its own cargo, timing and danger.
3. **The same disasters repeat, so they're the world's events, not one-off missions.** Demolitions at dawn (Makoko, Otodo-Gbame, Oshodi, Badia, Oworonshoki, Alaba Rago, Trade Fair). Floods every rainy season, everywhere. Market fires (Balogun, Boundary). Pipeline fires from fuel theft (Abule-Egba 2006, Ijegun 2019, Abule-Ado 2020). Tanker fires on bridges (Apapa). Riots that start with an okada accident (Mile 12) or a bike seizure (Iyana-Ipaja). Union factions fighting over a motor park (Idumota, Ijesha). **For Gidi:** a living city where these can happen while you're mid-run.
4. **Who you pay is the real map.** Agberos collect from every bus and okada; one road into the port had 58 collection points (police, traffic officers, union, area boys, the council). Markets have their own courts, jails and task forces (Ojo). Market women leaders lend money and deal with politicians (Lagos Island). **For Gidi:** "the toll run" is a core mission type, and Tunde's boss is one collector among many.
5. **Water is a second road network.** Canoes in the stilt villages, ferries from Ikorodu and Badagry, creek villages reachable only by boat (Kosofe), speedboat smuggling (Ojo, Badagry). Water hyacinth clogs it. **For Gidi:** a boat layer later, and hiding places the police can't drive to.
6. **Old and new sit side by side everywhere.** Rotting Brazilian houses beside bank towers; a fishing village beside billionaire estates; a 1915 waterworks beside a new metro; a flyover over an old junction. Gidi's look should always show both.
7. **Real memorials and real grief.** The 2002 armoury explosion (over 1,100 dead, most drowned in a canal covered in hyacinth), the Lekki toll gate shooting, the Abule-Ado blast, the 2012 plane crash in Iju-Ishaga, the 2014 church guesthouse collapse. Gidi can have its own invented past that echoes these, but never re-enacts them or uses the real victims.

## The sacred list (never criminal, never a joke, never a gang)

From all 20 reports: **Eyo** (Lagos Island), **Oro** (Ikorodu, Epe, Ibeju-Lekki, Alimosho, Ojo; curfews when women and outsiders must stay in), **Egungun**, **Gelede**, **Igunnuko** (Kosofe, Badagry), **Zangbeto** night guardians (Badagry), the **Kayo-Kayo** festival and its torch (Epe), **Ifa** divination, **Ogun** and **Esu** shrines, every **oba, baale and chief**, mosques and churches, and the slave-route sites in Badagry.

What we can do: invent our own festivals, masquerades and curfews. An invented curfew night that empties a district and changes the roads is fair game. Showing what's behind a real rite, or making a real tradition the villain, is not. *Gangs of Lagos* was sued for ₦10 billion for showing Eyo as a gang; a Badagry community made a crime film change its title.

## Things you didn't list that the research turned up

- **Okada ban zones**, which decide where Tunde can work.
- **Traffic task forces and bike-crushing yards.**
- **Market governance**: market courts, container jails, market women leaders, union factions.
- **Rail**: the new Red and Blue metro lines, the old railway towns (Ebute-Metta, Agege).
- **Ferries and canoes.**
- **Floods, blackouts and generators** as daily life.
- **Land**: omo onile, land grabbers, demolitions, the rich estates built on cleared land.
- **Ethnic mix**: northern communities (Agege, Mile 12, Idi-Araba), Igbo traders (Alaba, Ladipo, Computer Village), Egun people (Makoko, Badagry), Awori and Ijebu natives. The reports all warn the same thing: never make an ethnic group the criminal class.
- **Music**: AJ City's street sound, Fela's shrine, Wizkid's "Ojuelegba", Bariga street rap. Gidi's radio stations.
- **Smells**: fish, sawdust, ink, bread at dawn, rotting tomatoes, abattoir gutters, diesel.

# The 20 reports

## Lagos Island LGA: research brief for Gidi

**Note on method:** The shared web-search budget ran out partway through, so a few sections draw on general knowledge rather than pages I opened. Those claims are marked *[unsourced]*. Everything else comes from the pages listed in Sources.

#### 1. In one line
This is the old royal and trading heart of Lagos. Inside about 9 km² it holds the Oba's palace, Brazilian-returnee houses, the bank towers on the Marina and the crush of the biggest markets. It is very dense, at about 34,000 people per km² (2022 estimate).

#### 2. Areas inside it
- **Isale Eko:** the historic core. A tight web of narrow streets and family compounds that grew up before any planning. Old quarters include Iga, Idunmota, Olowogbowo, Offin, Faji and Ereko.
- **Marina / Broad Street:** the office and banking strip on the waterfront.
- **Idumota:** the market and traffic knot where Carter Bridge comes onto the island.
- **Campos / Popo Aguda:** the Brazilian Quarter, now mostly crumbling or replaced by shops.
- **Eastern side:** markets, poor housing and overcrowding. The Oba lives here. Strictly, the eastern half of the island is Lagos Island East LCDA.
- **Obalende:** Wikipedia places it in Eti-Osa (Ikoyi–Obalende LCDA), not in Lagos Island LGA. It is crowded, sits next to police and army barracks, and is known for nightlife, a red-light district and suya at "Suya Junction".

#### 3. Landmarks and monuments
- **Iga Idunganran:** the Oba's palace. The name means "palace on the pepper farm". It was first built in 1670, refurbished by the Portuguese with tiles brought from Portugal, and given a modern wing in 1960. It has an Esu shrine at the exit gate and an Ogun shrine in the grounds.
- **Lagos Central Mosque,** Nnamdi Azikiwe Street (1988): a gold-plated aluminium dome 15 m across and four minarets.
- **Cathedral Church of Christ** (Anglican, 1924–46): Norman Gothic, with the largest organ in Nigeria.
- **Tinubu Square** on Broad Street: once called Independence Square. It is fenced, has two fountains and a statue of Madam Tinubu.
- **Freedom Park:** built on the old Broad Street Prison. Prison walls and a watchtower survive, alongside an Eyo statue, a music stage and Nigeria's first skatepark (2024).
- **Ilojo Bar** near Tinubu Square: an Afro-Brazilian national monument, bulldozed on 11 September 2016 in what Wikipedia calls "suspicious" circumstances.
- **Marina towers:** National House, Investment House and the First Bank headquarters.
- **Bridges and markets:** Carter, Eko and Third Mainland bridges; Balogun and Idumota markets.

#### 4. Buildings and streets
- **Afro-Brazilian houses:** brick, with arches over doors and windows and wooden shutters. Most are now decaying. Named examples are Lumpik House, Doherty House and Water House, whose family sold water from its well.
- **Marina high-rises:** sit on piled or raft foundations because the soil is soft.
- **Market streets:** narrow alleys that delivery vehicles rarely enter. Goods move on carts and on porters' heads.
- **Family compounds:** many have been turned into shops.

#### 5. Roads, junctions, bridges, traffic
- **Carter Bridge** (1901, rebuilt in the late 1970s) runs from Iddo to Idumota. Roadside parking caused congestion and damage, and engineers warned in 2003 that it could collapse. Commercial vehicles were banned from entering the island over it in 2006.
- **Eyo Day closures:** the road from the end of Carter Bridge to Tinubu Square is shut to traffic.
- **Main streets:** Nnamdi Azikiwe Street and Broad Street.
- A Marina bus stop and the CMS Motor Park appear in Wikipedia photo captions.

#### 6. Economy and markets
- **Formal:** most Nigerian banks have their head offices here, including First Bank and UBA.
- **Balogun market:** fabrics, shoes and clothes. Each section is organised by trade guild and run by Iyalojas, market women leaders who settle disputes, lend money and deal with politicians. Trade is still mostly cash, with POS machines growing. Traders source goods in Guangzhou and Istanbul and sell on to Ghana and Cameroon.
- **Fires:** Balogun has burned repeatedly, mostly in the dry season (November 2019, January 2020, December 2025).

#### 7. Transport
- **Okada ban:** commercial motorcycles have been banned in Lagos Island LGA since 1 June 2022, after the lynching of a passenger in Lekki. Past bans "fizzled out" months later.
- **Rail:** the Blue Line runs from Marina to Mile 2, 13 km in phase one, with a test ride in December 2022.
- **Danfo, BRT and ferries** are the other main modes *[unsourced; ferries from the Marina/CMS jetties need checking]*.

#### 8. Sound, smell, look
- Hawkers calling out, horns blaring, and a smell of fabric, sweat and diesel (Discover Lagos).
- *[Unsourced]:* a crush of traders at dawn, a weekday office rush on the Marina, and markets and the business district going quiet after dark.

#### 9. People, languages, religion
- Indigenous Lagosians (Awori and Eko families, plus the Idejo landowning chiefs), Brazilian and Sierra Leonean returnees, and migrant traders.
- Islam and Anglican/Catholic Christianity both have major sites here.
- *[Unsourced]:* Yoruba and Pidgin dominate, with Igbo common among market traders.

#### 10. Food and nightlife
- Obalende suya, at Suya Junction.
- Ilojo Bar was historically the social centre of the Brazilian community.
- Freedom Park hosts music events.
- *[Unsourced]:* buka food (amala, ewa agoyin) in market lanes.

#### 11. History
- Lagos began as the Yoruba fishing village of Eko.
- Oba Gabaro moved the seat of government from Iddo to the island.
- Brazilian returnees settled in Campos in the 19th century.
- Britain made Lagos a colony in 1861.
- Lagos Island was the capital until 1957.
- **21 October 2020:** during the #EndSARS protests, thugs looted the Oba's palace and carried off traditional instruments of office.

#### 12. Danger and law
- **January 2022, Idumota:** rival groups fought with gunfire and broken bottles, and trading stopped. Police arrested Kunle Poly and Tafa Sego, NURTW (road transport union) figures, over a factional union clash in which one person was killed (some reports said two).
- **Responders:** the Rapid Response Squad, the state Taskforce and Area A Command.
- **Game takeaway:** motor-park unions fighting over turf is a reported, real dynamic.

#### 13. Environment
- The island sits on the Lagos Lagoon, with Apapa harbour facing its western side.
- The soil is soft, as the tower foundations show.
- Markets burn in the dry season.
- *[Unsourced]:* flash floods on Marina and Broad Street, and unreliable grid power.

#### 14. Festivals and sacred things (FLAGGED)
**Eyo** is a masquerade festival unique to Lagos. The white-robed masquerades represent the spirits of the dead and escort a dead Oba or chief.
- **Route:** Idumota to Iga Idunganran.
- **Groups:** Adimu is the senior group. Laba (red), Oniko (yellow), Ologede (green) and Agere (purple) parade earlier in the week.
- **Rules:** okada, bicycles, sandals, smoking, caps on men and head-ties on women are all banned near the masquerades. The masquerades beat offenders with their staffs.
- **Do not portray as criminal:** Eyo masquerades, the Oba and the palace, Ogun and Esu shrines, the Araba (the chief priest who performs the palace rites every 16 days), the Central Mosque and the cathedral.

#### 15. In pop culture
I could not verify anything before the search budget ran out *[unsourced]*. Candidates to check: Idumota as the old hub for selling Nollywood VCDs, and Fela-era Lagos Island.

#### 16. What Gidi can borrow
1. **Masquerade Day (set piece):** an invented festival in white robes closes the main artery from bridge to palace. Riding an okada or smoking near the procession draws staff beatings. It forces route changes and stealth riding.
2. **Union turf war (missions):** two motor-park factions fight over a market junction, and Tunde's boss is in one of them. The market shuts down mid-mission.
3. **Alley delivery (mission type):** trucks can't get in, so you ride oja through market lanes, or swap to a cart or head-porter mini-game.
4. **Okada ban zone (district rule):** the old island is "banned" for bikes, so police heat is higher there. The ban was fizzling in real life, so enforcement can rise and fall.
5. **District contrast:** glass bank towers on the waterfront sit 300 m from crumbling arched, shuttered returnee houses and a fire-scarred cloth market.
6. **Character:** a market queen modelled on the Iyaloja role, a fixer who arbitrates, lends money and has political pull.

#### 17. Things to avoid
- **Real people:** Oba Akiolu, Kunle Poly, Tafa Sego, Madam Tinubu.
- **Real brands and institutions:** First Bank, UBA, Shell, NURTW, LASTMA.
- **Real names:** "Eyo", "Adimu" and "Iga Idunganran" (invent replacements).
- **Sacred items:** real shrine iconography (Ogun, Esu).
- Do not rebuild the Central Mosque or the cathedral as places where crimes happen.

## Eti-Osa LGA research brief

I ran out of web searches before finishing. The shared limit for this turn ran out partway through, so a few areas had no page I could open. Those are flooding beyond Ikoyi, the Kuramo Beach demolition, the Oniru chieftaincy, Ajah's markets, and music. Lines tagged **[unsourced]** come from general knowledge, not from a page I opened. Check them before relying on them. Send a follow-up to continue the searching.

#### 1. In one line
A former marshland and colonial suburb on the coast that became Lagos's richest area: banks, embassies and mansions sitting next to fishing villages that were cleared out.

#### 2. Areas inside it
- **Ikoyi**: built under colonial rule as housing for British expatriates. "Ikoyi I" is the old core of large colonial houses built 1900–1950. "Ikoyi II" is newer luxury high-rises. Dolphin Estate floods constantly.
- **Banana Island**: an artificial island on about 1.63 km² of filled sand. It has 536 plots on cul-de-sacs, houses capped at 3 storeys, underground power and water, and billionaire residents.
- **Victoria Island (VI)**: the main business and banking district, with most of the consulates.
- **Oniru**: land reclaimed from the VI Annex out to Lekki, on Oniru family land.
- **Obalende**: a rough transport hub on the edge of Ikoyi.
- **Lekki Peninsula**: Lekki Phase 1 has some of the priciest property in the state. Also Ikate-Elegushi, Jakande, Chevron, Osapa, Agungi, Ikota, Victoria Garden City (VGC) and Ajah.
- **Ajah**: an old fishing town about 22 km south-east of central Lagos. It still has a Baale (traditional head) and 42 chiefs.
- **Eko Atlantic**: a new city of about 10 km² built on land reclaimed from the sea off Bar Beach.

Lagos State splits the LGA into Ikoyi-Obalende, Eti-Osa East and Iru-Victoria Island LCDAs (smaller council areas).

#### 3. Landmarks and monuments
- **Lekki-Ikoyi Link Bridge**: a 1.36 km cable-stayed bridge, the first of its kind in Nigeria, opened in 2013. It is tolled, joggers use it at dawn, and it is called the most photographed place in Lagos.
- **Lekki toll gate** (Admiralty Circle Plaza).
- Civic Towers on Ozumba Mbadiwe, Zenith Bank headquarters, Silverbird Galleria.
- EKO, Reddington and Euracare hospitals.
- Ikoyi Club and its golf club, Dodan Barracks (once a home of military rulers), Falomo.
- Lekki Arts and Crafts Market, called "the largest art market in Nigeria".
- Nike Art Gallery (about 8,000 works) and the Lekki Conservation Centre (78 ha, opened 1990).
- Elegushi Beach, Eko Pearl Towers, the Abraham Adesanya roundabout in Ajah.

#### 4. Buildings and streets
- **Old Ikoyi**: colonial bungalows on big plots and wide leafy roads such as Bourdillon and Awolowo Road (a high street of upscale shops).
- **Newer areas**: glass towers on VI and in Ikoyi II.
- **Lekki**: gated estates behind walls with gatemen. On Banana Island, Ocean Parade's 14 towers face the lagoon.
- **Construction risk**: a 21-storey tower on Gerrard Road collapsed during construction on 2 Nov 2021, killing workers and the developer.
- **Informal settlements**: on the waterfront edges, wooden fishing settlements on stilts and canoes (Otodo-Gbame). In Obalende, illegal shacks and kiosks, and cars dumped under the bridge.
- **The contrast to capture**: the gap between gated wealth and informal shacks is the area's defining visual.

#### 5. Roads, junctions, bridges, traffic
- **Main corridor**: the Lekki-Epe Expressway runs from VI to Epe and fed the growth of the whole peninsula.
- **Key roads**: Ahmadu Bello Way along the old beachfront often closed when the sea surged. Adeola Odeku and Akin Adesola are nightlife streets.
- **Bridges**: Falomo Bridge and the Obalende bridge, with the Obalende Bus Terminal opposite it.
- **Link Bridge rules**: no vehicles with more than 26 seats. No tolls collected since October 2020.
- **Traffic**: more commerce has meant heavier traffic and more street hawkers. The Lekki–Ajah go-slow is notorious **[unsourced]**.

#### 6. Economy and markets
- **Formal**: banking (GTBank and Access Bank headquarters, Zenith), IBM, Halliburton, oil companies (expatriate staff moved to Ikoyi after Niger Delta unrest), embassies, hotels, private schools.
- **Industry in the east**: the Lekki deep-sea port (first ship 22 Jan 2023) and the Dangote refinery (running since Dec 2023, 650,000 barrels a day at full capacity) **[these sit just east, in Ibeju-Lekki, outside Eti-Osa: verify]**.
- **Informal**: fishing (the original economy), trading, street hawking, gatemen and drivers for the rich, and agbero fees at the parks.
- **Odd detail**: a 2009 study counted 75 commercial gardens, mostly in Ikoyi and VI.

#### 7. Transport
- **Okada ban**: Eti-Osa is one of six LGAs where Governor Sanwo-Olu banned okada from 1 June 2022. The trigger was okada riders lynching a passenger, David Sunday, in Lekki.
- **Hubs**: Obalende is the danfo and bus hub. There is a BRT shed on CMS Outer Marina.
- **Keke**: tricycles (keke NAPEP) run in Ajah.
- **Rail**: lines are proposed but not built.

#### 8. Sound, smell, look
These are reasoned from the sources, not quoted.
- Dawn joggers on the Link Bridge.
- Midday: honking traffic on the expressway and hawkers working stuck cars.
- Sundays: crowds at the beaches.
- Night: clubs on VI streets.
- Contrast: generator hum in the estates against the sea wind and smoked-fish air of the fishing settlements **[unsourced]**.

#### 9. People, languages, religion
- **People**: mainly Awori and Ijebu Yoruba, with Hausa, Igbo and many others moving in. Ijaw and Ilaje lived in Maroko.
- **Population**: about 287,785 in the 2006 census.
- **Languages**: Yoruba, Pidgin and English. The rich speak English.
- **Religion**: no specific source opened. Expect both mosques and churches **[unsourced]**.

#### 10. Food and nightlife
- **Clubs**: Akin Adesola and Adeola Odeku are VI's club streets.
- **Beaches**: Elegushi Beach gets about 40,000 visitors a week, more than half of them on Sundays. The gate fee is ₦2,000.
- **Food**: suya spots, buka canteens for drivers and gatemen, and beach bars **[unsourced]**.

#### 11. History
- **15th–16th century**: fishing settlements, including Ajah.
- **Colonial era**: Ikoyi built as housing for British expatriates. The MacGregor canal was dug, and swamps filled to fight mosquitoes.
- **1948**: Oniru landholders were paid off and moved to Maroko.
- **1971–80s**: public firing-squad executions on Bar Beach, including the 1976 coup plotters.
- **1980**: an attempt to evict Maroko led to riots.
- **1990**: Maroko demolished under military administrator Raji Rasaki. Up to about 300,000 people lost their homes. Activists mark it every 14 July. Lekki and Oniru estates later grew on the cleared and filled land.
- **2008 onward**: Eko Atlantic reclaimed from the sea.
- **2013**: Link Bridge opened.
- **Nov 2016 – Apr 2017**: more than 30,000 people evicted from Otodo-Gbame, with fires, gunfire and drownings.
- **20 Oct 2020**: soldiers fired on EndSARS protesters at the Lekki toll gate.

#### 12. Danger and law
- **Lekki toll gate shooting**: the Lagos panel of inquiry found 9 dead and Amnesty said at least 12. The government disputes both, and the federal government rejected the panel's report. The lights and billboards were cut before the shooting. In 2024 the ECOWAS Court ordered ₦10m compensation per named victim.
- **Otodo-Gbame**: Amnesty says 11 people were killed, including drownings while fleeing gunfire. One killing was blamed on armed men from neighbouring Ikate-Elegushi.
- **Obalende**: before the 2010 clearance, residents described hoodlums who "maimed, harassed and robbed."

## Ibeju-Lekki LGA: research brief for Gidi

**Coverage note:** Web searches ran out partway through, because the shared budget was used up. Sections 7, 8, 10, 12 (cult activity) and 15 are therefore thin. Anything marked **[unverified]** comes from general knowledge, not a page I opened. Send a follow-up if you want those gaps filled.

#### 1. In one line
A coastal strip of farms and fishing villages being turned into "the new Lagos". A free trade zone, a mega-refinery, a deep sea port and endless estates are landing on ancestral land, and everyone is fighting over who owns it.

#### 2. Areas inside it
- **Igando Oloja** is the LGA headquarters. Akodo was the HQ earlier.
- **Ibeju** and **Lekki** are the two old communities the LGA is named after.
- Other villages: **Debojo, Orimedu, Lakowe, Elemoro, Okun Solu, Magbon-Alade**, plus **Ojuoto**, **Ilameja**, **Okun-Obadore** and **Abijo**, which all appear in land-dispute reporting.
- The western edge (Awoyaya, Lakowe, Abijo) is estate sprawl that bleeds into Ajah and Sangotedo, which are mostly Eti-Osa **[unverified boundary]**.
- The far east is the industrial zone around the Free Zone, the refinery and the port.

#### 3. Landmarks
- **Lekki Free Zone**, near the Lekki Lagoon. It is master-planned with three districts: residential (north), industrial (middle) and logistics/warehousing (southeast). Its roads are named E2, E4 and E9.
- **Dangote Refinery**: 2,635 ha, about $9bn, running since December 2023 and aimed at 650,000 barrels a day. It includes a fertiliser plant.
- **Lekki Deep Sea Port**: a 1,200m quay, three post-Panamax gantry cranes and storage for over 15,000 containers. The first ship, the *CMA CGM Mozart*, docked in January 2023.
- **LUFASI Nature Park**, a forest reserve.
- **Pan-Atlantic University / Lagos Business School** library.
- **Abraham Adesanya roundabout**, the gateway junction.
- A planned **Lekki-Epe International Airport**.
- Beaches such as Eleko and Akodo **[unverified]**.

#### 4. Buildings and streets
Four layers sit side by side **[general knowledge, unverified]**:
- old village compounds and farmland
- gated estates with security gates and estate signage
- half-built or abandoned buildings on speculated plots
- huge fenced industrial sites

Disputed plots carry "This land is not for sale" style warnings, fences, and bulldozed fence lines. In the Ojuoto case, hoodlums used a tractor to pull down fences. Inner roads are poor and the main expressway is the only real artery.

#### 5. Roads and traffic
- The **Lekki-Epe Expressway** is the spine. Commuters call the stretch from Abraham Adesanya to Ajah "hellish and terrible". One commuter reported Epe to Oshodi taking six hours, worse in rain, and peak fares rose more than 200%.
- **Jubilee Bridge** flyover in Ajah: pedestrians cross at both ends, NEPA Road was taken over by traders and keke, and sand-dredging lorries block a whole lane at Ilaje. A 25–40 minute trip took 3–4 hours.
- Reconstruction from January 2026 uses tidal lane-sharing: Lagos-bound gets two lanes 4am–noon, Epe-bound gets two lanes 1pm–3am. The **Coastal Road** is the detour.
- Expect sand tippers, port container trucks and refinery tankers **[the last two unverified]**.

#### 6. Economy and markets
- Old economy: farming and fishing (the Okun-Obadore protesters were "farmers and fishermen").
- New economy: refinery jobs (about 57,000 hires claimed), port logistics, warehousing, estate construction (bricklayers, site guards), land brokering and dredging.
- The real hustle is **land**: selling, re-selling and "guarding" plots.

#### 7. Transport
Danfo buses and roadside boarding dominate. BRT stopped on the Ajah axis for a time for lack of terminals. Keke and okada run on side roads. A Lagos Rail Green Line to Lekki is planned. Exact okada ban zones are **[unverified]**.

#### 8. Sound, smell, look
- Commuters leave "at first cockcrow", around 5am, to beat the go-slow, and road works run through the night.
- Sea wind and ocean surge sit beside a darkened village: over 40 communities reportedly went years without grid power while hosting billion-naira projects, so generators everywhere.
- The far east has the refinery and port glow at night **[unverified]**.

#### 9. People, language, religion
Indigenous Yoruba communities (Ijebu sub-group **[unverified]**) plus migrants from across Nigeria drawn by work. Yoruba and Pidgin dominate. Religious life mixes Islam, Christianity and traditional practice (see section 14). The 2006 population was 117,481, and is far higher now **[unverified]**.

#### 10. Food and nightlife
**[All unverified]** Beach-resort weekends, suya and fish at beach bars, mama-put food for construction crews, and estate lounges near Awoyaya and Sangotedo.

#### 11. History
- Old villages were settled for centuries.
- 2006: the Free Zone was launched under Governor Tinubu.
- Land acquisitions and speculation took off from there.
- Refinery and port came online in 2023.
- Coastal erosion is ongoing.
- Repeated expressway rebuilds, most recently 2026.

#### 12. Danger and law
These are reported allegations, not court findings:
- **Abijo, 2022**: five "Omo-Onile" (women among them) were arrested after firing shots at a task force. Guns and **charms** were recovered.
- **Ilameja**: land grabbers allegedly shot dead bricklayer Sodiq Kolapo on a building site and removed his body.
- **Ojuoto**: an 80-year-old's family alleges that police handed court-protected land to grabbers and their thugs.

Common terms include *omo onile* and *ajagungbale*, and there is a state **Anti-Land Grabbing** agency. Police are often accused of taking sides. Cult activity was not researched.

#### 13. Environment
- Ocean surge and erosion along the coast: coconut trees lost, and an estate's back fence hit by the sea.
- In nearby Okun Alfa (Eti-Osa), surges flooded homes and left the Baale's palace half-submerged.
- Rainy-season gridlock, chronic power cuts, and sand dredging.

#### 14. Festivals and sacred things
**Oro**: a Yoruba male-only rite observed in parts of Ibeju-Lekki, Epe and Ikorodu. During Oro, women and non-natives stay indoors. Communities also have Baales and Obas. **FLAG:** Oro, its shrines, chiefs and masquerades are living religion. Never make them gang fronts or villains. If used at all, a curfew night the player must respect is the most you should do.

#### 15. In pop culture
Not researched.

#### 16. What Gidi can borrow
1. **The land war district.** A fictional coastal zone where Tunde runs "site security" jobs: escort bricklayers, defend or knock down a fence, deliver survey papers before rivals do. Every plot has two "owners".
2. **The tidal expressway.** One lane each way that flips direction by time of day, with sand tippers, tankers and pedestrians dashing across the flyover. Okada lane-splitting becomes the core advantage.
3. **The refinery and port set piece.** A floodlit fenced mega-site on the horizon, with container trucks and tanker convoys. A heist or smuggling run uses the fictional port's gantry cranes.
4. **The ocean surge mission.** A night storm floods a beach village, and Tunde ferries people and goods while the sea eats the road.
5. **Character: the village land broker.** He sells the same plot twice, keeps charms and a pump-action, and has a police friend.
6. **Blackout village beside a glowing billion-dollar plant**, as a visual contrast for the world.

#### 17. Things to avoid
- Real names: Dangote, the Lekki Free Zone, the Lekki Deep Sea Port, the *CMA CGM Mozart*, LUFASI, Pan-Atlantic University, and real estates such as Amen Estate and Redbrick.

## Epe LGA: research brief for Gidi

**Search limits:** the shared web-search budget ran out partway through. I never got to search for crime in Epe itself, Noforija, Itoikin, the Epe–Ijebu road, nightlife or pop culture. Anything marked **[unverified]** comes from general knowledge, not a page I opened. Send a follow-up if you want those gaps filled.

#### 1. In one line
An old Ijebu lagoon port on the north shore of Lekki Lagoon. It is mostly Muslim and lives on fish. It sits where the Lekki–Epe toll road ends and the Lagos city sprawl gives out.

#### 2. Areas inside it
- **Two old halves of one town.** **Ijebu-Epe** is the older Ijebu settlement under the **Oloja of Epeland**. **Eko-Epe** was founded in 1851 by the exiled Lagos king Kosoko, and its ruler is the **Olu of Epe**. The two sides went to war in 1912.
- Named quarters and villages: Areke/Aleke, Apakeji, Etita, Poka, Odo-Ragunsen, Ibonwon, Igbodu, Araga. Ejirin and Ikosi have markets.
- The LGA has 294 rural and 24 semi-urban communities.
- **[unverified]** Noforija is a new land-banking and estate zone on the expressway side. Itoikin and Agbowa are farm and road towns toward Ikorodu and Ijebu.

#### 3. Landmarks and monuments
- **Sculpture of two giant fish** at the Lekki–Epe Expressway entrance.
- **Oluwo Fish Market:** the biggest fish market in Lagos State, called "Chief" locally. It started at the Marina front and was officially commissioned in 1989. It was remodelled with 339 shops, a jetty and a fish-processing area, opened in June 2022.
- **Epe Marina** and Recreation Centre.
- **Kosoko's disembarkation point**, including the boat he arrived in.
- The former **colonial Divisional Officers' house**.
- **First Epe Central Mosque** and the **Olu-Epe's palace**.
- A 960-seat mini-stadium.
- Murtala Mohammed Botanical Garden, CERUD Botanical Garden and Epe mangrove.
- Campuses: LASU Epe, YABATECH Epe, LASUED (formerly MOCPED).
- Older schools: St Michael's CMS School (1894), Government Muslim School (1898), Ansar-ud-deen School.

#### 4. Buildings and streets
I found no sources on this. **[unverified]** Expect a low-rise old town of bungalows and storey buildings, a few older colonial-era houses and mosques, and dense lanes running down to the Marina. On the expressway side, expect gated estates, fenced land plots, "This land is not for sale" signs and uncompleted buildings.

#### 5. Roads, junctions, bridges, traffic
- **Lekki–Epe Expressway**: 49.5 km, first built in the 1980s, run as a toll road by Lekki Concession Company.
- Named stops: **Epe Roundabout**, Epe Express Road, Sandfill bus stop, Epe Market.
- A bridge behind the fish market where traders hang baskets on the rails.
- **[unverified]** The Epe–Ijebu-Ode road and the Ikorodu–Itoikin road are bad, potholed rural roads.

#### 6. Economy and markets
- Fishing is the main job. Epe is called the "fish basket of the state." Men catch the fish, women buy and sell it, and big Lagos restaurants and cold-room owners get served first.
- **Bushmeat** is sold at the fish market, live or dead.
- Epe also supplies Lagos with cassava, maize, coconuts, palm produce, rubber and firewood. It trucks kola-preserving leaves to Ijebu-Ode and Shagamu.
- It builds **motorised shallow-draft barges**.
- Catches are falling. Fishermen go further out, and catfish prices tripled in three months, according to one report. The dry season means scarce fish and poor sales.

#### 7. Transport
- **Lagoon:** canoes, small boats and barges. The lagoon is too shallow for large ships.
- **Okada:** Epe is not in the 2022 Lagos okada bans, which covered Eti-Osa, Ikeja, Surulere, Lagos Island, Lagos Mainland, Apapa, Kosofe, Oshodi-Isolo, Somolu and Mushin. **[unverified]** So okada and keke probably rule here.

#### 8. Sound, smell, look
- Wet-market smell of fresh fish and bushmeat.
- Baskets of live fish half-sunk in the water.
- Bamboo stakes and water lilies marking private fishing patches near the shore.
- Hand-thrown dragnets with floats.
- Kids swimming off the shore.
- **[unverified]** Fish-smoking smoke and the call to prayer set the rhythm of the day.

#### 9. People, languages, religion
- Mostly Ijebu Yoruba, and predominantly Muslim. Islam came with Kosoko's followers in 1852.
- CMS Christianity arrived in 1892, the Catholic mission in 1903, and Cherubim and Seraphim in 1930.
- Population was 181,409 in the 2006 census.

#### 10. Food and nightlife
- Festival food: seafood, iyan, efo riro, **ikokore** (an Ijebu dish), amala.
- I found nothing on nightlife.

#### 11. History
- Founded by Ijebu hunters; an early name was "Igbo Obo." The Oloja line dates to about 1780–90.
- **1851:** Kosoko arrives with about 1,500 followers after the British bombard Lagos.
- **1863:** Governor Freeman attacks Epe and it is ceded to Britain.
- **1892:** Base for the British expedition against the Awujale at Ijebu-Ode.
- **1912:** War between Ijebu-Epe and Eko-Epe.

#### 12. Danger and law
**Not verified.** The cult-clash articles I opened were about Alakuko and Ajegunle, not Epe, so I won't attribute them here. Treat any Epe crime detail as needing more research.

#### 13. Environment
- Lagos State has warned Epe and Badagry coastal areas about **backflow flooding** when high tide in the lagoon stops storm water draining.
- Mangrove and swamp forest line the shore.
- Fish stocks are falling and fishermen blame the city's growth. One article title also points to "the oil refinery"; I only saw an excerpt, so this is not confirmed.

#### 14. Festivals and sacred things
- **Kayo-Kayo** ("eating to satisfaction"): a week-long Islamic New Year festival around 10 Muharram, about a month after Ileya. It re-enacts Kosoko's landing by boat to the Marina, includes a boat ride symbolising Noah's ark, a Tahajjud night at the Central Mosque and the Kayo-Kayo prayer at the palace. The Olu-Epe lights the **Etufu** torch, which burns for seven days. It also has a maidens' fashion parade, a youth parade and shared feasting.
- Other festivals: Ojude-Oba, Ebi Day, Epe Day.
- **FLAG: never portray as criminal or as a crime backdrop:** the Etufu torch, the mosques and prayers, the palaces and both obas, Kosoko's landing site, and the Kayo-Kayo procession.

#### 15. In pop culture
I found nothing. Famous Epe natives include Femi Otedola, DJ Cuppy and Akinwunmi Ambode. They are real people and must not be copied.

#### 16. What Gidi can borrow
1. **The end-of-the-toll-road district.** A big fish statue at the gate, then the city falls away into fenced land and estates on one side and an old lagoon town on the other.
2. **Fish-run missions.** Collect oja from barges at dawn at a jetty market and race it to "big restaurants" before it spoils, with a freshness timer. Dry season means scarce stock and higher stakes.
3. **Barge-yard set piece.** A boat chase or fight among half-built motorised shallow-draft barges.
4. **A town split in two.** An invented old feud between two quarters with rival rulers, used as gang-turf flavour. Keep the real rulers and history out of it.
5. **Okada heartland.** Outside the city ban zone, Tunde can ride freely. Make it a safe district for okada work in contrast with the banned core.
6. **Flood-tide event.** High tide blocks the drains and roads go under. This is dynamic weather that reroutes deliveries.

- Real names: Oloja, Olu-Epe, Kosoko, Oba Shefiu Adewale, the Oluwo family.
- The Etufu, Kayo-Kayo and mosques as crime settings.
- The Lekki Concession Company name.
- Real people: Otedola, Cuppy, Ambode.

## Lagos Mainland LGA: research brief for Gidi


---

#### 1. In one line
This is the old railway-and-market mainland of Lagos. It has colonial rail houses, big markets, a tech district around the colleges, a sawmill on the water and a fishing village on stilts, all within sight of Third Mainland Bridge. It is one of the most densely populated parts of Lagos (2006 census: over 500,000 people).

#### 2. Areas inside it
- **Ebute-Metta (East and West):** The LGA headquarters and the old railway town. The name means "three harbours" (Iddo, Otto and Oyingbo). It is now being gentrified, and one article calls it the "Brooklyn of Lagos".
- **Otto and Iddo:** Otto is the seat of the Oloto (the traditional ruler). Iddo is the peninsula that leads to Lagos Island.
- **Oyingbo:** A market and transport hub.
- **Yaba:** Colleges, the tech scene, and the cheap-clothes markets at Tejuosho and Yaba. It is now its own council area (Yaba LCDA).
- **Makoko:** A lagoon fishing community made of six villages. Four are on the water (Adogbo, Migbewhe, Oko Agbon, Yanshiwhe) and two are on land (Apollo, Sogunro).
- **Oko-Baba:** The sawmill district on the lagoon. Kano, Jebba and Ondo Streets run through it.
- **Others:** Alagomeji, Abule-Ijesha, Abule-Oja, Iwaya, Igbobi, and the Akoka/UNILAG edge.

#### 3. Landmarks and monuments
- **Jaekel House:** 17 Federal Road, Ebute-Metta. A two-storey colonial house built in 1898 for the railway's general manager. It is now a railway museum and a film location.
- **Railway buildings:** Nigerian Railway Corporation headquarters, and the Mobolaji Johnson Central Station (opened 2021, links Lagos to Ibadan, has a tent-shaped roof).
- **Oyingbo Market:** Rebuilt as a four-floor complex in 2015 with 724 shops and a car park underneath.
- **Other markets:** Tejuosho Market and Yaba Market.
- **Hospitals:** The Federal Neuro-Psychiatric Hospital (opened 1907), popularly called **"Yaba Left"**, and the 68 Nigerian Army Reference Hospital.
- **Schools and colleges:** Yaba College of Technology, Queen's College, Igbobi College and UNILAG.
- **Churches and halls:** St Jude's Church (founded by Egba settlers), St Paul's Catholic Church, Lisabi Hall (1938).
- **Third Mainland Bridge:** 11.8 km long, finished in 1990. From it you look down on Makoko.
- **Other:** Sabo Roundabout, the E-Centre (Ozone Cinemas), the National Stadium and Lagos City Mall.

#### 4. Buildings and streets
- **Old colonial houses:** Brazilian-style houses from the colonial era and colonial bungalows, now being turned into studio flats.
- **New builds:** Gated townhouses replacing small tenements.
- **Street grid:** The Egba settlers laid out streets named after missionaries and chiefs: Clifford Street (now Murtala Muhammad Way), Griffith Street and Osholake Street.
- **Tenements [GK]:** Face-me-I-face-you rooming houses.
- **Makoko:** Wooden houses on stilts over the lagoon, and wooden shacks on land.
- **Oko-Baba:** Timber workshops and offices.

#### 5. Roads, junctions, bridges, traffic
- **Third Mainland Bridge:** Very heavy weekday traffic. It has had repeated repair closures (2020–21 and 2024).
- **Flyovers:** Ebute-Metta is a main route to Lagos Island, Ikoyi and Victoria Island via flyovers across the Iddo peninsula.
- **Herbert Macaulay Way:** Links Yaba to the bridge.
- **[GK]:** Carter Bridge and Eko Bridge leave from Iddo. The Iddo motor park and Yaba bus stop are major hubs. Ojuelegba is just over the border in Surulere.

#### 6. Economy and markets
- **Markets:**
  - Oyingbo started in the 1920s as a farm-produce depot and added meat and livestock in the 1930s. Its Iyaloja (head of the market women) says it now trades in over 70 kinds of goods.
  - Yaba has cheap goods and evening discount sales.
  - Tejuosho sells cheap and second-hand imported clothes.
- **Tech:** CcHUB (2011), Andela and Konga.
- **Trades:** Sawmilling and wood-turning at Oko-Baba, fishing and small trading at Makoko, and students.

#### 7. Transport
- **Okada:** Banned in Lagos Mainland and five other LGAs from 1 June 2022, after riders killed a sound engineer in Lekki. Police and the Taskforce enforce the ban. **This matters for the game:** Tunde's okada job is illegal on these roads.
- **Rail:** The Red Line runs Agbado to Oyingbo (regular service since October 2024). There is also the Lagos–Ibadan standard-gauge line.
- **Water [GK]:** Canoes in Makoko. Danfo minibuses and keke are everywhere on the roads.

#### 8. Sound, smell, look
Nothing in my sources covers this directly, so it is all **[GK]**: sawdust and woodsmoke at Oko-Baba, smoked fish and lagoon water at Makoko, conductors shouting at bus stops, generators at night, and the Yaba evening sales crowd.

#### 9. People, languages, religion
- **Makoko:** Mostly Egun people, originally from Badagry and Benin. **[GK]** They speak Egun (Gun).
- **Ebute-Metta:** Founded by Egba Christians in 1867. Railway families followed.
- **Oko-Baba:** The wood-turners' leader is Igbo (Chukwuka Ogbonna).
- **[GK]:** Yoruba and Pidgin are the shared languages. There are many churches and mosques.

#### 10. Food and nightlife
**[GK]:** Bukas around the markets, suya, and student food spots in Yaba. Cinema-going at the E-Centre is sourced.

#### 11. History
- **1867:** Egba Christians fleeing Abeokuta are given land by the Oloto and found "Ago Egba".
- **1898:** Jaekel House is built.

- **After 1914:** The railway brings migrants who cannot get housing on the Island.
- **1977:** Lagos Mainland LGA is created. Surulere is split off in 1991.

#### 12. Danger and law
- **Makoko clearances:**
  - July 2012: About 3,000 people made homeless within 72 hours of notice.

- **Oko-Baba:** Demolitions were carried out on behalf of the Oloto family, the landowners. 17 people were reportedly arrested for rebuilding.
- **Not sourced:** Gangs and cult activity specific to this LGA. Yaba's Wikipedia page calls Makoko a "no-go area" to some.

#### 13. Environment

## Surulere LGA: research notes for Gidi

**Before you read:** the shared web-search limit ran out partway through. Facts with a [source] tag come from pages I opened. Facts marked **[unverified]** come from general knowledge and should be checked before you lean on them.

#### 1. In one line
Surulere is the crowded, middle-class part of the Lagos mainland. It holds the city's football, stage and music history, and Ojuelegba, its main crossroads, never stops moving.

#### 2. Areas inside it
The LGA is 23 km². The 2022 estimate is about 744,000 people, roughly 32,000 per km² [Wikipedia: Surulere]. Its neighbours are Yaba, Mushin and Ebute-Metta.
- **Ojuelegba:** the transport hub between the mainland and the Island, and between Yaba, Mushin and Surulere. It's known for traffic and nightlife [Wikipedia: Ojuelegba].
- **Aguda, Obele, Ikate:** older quarters. Aguda is named after the freed African-Brazilian and Cuban returnees who settled there in the 19th century. Many easterners settled in these three areas [Wikipedia: Surulere].
- **Idi-Araba:** where northern Nigerians first settled [Wikipedia: Surulere].
- **New Lagos:** also called the Surulere Re-Housing Estate, one of Nigeria's first public housing projects. People moved from Lagos Island to settle it [Wikipedia: Surulere].
- **Itire:** has its own traditional ruler, the Onitire of Itire [Wikipedia: Surulere].
- **Lawanson, Ijesha, Shitta, Animashaun:** dense residential and market streets.
- **Iganmu, Iponri, Coker:** the industrial edge [Wikipedia: Surulere].
- **Bode Thomas and Adeniran Ogunsanya:** the more upmarket commercial strips (see section 4).

#### 3. Landmarks and monuments
- **National Stadium:** designed by Fola Alade Associates and opened on 4 December 1972. It held 85,000 people at the 1980 Nations Cup final, where Nigeria beat Algeria 3–0. It also hosted the 2000 AFCON final. The last national-team match there was in 2004. After that it decayed and was "taken over by area boys and squatters", and renovation started in 2022 [Wikipedia: National Stadium].
- **Teslim Balogun Stadium:** right next to the National Stadium, with 24,325 seats. Work started in 1984, stalled for 23 years as a "white elephant", and finished in 2007. The facade was redone in 2023–24 [Wikipedia: Teslim Balogun].
- **National Theatre, Iganmu:** built to look like a military cap, and finished in 1976 for FESTAC '77. Its main hall seats 5,000. There were privatisation fights over it, and a 2014 report said it had been sold to a Dubai firm. It was renamed the Wole Soyinka Centre in 2024 and reopened on 1 October 2025 [Wikipedia: National Theatre].
- **Others:** Eko Bridge, the NYSC statue and Randle General Hospital [Wikipedia: Surulere]. Victor Olaiya's **Stadium Hotel** [Wikipedia: Victor Olaiya]. The Ojuelegba under-bridge [Wikipedia: Ojuelegba].

#### 4. Buildings and streets
The main commercial streets are Western Avenue, Adeniran Ogunsanya, Adelabu, Ogunlana Drive, Aguda and Bode Thomas [Wikipedia: Surulere].
- **[unverified]** The housing is mostly 1950s–70s storey buildings and bungalows, with "face-me-I-face-you" tenement compounds packed into the side streets.
- **[unverified]** Bode Thomas and Adeniran Ogunsanya have newer plazas, banks and a shopping mall.
- **[unverified]** The inner streets are narrow, with open drains along the edges and generator boxes and painted shop signs everywhere.
- New Lagos is an example of planned colonial-era public housing, which looks different from the unplanned streets around it [Wikipedia: Surulere].

#### 5. Roads, junctions, bridges, traffic
- Ojuelegba is a famous go-slow spot and a crossing point between the mainland and the Island [Wikipedia: Ojuelegba].
- On 29 January 2023, a 20-foot container fell off a truck on the Ojuelegba bridge onto a korope minibus that was picking up passengers. Eight people died. Rescuers used a forklift and cut open the bus roof to reach victims [ThisDay]. A trailer also crashed at Ojuelegba in 2015 [Wikipedia: Ojuelegba].
- Eko Bridge links the area to the Island [Wikipedia: Surulere].
- Bus stops named in reporting: Pako, Yeside and Cassette, all in Aguda [Guardian 2019].

#### 6. Economy and markets
- **Formal:** factories in Iganmu, Iponri and Coker. Nigerian Breweries has a presence [Wikipedia: Surulere].
- **Informal:** trading and transport-union work. Wikipedia says there are open markets in many neighbourhoods but doesn't name them [Wikipedia: Surulere].
- **[unverified]** The area also has a lot of entertainment and film-production work.

#### 7. Transport
- The dominant vehicles are danfo and korope minibuses with conductors shouting the stops. Wizkid's "Ojuelegba" video opens on a conductor calling for Ojuelegba passengers [Wikipedia: Ojuelegba song; ThisDay].
- **[unverified]** Okada are restricted: the 2022 Lagos ban covered Surulere LGA, but enforcement is patchy and keke are common. This matters directly for Tunde as an okada rider.
- **[unverified]** BRT routes run along the corridors near Ojuelegba.
- No rail or ferry is central to the area.

#### 8. Sound, smell, look
These are reasonable inferences, not reported fact:
- **Sound:** conductors chanting "Ojuelegba! Ojuelegba!", horns and generator hum.
- **Look and smell:** dust and exhaust under the bridge.
- **Match days:** crowds and noise around the stadiums.
- **Night:** the bars and hotels around Ojuelegba come alive, in keeping with its nightlife reputation [Wikipedia: Ojuelegba].

#### 9. People, languages, religion
- The area has long been mixed: Yoruba (Awori and Island families), northerners at Idi-Araba, easterners in Obele, Ikate and Aguda, plus Afro-Brazilian descendants [Wikipedia: Surulere].
- **[unverified]** Yoruba and Pidgin are the street languages.
- Islamic and Christian life sit side by side. The Muslim organisation Ansar-Ud-Deen is based here [Wikipedia: Surulere]. In the 1990s, some brothels and nightclubs in Ojuelegba were turned into mosques and churches [Wikipedia: Ojuelegba].

#### 10. Food and nightlife
- **Food:** amala and Tastee Fried Chicken are listed as local staples [Wikipedia: Surulere].
- **1970s–90s:** Fela's music drew revellers through Ojuelegba, and there was a red-light strip on Ayilara Street and parts of Clegg Street [Wikipedia: Ojuelegba]. The Wikipedia page puts Fela's Moshalashi Shrine on Agege Motor Road. His Kalakuta compound, also on Agege Motor Road, was at Idi-Oro, Mushin, which is next door rather than inside Surulere [Wikipedia: Kalakuta].
- **Highlife:** Victor Olaiya, the highlife bandleader who gave Fela an early start, ran the Stadium Hotel in Surulere [Wikipedia: Victor Olaiya].
- **Musicians from Surulere:** Wizkid, Simi and Seyi Sodimu [Wikipedia: Surulere].

#### 11. History
- **19th century:** freed African-Brazilians and Cubans settle at Aguda.
- **Colonial era:** the New Lagos re-housing scheme moves Island families here.
- **1972:** the National Stadium opens.
- **1973:** the All-Africa Games.
- **1976–77:** the National Theatre is finished and FESTAC '77 runs here (15 January to 12 February).
- **1980:** the Nations Cup final.
- **2000s:** the stadium declines.
- **2020s:** the stadium and theatre are renovated.

[Sources: Wikipedia pages for Surulere, National Stadium and National Theatre]

#### 12. Danger and law
- **1970s to early 1990s:** Ojuelegba had high crime. Armed robbery is now rare, but prostitution has come back [Wikipedia: Ojuelegba].
- **15–16 December 2019, Aguda and Ijesha:**
  - Cult turf fighting between "Mushin boys" and "Aguda boys".
  - At the same time, rival factions of the transport union (NURTW) fought over the Ijesha branch chair. One unit chairman was killed.
  - Revenge raids followed, and about 22 shops were destroyed.
  - A truck carrying beer was looted.
  - Police arrested 75 people. Local police couldn't contain it at first, and the special units (SARS, RRS) had to step in [Guardian; TheCable].
- **September 2019, Itire:** a fight between the Aiye and Eiye cults started at a drinking joint on Oseni Street and spread to a hotel on Babashola Street. A 29-year-old was stabbed to death, and the hotel and three cars were burned [Guardian].

#### 13. Environment
- **[unverified]** Flooding in the low parts of Aguda and Ijesha, plus the Aguda canal (named in reporting [TheCable]).
- **[unverified]** Blocked drains, generators running because of power cuts, and heavy-truck pollution near Iganmu and the bridges.

#### 14. Festivals and sacred things
- The **Onitire of Itire** is a recognised traditional ruler [Wikipedia: Surulere]. Never portray him as a criminal.
- **[unverified, FLAG]** The name Ojuelegba is popularly linked to a shrine of the Yoruba deity Èṣù/Elegba. Treat any shrine, Èṣù imagery, or masquerade or Oro rite as sacred, never as gang symbols.
- Mosques and churches likewise should never be used as crime fronts.

#### 15. In pop culture
- **Fela:** the *Confusion* album (1975).
- **Wizkid, "Ojuelegba" (2014):** a song about hustling as an unknown artist. The Drake and Skepta remix came out in 2015, and Clarence Peters directed the video.
- **Oritse Femi, "Double Wahala"**, which depicts Ojuelegba life.

[Sources: Wikipedia pages for Ojuelegba and the song]

Surulere-linked Nollywood figures include Ramsey Nouah, Desmond Elliot and the director Tade Ogidan. Writers include A. Igoni Barrett [Wikipedia: Surulere].

#### 16. What Gidi can borrow
1. **A crossroads district under a flyover:** a junction where touts, conductors and okada riders meet, with a "container falls off the bridge" disaster you can set off or survive as a set piece.
2. **A dead stadium as a hideout:** a decaying 1970s national stadium held by squatters and area boys, which gets cleared out mid-story as it's renovated. It can be both a gang base and a gentrification storyline.
3. **Union succession war missions:** Tunde's motor-park boss has to defend his branch chair against a rival faction. Missions include escorting voters, delivering oja during a riot, and getting goods out of a looted truck.
4. **Turf-raid events:** "boys" from a neighbouring district raid this one, shops get burned, a police strike force sweeps in, and you have to get out before mass arrests.
5. **A heritage quarter:** an Afro-Brazilian old quarter beside a planned colonial-era housing estate, with a clear visual contrast between the two.
6. **A highlife-hotel nightlife strip:** an old bandleader's hotel and a cap-shaped national theatre as cultural landmarks.

#### 17. Things to avoid

## Mushin LGA: research brief for Gidi

The web-search limit for this run ran out before I'd covered everything. These parts are thin or unverified: the Hausa quarter in Idi-Araba, festivals, Nollywood, nightlife, and the exact rules on keke (tricycles). Anything I haven't checked is marked **[unverified]**. Points marked **[my knowledge]** come from general knowledge, not from a page I opened. Everything else comes from a page I opened.

#### 1. In one line
Mushin is a crowded, mostly Yoruba working-class district on the mainland, about 10 km north of the old city. It's known for markets, spare parts, street fights between rival groups, and stars who started there with nothing. Wande Coal named his first album after it.

#### 2. Areas inside it
- **Ojuwoye**: Wikipedia calls it the town centre. It has the main textile and general-goods market.
- **Mushin Olosha**: a mixed Yoruba, Igbo and Hausa community. The street-food guide calls its junction the place to buy suya in the evening.
- **Idi-Araba**: in the south, home to LUTH (Lagos University Teaching Hospital) and the Lagos State University College of Medicine. LUTH Road floods whenever it rains. Hausa-speaking northerners live here, and this was the centre of the 2002 Yoruba–Hausa clash.
- **Idi-Oro**: a market street. It saw clashes in 2017 and 2020, and has a fresh-food hub.
- **Papa Ajao**: a ward with gated streets and private guards. Ladipo Market sits here.
- **Odi-Olowo**: its own LCDA (a smaller council area inside the LGA). It was named in the 2022 okada ban.
- **Akala, Ilasamaja, Ilamose, Itire-Ijesha**: mostly homes. Youths from Akala were blamed for the 2017 Idi-Oro attack.
- **Fadeyi**: probably sits just outside the LGA, on the Ikorodu Road side **[unverified]**.

#### 3. Landmarks and monuments
- LUTH, Idi-Araba
- Ladipo Auto Spare Parts Market (Ladipo Street and Akinwunmi Lane). It claims to be Africa's biggest vehicle-parts market and draws buyers from Ghana and Côte d'Ivoire.
- Mushin Main Market, plus the Ojuwoye, Daleko, Awolowo and Irepodun markets
- Mushin station on the Red Line. Trains run Agbado → Ikeja → Oshodi → Mushin → Yaba → Oyingbo, and services opened on 15 Oct 2024.
- Matori Industrial Estate
- Mushin Central Mosque
- Celestial Church of Christ, Mushin parish (from an estate-agent page, so treat it as weak)
- Ovuomaroro Gallery, run by the artist Bruce Onobrakpeya
- Olosan police station

#### 4. Buildings and streets
- Wikipedia says about 55,000 people per km² in 2022 and calls the housing "low-quality".
- The typical building is the **face-me-I-face-you**: one long corridor of single rooms. Many are now being knocked down for 3-storey blocks of flats.
- A lot of land is "family land" with several people claiming to own it.
- Streets are narrow and laid out in a grid, with names like Alaafia, Daniel, Awawu, Olusoga, Alamutu, Odushina and Oloje.
- In 2020 one transformer served a whole cluster of streets. When it was damaged, the area went dark.
- Bukas (cheap food stalls) sit under flyovers and next to mechanics' workshops.

#### 5. Roads, junctions, bridges, traffic
- **Borders:** the Oshodi–Apapa Expressway on the north and west (including the Itire junction), Agege Motor Road on the east, and Bishop Street (the boundary with Surulere) on the south.
- **Other roads:** Ilasamaja Road, Isolo Road, Palm Avenue, Daleko Market Road, Mushin Road (Kayode bus stop), Itire Road.
- Roads from Lagos, Shomolu and Ikeja all meet here.
- Traffic is worst around the markets.

#### 6. Economy and markets
- **Factories:** Britannica and Wikipedia list cotton spinning and weaving, shoemaking, bicycle and motorcycle assembly, and powdered milk.
- **Ladipo:** sells used imported ("tokunbo") and new car parts.
- **Daleko:** wholesale rice and beans.
- **Ojuwoye:** textiles and household goods.
- **Informal money:** "illegal ticketing", meaning area boys charging fees for parking or loading, plus market taskforces and touting.
- In 2023 the state shut five markets for dumping waste.

#### 7. Transport
- Danfos (yellow minibuses), kekes (tricycles), and okadas (motorbike taxis) until the ban.
- **Okada ban:** Mushin and the Odi-Olowo LCDA were added from **1 Sept 2022**. By then 7,500 bikes had been seized and crushed.
- BRT buses run on the main roads.
- The Red Line train stops at Mushin.

#### 8. Sound, smell, look
- **5 a.m.:** cauldrons of ewa agoyin and akara are already bubbling.
- **Midday:** amala (yam-flour swallow) bukas are full.
- **After the markets close:** suya grills, charcoal smoke, loud bar music, and bus conductors shouting stops.
- **When it rains:** rubbish washes onto the road. One resident said "the whole environment will be filled with smelly water and refuse."

#### 9. People, languages, religion
- About 935,000 people (2022). Most are Yoruba.
- Igbo traders dominate Ladipo; this is implied by the 2016 clash reporting.
- There are Hausa communities, mainly in Idi-Araba.
- Yoruba and Pidgin are the street languages **[my knowledge]**.
- There are mosques and Aladura churches (such as the Celestial Church).
- The oba is Fatai Ayinla Aileru II.

#### 10. Food and nightlife
- **Dishes:** akara in Agege bread, ewa agoyin (near Daleko), amala with ewedu and gbegiri, offal and ponmo pepper soup, and suya with yaji spice.
- **Named joints:** Amala Babalola and White House Amala (both from SEO guides, so they're weak sources).
- **Music:** the local heritage is fuji **[unverified]**.

#### 11. History
- The area grew fast after about 1950. It got overcrowded, sanitation was poor, and industry moved in.
- The name supposedly means "picking the ishin fruit".
- **Feb 2002:** a Yoruba–Hausa clash at Idi-Araba spread to Fadeyi and Obanikoro. Reports said dozens died, a sergeant counted about 50 bodies, and the OPC (a Yoruba militia) was reported to be involved.
- **2015:** parts of Ladipo were demolished, which caused a run of crises.

#### 12. Danger and law
- **May 2016, Ladipo:** a fight over parking-fee money became an Igbo trader vs Yoruba resident clash. About 40 vehicles were burned. Police blamed a fight over food instead.
- **Apr 2017, Alamutu Street, Idi-Oro:** youths from Akala attacked with machetes and axes, looting and burning shops. The Rapid Response Squad (RRS) arrested 8.
- **Nov 2020:** a fight at a party on Alaafia Street spread across Idi-Araba and Idi-Oro. Three died, about 50 shops were looted and about 150 vehicles destroyed. Residents said police told them to "handle it yourselves". The attackers later came back with guns and drove police out.
- No cult name was given in any report I read.

#### 13. Environment
- LUTH Road floods every time it rains.
- Blocked drains, rubbish dumping, and markets shut by the state.
- The power transformer is a weak spot.
- Mushin is inland: no lagoon or sea.

#### 14. Festivals and sacred things
- Lagos police have limited egungun (masquerade) outings to approved venues because of fights between rival groups. That report didn't name Mushin.
- One estate page says Mushin has the Eyo festival. That's doubtful, because Eyo belongs to Lagos Island.
- **FLAG:** Never show these as criminal or as targets:
  - egungun or oro masquerades and their shrines
  - the oba and chieftaincy
  - mosques and churches

#### 15. In pop culture
- Wande Coal's *Mushin 2 Mo'Hits* (2009) is the classic grass-to-grace album. The title is the whole story: from Mushin to the Mo'Hits label.
- I couldn't confirm any Nollywood films set here.

#### 16. What Gidi can borrow
1. **A spare-parts maze district**, Ladipo-style. Narrow lanes of tokunbo parts. It works as a mission hub for stolen-part fencing, chop-shop chases, and Tunde collecting parts on his bike.
2. **"Ticketing" turf.** Area boys charge parking or loading fees on each street. If Tunde refuses to pay, the street turns hostile, and a small dispute can grow into a riot. Base this on the 2016 and 2020 timelines: a party fight, then a gunfire night, then a blackout.
3. **Blackout riot set piece.** A transformer gets hit, the streets go dark, and looters are out. Tunde has to get oja (goods) out by okada while the police pull back.
4. **Okada-ban enforcement waves.** Bike-crushing raids by a task force are a dynamic threat in some districts.
5. **Night food economy.** A suya junction hub, and a 5 a.m. akara run as the "day starts" beat.
6. **Flood-day variant.** Market-road missions in rubbish-filled floodwater, with a hospital road as the landmark.

#### 17. Things to avoid
- Real names: Ladipo, Olosha, LUTH, Wande Coal, Mo'Hits, Amala Babalola, the oba's name, and the real OPC.
- Real street names from crime reports (Alaafia, Alamutu, Daniel, Awawu).
- Recasting the 2002 Yoruba–Hausa killings as a playable ethnic war.
- Showing masquerades or shrines as gang assets.

## Shomolu (Somolu) LGA: research brief for Gidi

Note: the shared web-search budget ran out partway through, so I could not open sources on markets, food, religion or Pedro/Onipanu street detail. Anything marked **[unsourced]** comes from general knowledge and needs checking, ideally against the designer's own memory of Lagos.

#### 1. In one line
Shomolu is a dense, working-class Yoruba district where thousands of family-run print shops share houses with the families who run them. On its lagoon edge, Bariga mixes houses on stilts, rival street gangs, street rap and guerrilla theatre.

#### 2. Areas inside it
- **The eight official communities** are Onipanu, Okesuna/Alase, Igbari, Fadeyi/Igbobi, Bashua, Orile/Alade, Bajulaiye and Ijebu-Tedo. The LGA covers about 14.6 km² and is about 90% built up (Wikipedia).
- **Onipanu, Pedro and Akoka** are the better-planned parts. **Ilaje and Bariga** are the poorly planned ones (Wikipedia).
- **Bajulaiye Road and Oguntolu** are the heart of the printing area.
- **Bariga** became its own council area (LCDA) in 2013, with its secretariat at 19 Bawala Street. A canal separates it from Somolu. Its waterfront houses stand on stilts under the Third Mainland Bridge approach (Strangers Guide).
- **Ilaje (Bariga)** is a waterfront settlement with no safe drinking water, heavy waste and poor sewage (UNILAG).
- **Akoka** is the education district: the University of Lagos (UNILAG) and the Federal College of Education (Technical) (Wikipedia).

#### 3. Landmarks and monuments
- **CMS Grammar School** on Asani Road, Bariga. It was founded in 1859 and is often called Nigeria's oldest secondary school.
- **UNILAG campus** in Akoka.
- **Crown Art Factory**, the base of Crown Troupe of Africa in Bariga.
- **Fadeyi bus stop footbridge** on Ikorodu Road. The old steel bridge was falling apart and the state replaced it (Guardian).
- **The Third Mainland Bridge** runs above Bariga's stilt houses. People shuck oysters in its shadow.
- **Lady Lak**, a Bariga bus-stop area that turns up in crime reporting.
- **Oluwole Market**, a former forgery hub, has been demolished. That is useful as backstory.
- **[unsourced]** Onipanu Junction, which Wikipedia says hosts a live-chicken trading community.

#### 4. Buildings and streets
- Most buildings are both home and workplace. Print shops sit in small kiosks that look added on later, squeezed between multi-storey residential blocks (TechCabal).
- Multi-storey buildings are replacing old bungalows. The occupancy ratio is very high, and there is informal shack housing (Wikipedia).
- Bariga's waterfront has rusted tin roofs and houses on stilts. One bar is built high on stilts above the street. The alleys and walkways are tight (Strangers Guide).
- **[unsourced]** Face-me-I-face-you rooming houses (rows of rented rooms facing each other across a shared corridor), and walls covered in hand-painted and printed shop signs.

#### 5. Roads, junctions, bridges, traffic
- **Ikorodu Road** is the main road, with bus stops at Fadeyi, Onipanu and Obanikoro.
- **Third Mainland Bridge** and its Oworonshoki on-ramp sit next to Bariga.
- **Fogo Junction (Iyana Oworonshoki)** has the Somolu Central Taxi Park beside it.
- **Kiniun Ifa Junction** in the Sawmill area of Gbagada.
- **[unsourced]** Pedro Road, Bariga Road, Gbagada Expressway, and go-slows (traffic jams) on Ikorodu Road whenever it floods.

#### 6. Economy and markets
- Printing is what the area lives on. It employed more than 15,000 people in 2010, and there were roughly 200 print shops in the cluster in 2016.
- Customers are small businesses ordering short runs, such as 200 business cards.
- "Somolu prints" is a slur for cheap, low-quality offset printing (Zenodo).
- Forgery is widely believed to continue, reportedly done in the early morning. Police raids scare customers away (Wikipedia).
- Waterfront trades include oyster shucking and smoked mackerel ("Titus") sold on sticks.
- Work is precarious, rents are rising, and poverty is widespread.

#### 7. Transport
- Commercial okada (motorbike taxis) have been **banned in Shomolu since 1 September 2022**, enforced by an "Anti-Okada Squad" (Channels TV). For the game, this means riding okada here is illegal.
- Danfo (minibuses) and taxis use parks such as Somolu Central Taxi Park.
- **[unsourced]** Keke (tricycle) restrictions, BRT on Ikorodu Road, and canoes on the Ilaje waterfront.

#### 8. Sound, smell, look
- Print shops are loud, with machines whirring all the time. The air smells of ink, paper and heated laminating film (TechCabal).
- In Bariga: smoke from burning trash, drums everywhere, and kids practising on yellow plastic petrol jerrycans (Strangers Guide).
- When cult fights break out, shops close early and streets are empty by about 8 pm (WithinNigeria, 2019).

#### 9. People, languages, religion
- Mostly Yoruba, from the Egba, Awori, Ijebu and Ilaje subgroups.
- Languages are Yoruba, English and Pidgin.
- Wikipedia links stillbirths partly to people relying on prayer rather than hospital care.
- Christian and Muslim life is surely central, but I could not source specifics **[unsourced]**.

#### 10. Food and nightlife
- Sourced: beer parlours and bars, including the stilt bar, and smoked fish and oysters.
- Olamide's YBNL movement "began in Bariga" (Native Mag), and Lil Kesh and 9ice are also from Bariga.
- **[unsourced]** Bukas (cheap local eateries) serving amala, and student spots around UNILAG.

#### 11. History
- 1976: created out of the Mushin council as "Mushin East" LGA.
- 1986–2006: almost all of its wetlands were lost to construction.
- 1996: Crown Troupe founded in Bariga.
- 2005: police ranked Somolu second among Lagos LGAs for crime.
- 2013: Bariga made a separate council area (LCDA).
- 2022: okada ban.

#### 12. Danger and law
- **Touts and agberos** (motor-park and bus-stop extortion boys) take money in markets and at bus stops (Wikipedia).
- **Gang territory:** every Bariga street has its own strongman, and the canal is the line between two machete gangs (Strangers Guide).
- **Eiye vs Aiye confraternity wars:**
  - 2018: two cult leaders were attacked and killed at a political event at 228 Ikorodu Road, near Obanikoro. Police said they had led fights in Shomolu, Bariga, Fadeyi and Ojuelegba.
  - July 2019: revenge killings ran across Gbagada and Oworonshoki. A barber was shot in his shop and a trader was hacked to death at Fogo Junction.
  - August 2021: more than 20 cars and several shops were smashed across Ilupeju and Shomolu.
  - July 2023: an internal Aiye faction fight near Lady Lak left at least three dead.
- **Police:** residents say officers arrive late and suspects are released within days.

#### 13. Environment
- Almost all wetland has been built over.
- Ilaje has unsafe water, waste piles and poor sewage.
- Rooms flood an inch deep in the rainy season.
- Smoke from burning trash.
- **[unsourced]** Power cuts and generator noise.

#### 14. Festivals and sacred things
- The **Eko Theatre Carnival** is a week-long annual festival held in Bariga.
- Crown Troupe draws on Yoruba Alárìnjó (travelling masked theatre) and masquerade traditions.
- **FLAG:** never show masquerades (egungun), shrines, Ifa imagery or obas/baales (kings and community chiefs) as criminal. The real "Kiniun Ifa" place name shows how close Ifa sits to everyday street geography; use it carefully.

#### 15. In pop culture
- Olamide and YBNL ("Yahoo Boy No Laptop") come out of Bariga, along with Lil Kesh and 9ice.
- Crown Troupe's plays, including *Exodus* and *Aiyedun*.
- Nollywood and film references: not found.

#### 16. What Gidi can borrow
1. **A print-quarter district.** A maze of house-front press kiosks under constant machine noise. Mission type: run fake documents out of a back-room press at 4 a.m. before a police raid.
2. **A stilt waterfront under an elevated bridge.** Chase across plank walkways, with a stilt bar as a hideout and canoe escapes.
3. **A canal turf line.** One canal splits two gangs, and crossing it on a bike changes who chases you. Run fake rival societies, not Eiye or Aiye.
4. **An anti-okada squad.** A district where okada is banned. Tunde risks having his bike seized. Picking alleys versus main roads becomes a real decision.
5. **A street rapper NPC** who records in a cramped room and wants rides to shows, as an invented character.
6. **A "funeral rumour" event.** Social-media panic empties the streets at night and changes traffic and spawns.

#### 17. Things to avoid
- Real cult names (Eiye, Aiye, Idi Aba, Ogundola).
- Real people: Olamide, Lil Kesh, 9ice, Segun Adefila, police officers and named victims.
- YBNL, Crown Troupe, CMS Grammar School, UNILAG, Lady Lak, and the real names of print firms.
- Masquerades and Ifa as crime imagery.
- The real Third Mainland Bridge name. Invent a bridge instead.

## Oshodi-Isolo LGA: research brief for Gidi

**Note on coverage:** the shared web-search limit ran out partway through. Sections 4, 8, 9, 10, 14 and 15 have little sourced reporting behind them. Anything marked **[unsourced]** is general knowledge that Daramola should check against his own memory of Lagos.

#### 1. In one line
Oshodi is the place every route passes through: an old colonial-era market and motor-park crossroads that the state has spent 15+ years bulldozing into a "megacity" bus terminal. It is ringed by working- and middle-class residential districts (Isolo, Okota, Ejigbo, Ajao Estate) that flood and have bad roads.

#### 2. Areas inside it
The LGA has eleven wards: Oshodi/Bolade, Orile Oshodi, Mafoluku, Shogunle, Shogunle/Alasia, Isolo, Ajao Estate, Ilasamaja, Okota, Ishagatedo and Oke-Afa/Ejigbo. It covers about 45 km² and had about 931,300 people in 2022 (about 21,000 per km²).
- **Oshodi / Bolade:** markets, motor parks and the interchange. Bolade is residential but sits beside the Army Arena market.
- **Isolo:** a mix of self-contain rooms, block-of-flats, bungalows and older duplexes, with "older industrial zones quieter at night." It is near Oshodi–Apapa Expressway, Isolo Road and Agege Motor Road.
- **Ejigbo:** a crowded, multi-ethnic LCDA (wards Aigbaka, Ailegun, Fadu, Ifoshi, Ilamose, Oke-Afa). It is known for its broken Ifoshi Road.
- **Ilasamaja:** low-lying, swampy and salty-watered. It floods every rainy season.
- **Okota:** has estates such as Hope Estate, where residents raise their houses above ground level against flooding.
- **Mafoluku / Shogunle:** on the airport side. **[unsourced: airport-road districts]**

#### 3. Landmarks and monuments
- **Oshodi Transport Interchange (OTI):** three multi-storey terminals of about 30,000 m² each, about $70m, commissioned 24 April 2019. It has skywalks, CCTV and surveillance towers, and is billed as Africa's largest bus terminal.
- **Oshodi bridge / "under bridge"** on the Oshodi–Apapa Expressway, and **Oshodi Oke** (the upper level).
- **Markets:** Oshodi Market (Oshodi bus stop to Suwebatu Street); Abibat Mogaji Modern Market ("Kairo") on Oshodi Road; Oshodi Resettlement Market (formerly the Isopakodowo sawmill area), running into Araromi Market and Macarthy Market on Brown Street; Bolade Market, opposite the Nigerian Army Shopping Arena ("Arena").
- **Oke-Afa Canal and Oke-Afa Memorial Arcade:** the mass grave from 2002, with victims' names on a wall. It is now overgrown.
- **Capa Motor Park** (Mosafejo, Oshodi).
- **Rail tracks at Oshodi**, where young people from poor areas play chess.

#### 4. Buildings and streets
Isolo has rooms, flats, bungalows and duplexes. The Oshodi Resettlement Market has multi-storey blocks, which traders disliked because they preferred single-storey stalls. Arena uses containerised shops. Before the clearances, traders' stalls filled the streets "leaving motorists only an alley." Residents fill flooded roads with red earth themselves. **[unsourced: face-me-I-face-you blocks, hand-painted signage and uncompleted buildings are typical]**

#### 5. Roads, junctions, bridges, traffic
- **Main roads:** Oshodi–Apapa Expressway, Agege Motor Road, Oworonshoki–Oshodi–Mile 2 road, and the 10-lane Murtala Mohammed Airport Road (work started late 2017).
- **Ikotun–Ejigbo junction.** Ifoshi Road ("Ejigbo's valley of death") is full of gullies, and a 2–3 minute trip takes 30+ minutes. Stones packed into the gullies slash tyres.
- **Local bus routes from Terminal 3:** Mile 2/Festac, Ejigbo, Iyana Isolo/Jakande Gate/Itire, Ojota/Ketu/Mile 12, and Apapa/Wharf.

#### 6. Economy and markets
- Before the interchange, Oshodi had more than 5,600 buses loading daily across 13 motor parks, about 200,000 passengers and 1 million pedestrians a day.
- Old Oshodi was called Nigeria's second-largest market after Idumota.
- Owonifari was an electronics market under the bridge. Up to about 70% of its traders came from Ihiala, Anambra, and stalls were sublet so that 500 registered traders became about 2,000 actual traders.
- Arena's shops mostly sell clothing materials.
- Okada riders in Ejigbo said they paid the LCDA ₦600 a day in levies.
- Other work includes petty trade, artisans, workshops and warehouses.

#### 7. Transport
- **Buses:** Yellow danfos dominate; they lost free access during the 2018 rebuild. The interchange handles BRT and mass-transit buses, with Terminal 1 interstate, Terminal 2 intercity and Terminal 3 local.
- **Okada:** banned in Oshodi-Isolo LGA and Isolo LCDA from 1 September 2022. This matters a lot for Tunde's okada job.
- **Rail:** the planned Red Line has an Oshodi station.

#### 8. Sound, smell, look
- During the 2009 demolition, thick black smoke hung over the area from 3 a.m. to sunset.
- Walkways were jammed. The market "demanded a keen sense of sight and wit."
- Bag-snatching and rioting at nightfall. The under-bridge was a night hotspot until streetlights and a permanent task force were installed.
- **[unsourced: conductors shouting routes, generators, exhaust, gospel and fuji from shops]**

#### 9. People, languages, religion
- Ejigbo is described as "thousands of residents from many ethnic groups."
- There is a strong Igbo trading presence: Owonifari traders, and Okota estate chairmen with Igbo names.
- Churches are present, for example the Apostolic Church, Ilasamaja.
- **[unsourced: Yoruba and Pidgin are the street languages; many mosques]**

#### 10. Food and nightlife
I found no sourced material. **[unsourced: buka, suya and roadside amala near the parks]**

#### 11. History
- **Origins:** the market reportedly dates to 1860, though sources disagree. Oshodi grew with the colonial railway and is said to have been a camp in the Yoruba wars.
- **Founding of the LGA:** created under Governor Jakande in the Second Republic.
- **27 January 2002:** the Ikeja Cantonment armoury explosion. Fleeing crowds ran onto the hyacinth-covered Oke-Afa canal, mistaking it for dry ground. At least 600 drowned, many of them children. About 1,100 died in total.
- **4 January 2009:** the Fashola-era clearance of stalls and kiosks for "rowdiness, lawlessness and disorderliness."
- **2016:** the Owonifari market under the bridge was razed. The state claimed it found an arms bunker under the shops.
- **2018:** buildings demolished for the interchange, including some recently built ones whose traders had just paid a year's rent.
- **2019:** the interchange opens.

#### 12. Danger and law
- **Motor-park unions:** factions fought over the right to collect tolls.
- **May 2021:** a plain-clothes airman was found robbed and killed on Oshodi bridge. Soldiers then went to Capa Motor Park, agbero (park touts) stoned them and burned their patrol vehicles, and looting followed.
- **July 2021:** police raids on Oshodi Oke and under the bridge led to 103 arrests, with weapons and Indian hemp recovered, after reports of robberies in traffic.
- **Levies and violence in Ejigbo:** an okada rider said that people who protest local levies get attacked ("will plan and attack you").
- **Cults:** I found no sourced reporting of cult activity specific to this LGA.

#### 13. Environment
- Ilasamaja floods from the Aswani and Iyana Isolo axis through a narrow canal that was not desilted for about five years. Idris Balogun Street was submerged on 10 July 2012, and reptiles came into homes.
- Okota's Hope Estate floods because of drainage blocked by neighbouring sites.
- The water-hyacinth canals are dangerous.

#### 14. Festivals and sacred things
I found no sourced material. Ejigbo has an Oba's palace. **[unsourced: a likely Ejigbo/Isolo Oro or Egungun tradition]**

#### 15. In pop culture
- *Oshodi Oke* (2000) is a Yoruba Nollywood film. Actress Ronke Ojo took her stage name, "Ronke Oshodi Oke," from it.

#### 16. What Gidi can borrow
1. **Clearance-day set piece:** bulldozers arrive at 3 a.m. with a task force and black smoke. Tunde has a few hours to move a trader's oja (goods) before the market is razed.
2. **Two-level hub district:** an "Oke" deck of skywalks and CCTV towers above an "under bridge" zone of touts and pickpockets. They should feel like different worlds stacked on top of each other.

6. **Character:** an Igbo electronics trader evicted from under the bridge and selling from a container shop. He is a fence or quest-giver.

## Apapa LGA: research brief for Gidi

#### 1. In one line
Apapa is Nigeria's main seaport district. It was laid out as a colonial garden suburb and is now choked by trucks: a leafy GRA (Government Reserved Area) on one side, poor rail-line settlements on the other, and thousands of tankers and container trucks in between.

#### 2. Areas inside it
- **Apapa GRA:** low density, tree-lined avenues, large plots, colonial bungalows and modern villas. It was an elite colonial reserve and is now home to maritime executives. Residents say it has declined badly.
- **Wharf / commercial area** (Wharf Road, Creek Road, Burma Road, Warehouse Road, Kofo Abayomi Avenue, Randle Road): mid-rise offices for shipping, insurance and logistics firms. Reporters describe these streets as turned into "trailer parks."
- **Ijora / Badia:** working-class, informally built areas in the north, home to port and factory workers. Badia sits along the railway line.
- **Tin Can Island / Coconut / Liverpool:** the second port, tank farms and the truck approach.
- **Pelewura, Marine Road, Point Road, Calcutta Crescent, Apapa Quays:** residential streets and estates.
- Boundaries are uncertain. Badia is in Apapa-Iganmu LCDA, and Olodi-Apapa and Kirikiri may sit partly in neighbouring LGAs.

#### 3. Landmarks and monuments
- **Lagos Port Complex** ("the Wharf", from 1913) and **Tin Can Island Port** (1977).
- **Apapa Amusement Park:** opened as Lagos Lunar Park in 1976, demolished in 2012, rebuilt in an "Arabian" style with flat roofs, horn ornaments and dark yellow walls. Entry is free.
- **Apapa Club:** 1930s, with tennis, squash and a pool.
- **Apapa Mall:** has a cinema.
- **Bridges:** Ijora Bridge, Marine Bridge, Liverpool Bridge, Coconut Bridge, the Apapa–Ijora–Costain bridge and Eko Bridge.
- **Others:** Area B Police Command, the disused Apapa North railway station, Apapa Railway Station, the Flour Mills site, Ijora Central Mosque and the Military Hospital.

#### 4. Buildings and streets
- **Two layers:** a planned 1950s industrial and housing estate of about 1,000 acres sits on top of informal rail-line settlements. In Badia, the multi-room buildings were torn down in the 2015 evictions and replaced by zinc, tarpaulin and tent shelters.
- **Port edge:** warehouses, tank farms, flour mills and silos. One writer said "almost all the land locations with access to the sea are now tank farms."
- **Wide but wrecked roads:** a four-lane dual carriageway at Ijora, failed sections, potholes "deep enough to almost swallow an SUV," broken drains and no street lights at night.
- **Decline:** many landlords and offices have left. In 2014 property values had fallen by up to 50% and vacancy was above 40%.

#### 5. Roads, junctions, bridges, traffic
- **Oshodi–Apapa Expressway:** built 1975–78, collapsed through neglect, rebuilt in concrete from 2019 to about 2021.
- **Choke points:** Mile 2, Kirikiri, Coconut, Liverpool roundabout, Ijora Olopa, Cocoa Board, Marine Beach, Creek Road and the Tin Can first and second gates.
- **Journey times:** a drive that should take under five minutes takes about two hours on a good day and four to five hours on a bad one.
- **Tankers** queue on Liverpool Road for the tank farms.
- **Extortion (Vanguard, 2023):** 58 extortion points between the expressway and the ports. A truck paid about ₦61,000 inward to Tin Can and about ₦52,000 on the Apapa corridor. Collectors included police, LASTMA (Lagos traffic officers), the "ECOMOG" park-and-garage enforcers, "Oduduwa boys," the dockworkers' union, the LGA itself and area boys. "Mr Biggs" alone took ₦10,000.
- **The electronic call-up system** for booking truck entry into the ports was called "collapsed" and "fraud" by truckers.

#### 6. Economy and markets
- **Formal:** the ports (the Apapa container terminal has been run by Maersk's APM Terminals since 2005), Customs, clearing agents, tank farms, flour and grain mills, shipping firms and the ThisDay newspaper.
- **Informal:** okada riders, roadside vendors, mechanics, touts, the "traffic managers" collecting tolls, sex work, and drugs sold under bridges.
- **Smuggling:** in 2018 Customs seized 13 containers of tramadol and other drugs at Tin Can, worth ₦3.13bn in duty-paid value. About a day earlier it had seized 40 tramadol containers at Apapa, worth ₦7.3bn.

#### 7. Transport
- **Vehicles:** articulated trucks, tankers and flatbeds dominate. Officials said fewer than 20% were roadworthy and about 90% had no rear lights.
- **Okada:** "a battalion" of okada riders controlled movement inside Apapa until a ban on the Apapa axis around February 2020. A clearing agent asked: "Do you expect us to trek from Mile 2 to Apapa?" Riders said the taskforce extorted them after the ban.
- **Workarounds:** one office worker bought a 650cc power bike to get through the trucks.
- **Rail:** freight rail at Apapa terminal. The Blue Line stops nearby at Iganmu and National Theatre.

#### 8. Sound, smell, look
- **Sound:** "a deafening cacophony of horns" from heavy trucks.
- **Smell:** a "very choking smell of urine" on Creek Road, flies swarming, and rubbish and human waste wrapped in food packs and newspaper and thrown on the roadside.
- **Look:** containers swaying on trailers, tankers parked on street corners, mud when it rains.
- **Night:** no street lights. Phone snatching under Liverpool Bridge peaks around 9pm. Lebanese-run restaurants and clubs once kept Apapa busy "24 hours."

#### 9. People, languages, religion
- **Origins:** the indigenous people are Awori Yoruba. Apapa started as a fishing village.
- **Population:** 217,362 in 2006, with a mix of port workers and migrants (one cult-clash victim was a trader of northern origin).
- **Religion:** Christian and Muslim festivals are celebrated side by side. Ijora Central Mosque and Liberty Church of Zion (Badia) are named in reporting.
- I found no sourced breakdown of languages. Yoruba and Pidgin are a reasonable assumption but unverified.

#### 10. Food and nightlife
- Named venues include The Pub (live music), Stephanie's Bar on Calcutta Crescent, Doris Bar, Avondale Club, the Bullus Hotel nightclub, Apapa Club House, Arewa African Cuisine, Kobis and Tantalizers.
- The space under Liverpool Bridge, once a food market, was described in 2021 as a "red zone." Truck drivers, clearing agents, okada riders and uniformed men drink, smoke "Indian hemp" (weed) and buy sex there, and cheap unregistered herbal alcohol is openly on sale.

#### 11. History
- 1907: a 16th-century bronze hoard was found.
- 1913: harbour moles let ocean ships in. 1919: a wharf was extended to Apapa.
- 1926: berths opened. 1950–58: the industrial and housing estate was built.
- 1960s: port extensions. 1977: Tin Can port. 2005: the ports were concessioned to private operators.
- 2013 and 2015: forced evictions in Badia.
- 2019–21: the expressway was rebuilt.

#### 12. Danger and law
- **Badia cult war, December 2018:** the "Railway boys" fought the "Suara boys." Seven people died over three days and 16 were arrested. Residents said the real culprits got away and brought in the OPC (a Yoruba vigilante group) for protection.
- **Liverpool Bridge informant:** "this place is like Fela shrine, they don't arrest people" there for smoking weed.
- **Traffic robbers** work the stalled jams at Coconut.
- **Police** sit on both sides of the system. They protected the eviction bulldozers and also appear on the extortion lists.

#### 13. Environment
- **Tanker fires:**
  - January 2014: a tanker fire at Olodi-Apapa killed 15.
  - 2013: an explosion at a Tin Can tank farm.
  - October 2023: a tanker rolled back while climbing a bridge at inward Costain/Ijora and started a fire involving about nine vehicles.
- **Other:** failed drainage turns roads impassable in rain. A 2014 op-ed warned that a single fire could form a "ring" from Kirikiri through Tin Can to Ijora.

#### 14. Festivals and sacred things
- **The Ojora of Ijora** is the traditional ruler of the Awori Ijora kingdom. He is chosen from four ruling families through **Ifa consultation**. The Ojora chieftaincy family, as landowners, carried out the 2015 Badia demolitions.
- **FLAG:** never show Ifa divination, the Ojora's crown or regalia, or the Ojora stool as criminal or comic. Do not use real mosques or churches as crime sets.
- I found no sourced Apapa-specific masquerade or festival.

#### 15. In pop culture
I found nothing verifiable. Ajegunle's music scene is next door, but that is my assumption and unverified, so it needs its own research.

#### 16. What Gidi can borrow
1. **"The Toll Run":** Tunde rides oja through a chain of uniformed and union collectors. Each stop is pay, argue, bribe or run, and the money adds up across the route.
2. **Truck-canyon district:** a dock zone where parked tankers form moving walls. Only an okada fits through the gaps, which makes Tunde the fastest way in.
3. **Set piece:** a tanker rolls back on a bridge climb and sets off a chain fire, followed by an escape run or rescue.
4. **"Under the Bridge":** an open-air night hub with no law, which works as a mission-giver and fence location.
5. **The container that isn't what the paperwork says:** a fictional pill shipment worked through a corrupt clearing agent.
6. **Dual district:** a quiet garden-reserve enclave for the bosses, sealed in by gridlock.

#### 17. Things to avoid
- **Real names:** Ojora and the Ijora stool, the Railway and Suara cult names, APM Terminals/Maersk, Dangote, Flour Mills, MRS, Sifax, ThisDay, Mr Biggs, LASTMA, ECOMOG, the dockworkers' union and the OPC.
- **Real people:** the named victims and officials.
- **Places:** real mosques and churches.

## Ajeromi-Ifelodun LGA (Ajegunle, "AJ City"): research notes for Gidi

I hit the shared web-search limit before I could search for food and nightlife, festivals and chiefs, or films set here. Those sections are thin and marked **[unverified]**. Everything else comes from pages I opened.

**Name trap:** there is a second Ajegunle on Ikorodu Road (Owode-Elede/Kosofe side), known for canoe ferries during floods. It is a different place, so I left its material out.

#### 1. In one line
A port-side ghetto of about 1 million people on 12.49 km² of reclaimed marshland, roughly 81,000 people per km². It is multi-ethnic and was the birthplace of "AJ music" and galala dance. Lagos calls it "Jungle City"; its official nickname is "The Land of Opportunities."

#### 2. Areas inside it
Wikipedia lists these localities: Ajegunle (the commercial and cultural heart), Boundary (the transit and trading node), Olodi-Apapa, Tolu, Wilmer, Layeni, Amukoko, Suru-Alaba, Cardoso, Orodu, Temidire, Onibaba, Ashafa, Awodi, Aiyetoro and Araromi Ajeromi. The Native also names Orege, Awodi-Ora, Tolu Complex and Kirikiri Road, and calls Boundary Market the "real" Ajegunle. The homes sit between the Oshodi–Apapa and Lagos–Badagry Expressways, with the Apapa and Tin Can ports to the east and south. I found no reliable source on how the neighbourhoods differ in character.

#### 3. Landmarks
- **Boundary Market and Boundary Roundabout.** The market has a "plank section" that burned on 25 September 2020; LASEMA and the Federal Fire Service put it out.
- **Wilmer Market and Odunade Market.**
- **Ajegunle Central Mosque.**
- **Ajeromi General Hospital**, which has a morgue.
- **Abayomi Hall**, used for community events.
- **Jetties at Wilmer and Ajegunle**, both underused.
- **Africa Terminals fuel depot**, by the water near the Oshodi–Apapa Expressway.
- **Boundary Road–Aiyeke Bridge.**
- **Jahoha Studio**, the studio of Mighty Mouse, "Father of AJ Music." I don't know where it is.

#### 4. Buildings and streets
- The old housing stock is ten-room "face-me-I-face-you" bungalows with shared toilets and kitchens, poor ventilation and rusted brown roofs (BusinessDay, 2019).
- A 2007 Washington Post piece describes dirt roads, metal shacks, homes and shops made of rusty tin and thin boards, and waist-high piles of rubbish.
- Gentrification is replacing these block by block with two- to four-storey flats, hotels, event halls and bars. The new buildings have Plaster of Paris (POP) decoration and bright paint. One example: 251 Ojo Road went from a face-me-I-face-you motel to modern flats. City Point Hotel stands near Itire bus stop.
- Many small hotels on quiet streets hosted school parties.
- The Building Collapse Prevention Guild lists Ajegunle as needing urban renewal.

#### 5. Roads, junctions, traffic
- Main roads named in the 2020 okada-restriction list: Ojo Road, Mobile/Malu Road (up to Boundary Road), Boundary Road–Aiyeke Bridge, Industry Road, Mba/Cardoso Street (up to Ota Wharf), and Cemetery/Mosafejo/Alaba Road.
- The area is boxed in by the Mile 2–Apapa corridor. There, tankers and trucks queuing for the ports and tank farms back up to Cele bus stop. Commuters lose hours, and one tanker driver reported waiting three days (Shipping Position, December 2023).
- A 2013 Vanguard headline warned that Ajegunle was "on verge of isolation" because of bad link roads. I saw that headline cited on Wikipedia but did not open the article.

#### 6. Economy
- Trade first: the big markets and street hawking.
- The 2007 Washington Post piece mentions vegetable stalls, plantain and bread hawkers, and craftspeople making hats, sandals and belts.
- Port and maritime work at Apapa and Tin Can was the reason the area filled up with workers and migrants in the 1970s.
- Music, DJing and studio work are a real, if informal, economy.
- Hotels and event centres are the new money.

#### 7. Transport
- Danfo buses and keke tricycles are the main transport.
- Okada were restricted on 13 named roads in the LGA from 1 February 2020.
- The Lagos Blue Line has stations close by at Suru Alaba, Iganmu and Mile 2.
- There are jetties, but they see little use.
- Trucks and tankers dominate the edges of the area.

#### 8. Sound, smell, look
- Hot and humid, around 88% humidity.
- Bright red tomato and pepper stalls stand out against grey tin.
- Barefoot kids play around rubbish heaps, and men play chess at street tables (Washington Post, 2007).
- Music comes out of hotels and DVD shops (The Native mentions one playing African China), and vendors sell drinks and ice cream at school parties.
- Night means dance halls and DJs.
- I have no sourced detail on how the day runs from morning to night **[unverified]**.

#### 9. People, languages, religion
- Yoruba are the majority, but people come from all over Nigeria and from neighbouring countries. There are Ijaw, Ilaje, Isoko and other communities; Daddy Showkey, for example, is Isoko.
- Yoruba, English and Pidgin are the working languages. The music is sung largely in Pidgin.
- Christianity and Islam are the main religions. Musicians perform in churches as well as clubs.

#### 10. Food and nightlife
- **Food:** I have no sourced detail. Expect buka, roadside suya and the like, but treat that as **[unverified]**.
- **The AJ sound:** ragga, reggae and dancehall loops mixed with praise-singing taken from Fuji music, galala dance, and the "Kwete" dance.
- **Key DJs:** Solo B, DJ Webo, DJ C3.
- **Artists:** Kosere Master, Daddy Showkey, Daddy Fresh, Baba Fryo, African China, Marvellous Benji, Oritsefemi, Danfo Drivers, Mighty Mouse.
- **Others from the area:** Don Jazzy and Patoranking; footballers Samson Siasia, Taribo West, Emmanuel Amuneke and Odion Ighalo.

#### 11. History
- Settlers from two communities, Ajeromi and Ifelodun, merged as the area grew in the mid-20th century.
- The 1970s port boom (Apapa, Tin Can) filled the marshland with workers.
- It became an LGA on 1 October 1976, split from the old Ojo LGA.
- **October/November 1999:** OPC (Oodua People's Congress) members attacked Ijaw youths at a funeral. The Ijaw burned the OPC headquarters in reprisal. At least 12 people died, there was an 8pm–6am curfew, and 56 people were charged in court.
- An Ijaw/Ilaje conflict inspired African China's 2007 album *CRISIS*.
- The music scene peaked in the late 1990s to early 2010s and has faded since.

#### 12. Danger and law
- **August 2016:** rival cult groups based on Kehinde Street and Izeagwu Street clashed, and an 18-year-old was killed. Police from the local Ajegunle station made two arrests.
- **April 2024:** cult clashes on Nasamu Street and Iyalode Street. Six commercial vehicles were damaged at Boundary Roundabout and one suspect died. Police responded with foot and vehicle patrols.
- The Native describes rival school gangs fighting with stones, knives, machetes and sometimes guns near Boundary Market.

#### 13. Environment
- The ground is low-lying reclaimed marsh, and flooding comes every year and is getting worse.
- On Opeleye Street, a trader lost his shop and homes took on water. Residents say the canals are blocked by rubbish or built over. (Guardian, undated.)
- In October 2021, Arise TV reported people living in "several feet" of floodwater.
- I found nothing sourced on power cuts or port pollution **[unverified]**.

#### 14. Festivals and sacred things
- I could not identify a traditional ruler or festival for Ajeromi **[unverified]**.
- **Flags for the game:** never show mosques, churches or (if you add them) Ijaw and Yoruba shrines and masquerades as gang hideouts or as targets.
- Don't tie criminal factions to a real ethnic group. The 1999 OPC–Ijaw violence is a real, painful event.

#### 15. In pop culture
- **Albums:** African China, *CRISIS* (2007).
- **Songs:** Daddy Showkey's "Fire Fire" (1991), "Diana" (1996) and "The Name" (2000); Oritsefemi's "Double Wahala"; Danfo Drivers' "Kpolongo"; Kosere's "Designer L'omo."
- **Albums:** Dagga Tolar's *Social Revolution*.
- **Today:** Ruger, Bella Shmurda and Zinoleesky carry on the street sound.
- **Films:** I found no Nollywood films set here **[unverified]**.

#### 16. What Gidi can borrow
1. **A "Jungle" district** wedged between a port and two expressways. Its only exits are choked by tanker and truck queues, so escaping a chase means weaving through stalled trailers.
2. **A market-fire set piece:** a fire breaks out in the plank section of a Boundary-style market while Tunde is mid-delivery and has to get his goods out.
3. **A mission type: "street-to-street" turf.** Factions are named after streets and tied to no ethnic group, and fights flare at a roundabout full of keke and danfo.
4. **An okada lockout mechanic:** certain named roads are banned for okada, and riding them draws task-force chases.
5. **A character type: the AJ DJ or producer**, a Mighty Mouse-like mentor running a cheap studio. He runs a side questline with a school-party gig at a small hotel. The radio station would play an invented "AJ sound."
6. **A flood-season district state:** knee-deep streets, blocked canals and an underused jetty that suddenly matters.

#### 17. Things to avoid
- Real artist names and likenesses, and their song titles (Showkey, Kosere, African China, Mighty Mouse/Jahoha).

- Real cult-group names.

- Real company names: Africa Terminals, LASEMA, Paradigm Initiative.
- Real victims.
- Religious buildings as crime locations.

## Ikeja LGA: research brief for Gidi

#### 1. In one line
Ikeja is Lagos State's capital and the mainland's commercial centre. Government offices at Alausa, Africa's biggest phone and gadget market, an airport, an army barracks and Fela's Shrine all sit within a few kilometres of each other.

#### 2. Areas inside it
- **Alausa**: the state government seat. It has the Secretariat, the House of Assembly and Lagos House. Expect official buildings, a park, and protest gatherings.
- **Otigba / Computer Village**: a dense, unplanned grid of streets that used to be homes and offices and is now multi-storey phone malls.
- **Allen Avenue and Opebi**: offices and banks by day, nightlife and sex work by night. Locals call Allen "a mixed place".
- **Ikeja GRA**: once a quiet area of low-rise colonial homes. It is now walled high-rise apartments and hotels on Isaac John Street, and many of its roads are potholed.
- **Oregun, Agidingbi, Ogba**: industrial and media areas, with factories, TV stations and the Guinness brewery.
- **Onigbongbo / Maryland**: a mall and a major junction.
- **Ipodo**: the traditional town core, home of Obada market.
- Wikipedia's district list also includes Anifowose, Akiode, Ojodu and Magodo. Some of these technically fall in neighbouring LGAs or LCDAs (unverified).

#### 3. Landmarks and monuments
- Lagos State Secretariat and House of Assembly (Alausa). Lagos House (Governor's office).
- **JJT Park** (Johnson Jakande Tinubu), next to the Assembly, opened in 2017.
- **Kanu Ndubuisi Park** (Oregun), with a "Statue of Legends" and a waterfall.
- Statue of Obafemi Awolowo, and the Statue of the Lagos Idejo Chiefs.
- **A bust of MKO Abiola** in the central garden at Allen. Cars stop around it at night.
- **Foot Statue** at Berger roundabout (2017). Maryland Monument.
- **New Afrika Shrine**: a church-like building with two bent hands over the entrance, holding about 2,500 people.
- **Kalakuta Museum** at 7 Gbemisola St, with Fela's grave by the gate.
- Lagbaja's **Motherland** venue (Opebi).
- **Ikeja City Mall** (194 Obafemi Awolowo Way), the biggest mall on the mainland. Also Maryland Mall.
- **Ikeja Bus Terminal** (Simbiat Abiola Way). Ikeja Cantonment.
- **Archbishop Vining Memorial Church** (Oba Akinjobi Way).
- **Obada market** (Ipodo).
- Murtala Muhammed International Airport, plus "Airways House" and other airline head offices.

#### 4. Buildings and streets
- **Computer Village**: former houses turned into stacked mini-malls, phones in glass showcases, and sellers working off car bonnets, walkways and makeshift display units. It overloads the power grid and has almost no parking.
- **GRA**: the old single-storey bungalows are being replaced by tall, walled apartment blocks. Streets are named after notable people (Sobo Arobiodu, Oduduwa Way, Remi Fani-Kayode Avenue).
- **Alausa**: wide boulevards, institutional blocks and landscaped parks.
- **Industrial estate** (from the mid-1960s): factory sheds and walled compounds.
- **Allen at night**: yellow streetlights mixed with blue hotel neon.
- A slum in Ikeja was a filming location for the Nollywood film *Ojuju*. Informal housing exists here, but I have no specific source on where.

#### 5. Roads, junctions, traffic
- Main roads: **Obafemi Awolowo Way** (Awolowo Way), **Oba Akran Road**, **Allen Avenue roundabout**, **Toyin Street**, **Adeniyi Jones**, **Opebi Road** and Opebi Link, **Mobolaji Bank Anthony Way** (Airport Road).
- **Ikeja Under Bridge** is the transport knot next to Computer Village, where many roads and bus routes meet.
- Bus stops: Salvation stop (Opebi), Allen junction, Berger, Maryland.
- The state taskforce regularly clears street traders, illegal ticketers (fare-collecting touts) and unapproved bus stops from Awolowo Way, Oba Akran, the Under Bridge and Tonade Street. Goods are seized and people are arrested.

#### 6. Economy and markets
- **Computer Village** started in 1997 with about 10 shops and now has more than 10,000. It sells and repairs phones and computers and draws traders from across Africa. Its trade body is CAPDAN. It has two classes of trader: registered shop owners and unregistered "free" hawkers who move around.
- **Pharmaceutical plants** (for example May & Baker), the **Guinness brewery**, and TV and media (Channels, AIT, Lagos Television, Radio Lagos/Eko FM). The many airline offices and advertising agencies add a formal white-collar layer.
- **Informal work**: hawking at junctions, food vending at night, ticket touts, and sex work on Allen.

#### 7. Transport
- **Danfo**: yellow-and-black minibuses, organised under NURTW (the transport workers' union).
- **BRT buses** using the Cowry tap card.
- **Ikeja Bus Terminal** handles more than 50,000 passengers a day and has air conditioning and food courts.
- **Red Line rail**: Ikeja station opened to the public on 15 Oct 2024. It runs Agbado to Oyingbo, ₦500 for one zone.
- **Okada ban**: from **1 June 2022**, Lagos banned okada in six LGAs, including Ikeja. It followed the lynching of a passenger in Lekki. Past bans have tended to lose enforcement within months.
- Keke tricycles operate here. The airport means cabs, Uber and Bolt.

#### 8. Sound, smell, look
- **Computer Village**: loud music from advertisers, hawkers grabbing your arm and whispering offers, people watching the street, Yoruba and Pidgin, and crowds that gather instantly around any dispute.
- **Night on Allen and Opebi**: the area wakes up slowly around 9:30pm and fills up after midnight. Suya and pepper soup smoke. Mutari's noodle stall runs 8pm to 6am on Fridays.
- **The Shrine**: loud Afrobeat through bad speakers, a thick cloud of weed smoke under a "no drugs" sign, plastic tables on the road, and teenage boys hustling passers-by.

#### 9. People, languages, religion
- The original settlers are the Awori, from Ota. The name comes from "Akeja", a deity.
- Population is about 470,000 (2022 estimate).
- Languages heard: Yoruba and Pidgin (confirmed in Computer Village). There is a large Igbo trading presence in Computer Village by common knowledge, but I did not verify it.
- Both Christian and Muslim life are present. The palace and traditional authority are covered in section 14.

#### 10. Food and nightlife
- **Food**: suya, pepper soup, noodles with egg and bread at night stalls, bukas such as Commint Buka (Allen), and mall food courts.
- **Clubs**: The Night Shift Colosseum (9pm to 5am), Options (Allen/Toyin T-junction, 10pm to 5am), New Afrika Shrine (Femi Kuti shows), Motherland. GRA has upscale hotel bars and restaurants.

#### 11. History
- Slave raids hit the area until the mid-1800s.
- The Lagos to Ibadan railway arrived in 1901, followed by suburban growth.
- An industrial estate was set up in the mid-1960s.
- Ikeja became the state capital in 1976.
- Fela moved to Ikeja after soldiers burned his Surulere home in Feb 1977.
- Computer Village began in 1997, after the market moved from Ogunlana Drive, Surulere.
- **27 Jan 2002, Ikeja Cantonment explosion**: a fire in a street market next to the army munitions store set it off. At least 1,100 people died. More than 600 of them, many children, drowned when they fled into a canal they couldn't see because it was covered in water hyacinth. More than 12,000 people were left homeless. An inquiry blamed the army.
- **19 Oct 2020**: #EndSARS protesters (against police brutality) shut down the Alausa Secretariat.

#### 12. Danger and law
Sahara Reporters (2018) documented these patterns in Computer Village:
- **Phone-swap scam**: the seller swaps in a dud while the buyer counts money.
- **Lure-and-vanish**: a "come to my shop" trick where the stranger disappears.
- **"London used" faulty phones**.
- **Stolen phones**, linked to robbers in Mushin and Obalende.
- **Fake police** used to intimidate victims.
- **Extortion**: hawkers seized a reporter's phone, moved the reporters to a deserted corner and took ₦8,000. A police station sits in the middle of the market, and officers were reluctant to help.

Elsewhere: sex work on Allen; regular taskforce raids on traders and ticket touts. I found no sourced cult or gang incidents specific to Ikeja, so treat any as unverified.

#### 13. Environment
- **Flooding**: GRA floods every year because of poor drainage. Ikeja roads were cut off in the floods of 30 June 2026, which were blamed on blocked channels and buildings on waterways.
- **Roads**: badly potholed in GRA, with weekly suspension repair bills for drivers.
- **Power**: Computer Village overloads the grid, which suggests generator noise (inferred).
- Climate is tropical monsoon, wet in most months.

#### 14. Festivals and sacred things
- The **Olu of Ikeja** stool (traditional king) has been empty since Oba Amore died on 1 July 2014. Three ruling houses (Oshoja, Iyade, Amore) dispute it, and the kingmakers are the Akogun, Ekerin and Balogun.
- **FLAG**: the Akeja deity, the Olu's stool and its chiefs, Oro or Egungun rites (general Yoruba practice, not sourced for Ikeja specifically), and churches and mosques must **never be portrayed as criminal**.
- Also handle the 2002 blast victims and Fela's grave with respect.

#### 15. In pop culture
- Fela Kuti, Kalakuta and the Shrine.
- Lagbaja's Motherland.
- Femi Kuti's shows at the Shrine.
- The film *Ojuju* (2014), shot in an Ikeja slum.
- I verified no specific songs or books about Ikeja.

#### 16. What Gidi can borrow
1. **"Gadget Village" district**: a maze of converted houses where Tunde fences, collects or delivers phones. Use the swap scam as a minigame, and a crowd that forms around any argument and takes sides.
2. **Capital-district protest event**: the government quarter gets blockaded. Roads close, traffic diverts and the city's mood changes (based on #EndSARS at Alausa).
3. **Okada-ban districts**: certain districts are "no okada" zones with roving taskforce patrols. The heat rises if Tunde rides there, which makes the ban a core system for an okada hero.
4. **Night strip**: a neon roundabout with a bust in the middle, all-night noodle and suya stalls, hotel touts, and a live-music hall playing Afrobeat from bad speakers.
5. **Barracks-explosion backstory**: a fictional armoury disaster from 20 years earlier, and a canal everyone fears. This gives older characters trauma and gives the canal a meaning.
6. **Under-bridge hub**: a bus terminal knot where touts (agberos) collect "ticket" fees from drivers. This is a good place for Tunde's motor-park boss to operate.

#### 17. Things to avoid
- Real names: Computer Village, Otigba, Allen Avenue, Ikeja City Mall, Shoprite, Silverbird, Guinness, Channels, AIT, BRT/Cowry, NURTW, LASTMA.
- Real people: Fela, Femi Kuti, Lagbaja, Awolowo, MKO Abiola, Sanwo-Olu, Tinubu.
- The real Shrine building design.
- The real Olu of Ikeja stool and its ruling houses, and the Akeja deity.
- Real victims of the 2002 blast and the actual name of the cantonment.

## Agege LGA: research brief for Gidi

A shared web-search limit ran out partway through, so pop culture, festivals and nightlife are thin. Anything marked *[unverified]* is general knowledge I could not check against a source.

#### 1. In one line
Agege is an old Awori town that grew into a crowded railway trading town. It is known for the bread named after it, the abattoir's cattle and the Hausa community by the rail line. A blogger I read called it "noisy, busy, angry, and, parts of it, muddy."

#### 2. Areas inside it
- **Orile Agege:** the old Awori core ("proper Agege"), with Isale Odan, Ipodo, Magbon and Mulero. The Alayige (the area's traditional ruler) is based here.
- **Agege Station / the rail corridor:** historically a separate traders' settlement around the railway. It is the commercial heart and home to the Hausa community.
- **Ogba** (Ashade, Alausa, Agbede): a separate kingdom with its own ruler, the Ologba.
- **Oko-Oba:** the abattoir and cattle-market zone off Succo Road.
- **Dopemu:** a major junction on the boundary road.
- **Also listed:** George (Omole, Aguda, Suberu), Ajegunle, Sango, Keke, Oniwaya, Papa-Uku, Oyewole and Tabon-Tabon.
- **Density:** about 683,600 people on 12.25 km², roughly 55,800 per km² (2022 figure, from a poorly edited Wikipedia page).

#### 3. Landmarks and monuments
- **Pen Cinema:** once a cinema at the heart of Agege's era as an entertainment hub. The name now survives as a bus stop and junction. I could not confirm when it was built or closed.
- **Pen Cinema Flyover:** 1.4 km dual carriageway opened on 5 March 2021. It runs along Agunbiade Street over Old Abeokuta Road, the railway and Iju-Ishaga Road, with a ramp toward Abule-Egba. Five side roads were rebuilt as bypasses.
- **Agege Stadium:** 4,000 seats on grass. Home of MFM F.C. and DreamStar Ladies; also hosts trade fairs.
- **Oko-Oba Abattoir and cattle market.**
- **Agege rail station:** a stop for the Lagos-Ibadan train and the Red Line.
- **Statue of Oba Agege.**
- **Others:** the Agege Local Government secretariat, the NYSC orientation camp on Iyana-Ipaja Road, a LASU campus, and the Heritage Center, a six-storey mall on Ifako-Ijaiye Road with a planned cinema.

#### 4. Buildings and streets
- **What sources say:** very high density. Balogun Road and Desalu Street lead to open ground where the rail tracks cross. There are puddles and mud after rain, and Hausa families sit on the tracks.
- **[unverified]:** I found no source on architecture. My general picture is two- to three-storey face-me-I-face-you blocks, mixed-use buildings with shops below, and shanties and scrap yards along the rail line. Streets are narrow, side roads are unpaved, and painted shop signs are everywhere.

#### 5. Roads, junctions, bridges, traffic
- **Main roads:** Agege Motor Road, Old Abeokuta Road, Iju-Ishaga Road, Ipaja Road, and Dopemu Road (now Babajide Sanwo-Olu Road). The Lagos-Abeokuta Expressway runs along the edge.
- **Traffic:** before the 2021 flyover, the Pen Cinema intersection had "perennial gridlock." Residents describe the area as a back route from Ikeja to Oshodi that avoids the expressway.
- **Bus stops and junctions:** Pen Cinema, Iyana-Dopemu, Cement and Ile Zik.
- **Back streets:** 2022 diversion routes ran through Oyewole, Seriki, Kolawole, Adebambo, Adealu, Markaz Road, Awori Street, Oniwaya and Alfa Nla.

#### 6. Economy and markets
- **History:** from the 1907 Agege Planters' Union onward, Agege was a cocoa and kola hub. In 1933 over half of Nigeria's kola reportedly passed through its railway goods sheds.
- **Today:**
  - Oko-Oba abattoir and meat market, with Fulani cattle breeders (Miyetti Allah) and butchers' associations. A 2018 report says foreign dealers arrive at night.
  - Abattoir market and Alade market.
  - Hausa scrap collectors living by the rail line.
  - Bakeries.
  - Street trading along the traffic.

#### 7. Transport
- **Rail:**
  - The **Red Line** (Agbado–Oyingbo, opened October 2024) stops at Agege, which is also the planned link to the Purple Line.
  - The **Lagos-Ibadan train** stops here too.
  - The line shares its route with the Lagos–Kano standard-gauge railway (the new wide track). The original 19th-century railway is what built the town.
- **Okada and keke:** the 2020 ban covered highways and named corridors, and Agege Motor Road had gridlock that week. The 2022 phases that I could confirm (June: 6 LGAs; September: 4 LGAs and 6 LCDAs) **did not include Agege**. I could not check whether a later phase added it, so treat Agege as okada-friendly but under pressure.
- **Danfo:** dominant *[unverified for this LGA specifically]*.

#### 8. Sound, smell, look
- **Sourced:** the abattoir has blood-tinged water in the gutters, meat butchered on a dirt pile beside wood-fire smoke, and camels and goats standing among the stalls.
- **[unverified]:** I found no sourced detail on sound or daily rhythm. Plausible elements are train horns, call to prayer, bread hawkers at dawn, and generators at night.

#### 9. People, languages, religion
- **Indigenous people:** Awori (Yoruba). The area is now mixed but mostly Yoruba-speaking.
- **Hausa:** about 60% of the township in 1912. A strong Hausa and Fulani presence remains around the rail line and abattoir, led by community heads such as the Galadima (a Hausa title).
- **Religion:** heavily Muslim, plus churches. MFM (a large Pentecostal church) owns the stadium's football club. Mosques stand at the abattoir.

#### 10. Food and nightlife
- **Agege bread:** a soft, sweet, close-crumbed loaf baked in lidded tins.
  - Origin traced to Jamaican-born baker Amos Shackleford (from 1913), then Alhaji Ayokunnu's bakery near the railway station.
  - Hawked by young women carrying loaves on their heads.
  - Eaten with ewa agoyin (mashed beans with pepper sauce), akara or butter.
  - Some bakers have been accused of using potassium bromate, which NAFDAC (Nigeria's food regulator) bans.
- **Suya and meat:** suya and fresh meat are plausible because of the abattoir *[unverified]*.
- **Nightlife:** the sourced picture is "relaxation spots" and small hotels, which also appear in cult-clash reports.

#### 11. History
- Founded by a hunter named Aige or Ayige; Awori settlers arrived during the 17th-century Otta-Egba war.
- People were displaced by the Dahomean wars in the 1840s–50s.
- The railway arrived in the late 1800s and created "Agege Station."
- Agege was capital of Ikeja Division until 1927.
- **3 June 2012:** Dana Air Flight 992 crashed into homes at Iju-Ishaga on the Agege side.
  - 153 people on board and at least 6 on the ground died (Wikipedia's Agege page says 10 on the ground).
  - Crowds fought the fire with buckets, and soldiers whipped back onlookers.

#### 12. Danger and law
- **"Awawa" cultists:**
  - A joint council and police Rapid Response Squad (RRS) task force was set up in January 2022.
  - The council pays 250 youths N10,000 a month to attend vocational centres.
- **Raids and clashes:**
  - June 2022: 14 suspected cultists arrested and drugs recovered.
  - November 2022: 11 arrested after a cult clash at a hotel.
  - Shots fired at a "relaxation spot" in Omoteye Estate (date not given).
- **Hausa–Fulani clash at the abattoir:**
  - Started after the railway corporation demolished Hausa shanties.
  - A 1–2 a.m. attack was followed by a dawn reprisal.
  - Reports say 1 to 3 dead, and 7 cattle died.
- **Government raids:** demolition threats at Oko-Oba in 2018, and in June 2025 a closure over hygiene plus a raid on an illegal abattoir opposite the RRS barracks.
- **Smaller incidents:** in January 2024 an ear was cut off in a fight at the abattoir market.

#### 13. Environment
- Flooding and muddy streets; the July 2026 floods pushed vegetable prices up.
- Abattoir waste in the gutters.
- Dust, which residents said eased after the flyover was built.
- There is no lagoon here; it is an inland mainland area.
- Power cuts *[unverified, but standard for Lagos]*.

#### 14. Festivals and sacred things
- **Festivals:** Egungun and Gelede festivals are listed. I could not search for details.
- **Traditional rulers:** the Alayige of Orile Agege, the Olu of Agege and the Ologba of Ogba.
- **FLAG: never portray these as criminal or comic:**
  - Egungun masquerades and Gelede masks.
  - The obas and their palaces.
  - Mosques (including the abattoir mosques) and churches.

#### 15. In pop culture
- **Agege bread** is the main cultural export.
- **Olajumoke Orisaguna**, a former Agege bread hawker, went viral in 2016 when she walked into a photo shoot and became a model.
- **Dana crash:** it is part of Lagos collective memory.
- **Not found:** I could not search for songs or films before the budget ran out.

#### 16. What Gidi can borrow
1. **Bread-run missions:** Tunde collects loaves from a railside bakery at dawn on his okada and delivers to bukas and hawkers before the rush. It is timed, and he must not crush the stack.
2. **Abattoir district:** a cattle-market zone with night arrivals, butchers, smoke and red gutters, where cattle, goats and the odd camel wander into traffic. It suits an R-rated "oja under the meat" smuggling route.
3. **The rail spine:** a district split by an active line where trains cut traffic and shanties crowd the right-of-way. Set pieces include outrunning a commuter train and a demolition-day riot.
4. **Flyover vs underneath:** a new flyover for cars, with the old junction and market chaos below it. That gives the player a choice between a fast route and a hustle route.
5. **Night-crawl "relaxation spot":** a small hotel and bar where a rival gang clash erupts, with shots fired into the air and the police squad arriving late.
6. **Character:** a Hausa scrap-yard boss by the tracks who trades information.

#### 17. Things to avoid
- **Real names:** "Pen Cinema", "Oko-Oba", MFM, Dana Air, Miyetti Allah, the RRS, real oba titles (Alayige, Ologba), Olajumoke, real politicians, and real cult names such as "Awawa".
- **The Dana crash:** never re-create it as gameplay.
- **Ethnic framing:** do not cast Hausa or Fulani people as the criminal class, and do not make the ethnic clash itself a mission.
- **Bread brands:** do not copy any real bakery brand.

## Ifako-Ijaiye LGA: research brief for Gidi

**How this was sourced:** The facts below come only from pages I opened. Anything marked *(unsourced)* is general Lagos knowledge or inference, and you should check it against your own memory of the area. Reporting on this LGA is thin. I found nothing usable on its festivals, its nightlife, its own markets or how it appears in pop culture.

#### 1. In one line
A dense Egba-Yoruba suburb on the Lagos–Ogun border. It grew up around a colonial waterworks, a railway and the Lagos–Abeokuta Expressway, and it is better known for gridlock, floods and a deadly pipeline fire than for any landmark.

#### 2. Areas inside it
Wikipedia lists Ijaiye, Ifako (the LGA headquarters), Oke-Ira, Iju-Ishaga, Obawole, Iju-Ogundimu, Fagba, Agege Pen Cinema and Ojokoro. Ojokoro is now its own LCDA (a smaller council carved out of the LGA) and holds Agbado, the end of the Red Line rail. **Boundary caution:** Wikipedia and ICIR place Abule-Egba itself, and Ile-Epo, in Alimosho / Agbado-Oke Odo, so those places sit on Ifako-Ijaiye's edge rather than inside it. In practice the area divides into three:
- **Ijaiye and Ifako:** the older core.
- **Iju and Fagba:** residential, built on low wetland.
- **Ojokoro and Alakuko:** the expressway and border corridor.

#### 3. Landmarks
- **Iju Waterworks.** Built 1910–15 for just over £300,000 and commissioned on 1 July 1915. It stands where the Iju and Adiyan rivers meet. The colonial government leased 151 acres from the Egba United Government for 999 years. It first served European residents of Ikoyi and reached 45 million gallons a day by 1985. Its output depends on electricity, and it got its own power plant in 2010.
- **Abule-Egba Flyover.** 1.2 km long on the Lagos–Abeokuta Expressway, opened in 2017.
- **Pen Cinema Flyover.** 1.4 km dual carriageway opened in March 2021. It crosses the railway and links Oba Ogunji Road to Old Abeokuta Road.
- **Pen Cinema.** A former cinema from when Agege was a "theatre hub", now just a place name. The six-storey Heritage Centre mall on Ifako-Ijaiye Road plans to bring a cinema back.
- **Jankara Market, Ijaiye.** Listed on Wikipedia, but no details given.
- **Red Line stations:** Iju and Agbado.
- **Ifako-Ijaiye stadium.** Promised under Governor Fashola and reportedly built (a politician's own claim).

#### 4. Buildings and streets
Nothing I opened describes the housing. Known from reporting:
- Very high density: about 633,000 people on 43 km² in 2022, roughly 15,000 per km².
- Narrow residential streets off main roads, such as Akinde Street off Balogun Road in Ishaga.
- Housing estates such as Ijaiye Housing Estate and Igbolu Estate.
- Fences and houses that collapse in floods.
- Empty plots grown into bush, where residents have killed snakes.
- Before the Pen Cinema works, traders occupied the walkways.

*(Unsourced:)* expect bungalows and one- or two-storey "face-me-I-face-you" rooming houses, uncompleted buildings and painted shop signs.

#### 5. Roads, junctions, traffic
- **Lagos–Abeokuta Expressway.** Residents call its gridlock "perennial".
- **Main junctions and bus stops:** Kola Bus Stop, Abule-Egba, Pen Cinema junction, Oke-Koto, Balogun Bus Stop on Iju-Ishaga Road, Ile-Epo.
- **Other main roads:** Iju Road, Oba Ogunji Road, Agunbiade Road.
- Ex-lawmakers say heavy vehicles wreck the local roads.
- Before the 2021 flyover, commuters were attacked at Pen Cinema even though a police station stood beside the railway.

#### 6. Economy and markets
- **Oke-Odo Market at Ile-Epo**, just outside the LGA. Founded 1983, with thousands of stalls selling yam, garri, beans, palm oil, pepper, fish, meat and grains. Ogun farmers sell there, and some trucks are routed in from Mile 12.
- Traders spill onto the expressway, and a nearby dumpsite causes smell and standing water.
- Each market has a market chief, the **Babaloja** (Ojokoro has a "Babaloja General").
- *(Unsourced:)* the rest of the economy is small trade, artisans and transport.

#### 7. Transport
- **Red Line rail.** Opened 15 October 2024. Agbado to Oyingbo is 27 km, about 50 minutes, ₦1,000 end to end. Trains start at 6 a.m. Lagos's earlier metroline was stopped after the 1983 military takeover.
- **Okada (motorbike taxi) restrictions.** Okada are restricted on major routes, with up to 3 years' jail for rider and passenger. In August 2025 the state task force seized 668 bikes in one week, with raids at Kola Bus Stop, Ishaga and Balogun Bus Stop. At Kola, hoodlums and okada riders tried to block officials and 7 people were arrested.
- *(Unsourced:)* danfo minibuses and keke tricycles are everywhere.

#### 8. Sound, smell, look
Sourced details are few: rotting refuse at the market, mosquitoes over still drains, and expressway traffic that never clears. *(Unsourced:)* Lagos staples such as conductors shouting stops, generators running through power cuts, and the call to prayer alongside church bells.

#### 9. People, languages, religion
Mostly **Egba Yoruba**. A Mrs Ugo appears among flood victims, a sign that other ethnic groups live there too. *(Unsourced:)* Yoruba and Pidgin are the street languages, and Muslims and Christians live side by side. The 2006 fire destroyed both a mosque and a church.

#### 10. Food and nightlife
No sources found. *(Unsourced:)* buka (cheap eateries), suya (grilled meat) spots and beer parlours.

#### 11. History
- **1910–15:** Iju Waterworks built on land leased from the Egba.
- **1 October 1996:** LGA created out of Agege under Abacha.
- **26 December 2006:** pipeline thieves tapped a line at Abule-Egba and it exploded at dawn. The documented toll was about 260 dead (some estimates said 500), with about 60 badly burned. Homes, a mosque and a church were destroyed.
- **2018:** a tanker carrying stolen fuel caught fire.
- **January 2020:** another pipeline fire. 5 dead and 150 displaced. 39 vehicles burned, 33 of them trucks.
- **2016–17:** Abule-Egba flyover built.
- **2021:** Pen Cinema flyover opened.
- **2024:** Red Line opened.

#### 12. Danger and law
- **Pipeline vandals and fuel thieves.** The defining real crime here.
- **Iju-Ishaga, 9 December 2023:** a police officer was murdered. Police later arrested 8 armed robbers and carjackers aged 22–32 and recovered locally made pistols and charms.
- **Pen Cinema:** commuters were attacked before the 2021 redevelopment.
- **Okada and enforcement:** standoffs with the task force at Kola Bus Stop.
- I found no reporting specific to cult gangs in this LGA.

#### 13. Environment
- **Floods:** after the Iju rail station was built, streets in Ishaga flood "with small rain". Fences collapse, families are displaced, and residents say people have been washed away over the years.
- **Ijaiye Housing Estate:** blocked drains, and refuse flowing in from the Iju road end.
- **Power:** cuts reduce the waterworks' output.

#### 14. Festivals and sacred things
No source specific to this LGA. Elsewhere in Lagos, **Orò** confines women and non-natives indoors while initiates move through the streets. **FLAG:** never show Orò, egungun masquerades, shrines, obas (traditional kings) or Babaloja as criminals.

#### 15. Pop culture
Nothing found. Patoranking's song "Abule" is about **Ebute Metta**, not Abule-Egba.

#### 16. What Gidi can borrow
1. **"Pipeline Night" set piece.** A ruptured pipeline at midnight, a crowd with jerrycans, and Tunde has to choose between loading oja and getting out before dawn ignition. Handle it carefully: this was a real tragedy.
2. **Border-run missions.** The expressway is the last stretch before the state line, so fuel and goods move from a rural "Egba-side" supply market into Gidi.
3. **Okada task-force raids** as a dynamic event at a Kola-style bus stop: chases, impounded bikes and a crowd that turns on the officials.
4. **Old colonial waterworks** as a district landmark and hideout. Cut the power and the city's taps run dry.
5. **Flood-district streets.** Low wetland roads that fill fast and lose fences, changing okada routes in the rainy season.
6. **New elevated train line** cutting across old junctions, with a dead "cinema" place name kept as a bus-stop name.

#### 17. Things to avoid
- Real names: Ifako, Ijaiye, Ojokoro, Iju, Abule-Egba, Pen Cinema, Kola, Oke-Odo.
- NNPC, LAMATA, Red Line branding and real politicians.
- Recreating the real 2006 victims or death toll.
- Orò, egungun, obas or Babaloja shown as criminal.

## Alimosho LGA: research brief for Gidi

The sourcing is uneven. Roads, environment, crime and history are backed by reporting I opened. Food, nightlife, pop culture and the look and sound of the streets had little online reporting I could open, so those parts lean on general knowledge and are marked **[unsourced]**.

#### 1. In one line
Alimosho is Lagos's biggest, loudest commuter suburb. Its population is projected at about 4.08 million (2019), far above the 1.29 million counted in 2006. Wikipedia calls it "the noisiest LGA in Lagos State". It is a sprawl of old Egba and Awori villages joined together by markets, roundabouts and danfo routes.

#### 2. Areas inside it
- **Six LCDAs:** Agbado/Oke-Odo, Ayobo/Ipaja, Alimosho, Egbe/Idimu, Ikotun/Igando and Mosan-Okunola.
- **Older towns** with their own obas: Igando, Isheri-Olofin, Egbeda, Akesan, Ipaja, Orisunbare, Shasha, Ijegun and Ikotun.
- **Akowonjo/Egbeda:** one of the oldest settlements. Egba people fleeing the inter-tribal wars cleared the forest there around 1830.
- **Ikotun/Igando:** the market and roundabout heart of the south.
- **Iyana-Ipaja/Abule-Egba:** the edge along the Lagos-Abeokuta Expressway, built around transit.
- **Ayobo, Ipaja and Cardoso ("Kaka"), Baruwa, Ashipa, Bada, Megida:** outer sprawl. People there complain of long detours through Egbeda, Idimu and Isheri just to reach Igando or LASU.
- **Gowon Estate:** a planned Federal Housing Authority estate. Residents allege its master plan has been eaten away by stalls, car washes, mechanic sheds and block-moulding yards.

#### 3. Landmarks and monuments
- **Ikotun roundabout:** four exits, to Ijegun (west), Idimu and Iyana-Ipaja (east), Isolo (south) and Igando (north).
- **Ikotun (Irepodun) Market:** about 8,400 lock-up shops and more than 10,000 traders.
- **Synagogue Church of All Nations, Ikotun-Egbe:** a guesthouse building collapsed there on 12 September 2014, killing 116 people.
- **Jubilee Bridge (Abule-Egba flyover):** built in 13 months, with fountain and street lights. It became a place people go at night.
- **Other markets:** Katangua/Katangowa second-hand clothes market at Abule-Egba, Ile-Epo, Akesan and Igando.
- **Igando:** Alimosho General Hospital on Igando Road, and the Solous 3 landfill, which covers 12 hectares.

#### 4. Buildings and streets
- **Gowon Estate:** a planned grid that informal businesses have taken over.
- **Ajasa-Command road:** residents nicknamed it "Ajasa-London" once it got street lights.
- **Solous edge:** homes on Ogunmeru Street sit about 10 ft from the landfill, across an untarred road.

- **Romance scammers:** Wikipedia cites a 2026 book, *Yahoo Boys*, about scammers based in Ikotun. Treat this as uncertain.
- **[unsourced]** Okada and keke riding, artisans, and long commutes to jobs in Ikeja and on the Island.

## Kosofe LGA: research brief for Gidi

The clashes you listed as 2017 actually happened in **March 2016**. Every source I opened dates them then. Sections 8, 10 and 15 have little sourced material because the shared web-search limit ran out partway through. Lines marked *(unsourced)* are general knowledge for you to check against your own memory of Lagos.

#### 1. In one line
Kosofe is the main road into mainland Lagos from the north. Ikorodu Road carries northern food trucks to Mile 12 market, buses to the Ojota terminal and protesters to Ojota's freedom park. Gated estates sit beside it, and Awori fishing villages lie behind it on the creeks.

#### 2. Areas inside it
- **Mile 12 / Agiliti / Gengere:** the dense market district. Its named streets include Oniyanri, Maidan, Agiliti 1 and Agiliti 2, and the Agiliti link bridge.
- **Ketu:** a market-and-transit strip with its own fruit market.
- **Ojota:** an interchange with a motor park and Gani Fawehinmi Freedom Park.
- **Magodo:** a high-end gated estate.
- **Ogudu:** has its own traditional ruler, the Ologudu.
- **Isheri North:** a planned residential scheme on low ground that floods.
- **Oworonshoki:** an old waterfront settlement by the Third Mainland Bridge, heavily demolished in 2025.
- **Agboyi-Ketu:** riverine Awori villages you can only reach by boat.
- **Alapere:** the nearest dry-land shops for Agboyi residents.
- Wikipedia also lists Ifako/Soluyi, Ajao Estate, Maryland, Shangisha and Odo-Ogun.

#### 3. Landmarks
- Mile 12 Market
- Ketu fruits market
- Owode-Onirin spare-parts market on Ikorodu Road
- Gani Fawehinmi Freedom Park, Ojota
- "Peace Park" in Ketu, the other court-designated protest ground
- Ojota motor park
- Kosofe Magistrate Court, Ojota
- The Agiliti link bridge
- The Third Mainland Bridge approach at Oworonshoki
- The Magodo estate gates

#### 4. Buildings and streets
- **Oworonshoki:** multi-generation family compounds built up slowly over years. After the demolitions, people live in shelters of salvaged zinc, tarpaulin and wood among broken concrete foundations.
- **Agboyi:** houses at the water's edge, wooden platforms and rickety boats.
- **Magodo:** a walled estate whose gates residents lock. In 2021 more than 300 houses there were spray-painted with court-order markings.
- **Mile 12:** a stretch of service lane separated by a concrete-and-grating fence, Jersey barriers and bus lay-bys. Roads in the market are poor and turn to mud after rain; traders wear rain boots.

#### 5. Roads and traffic
- **Ikorodu Road** is the spine of the district, with a service lane and a main carriageway.
- The **Mile 12 to Ketu stretch** is known for constant gridlock. LAMATA, the state transport authority, built a 1 km fence there to separate the lanes.
- In **2017 the state banned interstate buses from Ikorodu Road** and pushed them into the Ojota and Ojodu Berger parks. Officials cited damaged barriers, an accident and crime at night.
- The Lagos–Ibadan Expressway and the Third Mainland Bridge both run through the LGA.

#### 6. Economy
- **Mile 12** has run for about 40 years. It took over from Iddo as the end point for food coming from the north.
  - Lorries unload pepper, tomatoes, onions, yams, plantain and cabbage several times a day.
  - Labourers carry produce straight from the trucks to buyers.
  - It sells wholesale to caterers and market women, who resell it.
- **Owode-Onirin** sells car spare parts.
- **Agboyi** lives mainly by fishing, with some traditional medicine. Its young people leave to work in Apapa and Badagry.
- **Oworonshoki** traded in food, hair salons and tailoring.
- Who controls a market's revenue collection is worth fighting over (see section 12).

#### 7. Transport
- **BRT:** the Mile 12–Ikorodu route opened with 434 buses.
- **Danfo:** yellow commercial buses kept running even through the 2024 protests.
- **Okada:** banned in Kosofe from 1 September 2022, in the ban's second phase. The state crushed seized bikes in public, about 2,000 in one batch at Ikeja. Banned riders were offered vocational centres or the First and Last Mile bus scheme.
- **Boats:** wooden boats are the only way into Agboyi. Residents say the creeks are clogged with mud and weeds, and a promised dredging programme has barely started.

#### 8. Sound, smell, look
- **Sourced:** the rumble of bulldozers at midnight, women cooking over firewood among ruins, smoke from small fires, fish waste and crabs at Agboyi, and salty water that makes your toes itch.
- *(Unsourced)* Mile 12 at dawn: the smell of rotting tomatoes, trucks reversing, porters shouting, bus conductors calling out stops.

#### 9. People, languages, religion
- About 1.01 million people in 2022, at roughly 12,000 per km².
- Large Hausa trading communities live alongside Yoruba ones; traders come from all six regions of Nigeria. Agboyi's people are Awori.
- Both churches and mosques are present. Churches were reportedly burned in the 2016 riot.
- Languages *(unsourced but safe to assume)*: Yoruba, Hausa, Pidgin and Igbo.

#### 10. Food and nightlife
I found nothing sourced. *(Unsourced)* Expect suya and Hausa food spots around Mile 12, and buka canteens and beer parlours along Ikorodu Road.

#### 11. History
- **1996:** the LGA was created.
- **March 2016:** the Mile 12 clash.
  - **Trigger:** a Hausa okada rider knocked down a Yoruba pedestrian, or two riders fought; accounts differ.
  - **Toll:** 4 to more than 10 dead, with knives used. A school, houses and vehicles were burned.
  - **Response:** more than 50 arrests, soldiers on Ikorodu Road, and the market closed for a week. One estimate put the cost at about ₦1.7bn.
- **2017:** interstate buses were banned from Ikorodu Road.
- **2021:** the Magodo siege.
- **2022:** the okada ban.
- **2024:** #EndBadGovernance protests were confined by court order to Ojota and Ketu.
- **2023–2025:** the Oworonshoki demolitions.

#### 12. Danger and law
- **Ketu, March 2021:** a fight between cult groups over revenue collection at the fruits market. Police arrested 15 and seized locally made pistols and "assorted charms."
- **Mile 12 (Gengere), May 2021:** a fight between gangs of thugs.
- **Agboyi:** residents report burglaries and fights between youth gangs, and want a police post.
- **Magodo, December 2021:** police arrived with apparent thugs to mark houses for possession. This comes from the long Shangisha landlords' land case, which dates to 1982.
- **Oworonshoki, 2025:** demolitions at night with about five minutes' warning. Residents allege looting by thugs who came with security personnel.
- **Ojota, 2024 protests:** "area boys" who support the ruling party tried to break up the protest.

#### 13. Environment
- **Isheri North** has flooded every year since about 2018; 2019 was the worst. Residents blame water released from the Oyan Dam and fishponds blocking the flow. The river basin authority blames rain and people building on the floodplain.
- **Agboyi** floods in the wet season and has unsafe water and cholera.
- **Mile 12** turns to mud after rain.

#### 14. Festivals and sacred things
- **Ogun festival.**
- **Igunnuko:** a very tall masquerade on stilts, brought to Lagos from Pategi (Nupe) in 1814. It involves the *Kuso* tree-uprooting rite.
- **Traditional rulers:** the Ologudu of Ogudu, the Oba of Oworonshoki and the Oba and Baales of Agboyi.
- **FLAG:** never show masquerades, Ogun worship, shrines or traditional rulers as criminal, or as the reason for crime. The "charms" seized from cultists should be fictional items, not real Ifa, Ogun or Egungun objects.

#### 15. In pop culture
The only sourced item is that comedian AY posted footage of the 2021 Mile 12 fight. I found no sourced songs, films or books.

#### 16. What Gidi can borrow
1. **"Mile Market" dawn rush:** Tunde's okada meets trucks from the north, and he moves oja between unloading lorries and the buyers waiting for them, racing against produce that spoils.
2. **Riot set piece:** a small road accident turns into an ethnic riot. The player must get a passenger out through army checkpoints with hands raised. Write it with no "good side".
3. **Okada-ban heat:** task-force raids, seized bikes and a crushing yard where Tunde has to steal his bike back.
4. **Creek route:** a district you can only reach by canoe, used for smuggling, with its own Awori-style elders.
5. **Night demolition mission:** bulldozers at midnight and hired thugs, with a choice to protect or exploit the victims.
6. **Gated-estate siege:** police and thugs lock down a rich estate over a land case.

#### 17. Things to avoid
Don't use these real names in the game: Mile 12, Ketu, Ojota, Magodo, Oworonshoki, Agboyi, Gani Fawehinmi or Freedom Park, LAMATA, BRT livery, the Shangisha Landlords Association, real governors, police commissioners or monarchs (Ologudu and others), or the Igunnuko name and costume. Don't tie any ethnic group to the role of aggressor.

## Amuwo-Odofin LGA: research brief for Gidi

#### 1. In one line
Amuwo-Odofin is where Lagos's planned-estate dream went wrong. It holds Festac Town, a 1977 government showpiece that turned into a crowded scam suburb, plus the Mile 2 truck gridlock, a giant wholesale market at Trade Fair, and creekside prison country at Kirikiri, all strung along the broken Lagos-Badagry Expressway.

#### 2. Areas inside it
- **Festac Town**: the headquarters. It is a grid of 1st to 7th Avenues with numbered "Roads" (403 Road, 41 Road, 23 Road) and closes named by letter.
  - A few enclaves are still orderly: 400, 403 and 40/41 Road have lawns, working streetlights and guarded gates.
  - 1st and 5th Avenue have degraded into slum-like strips.
- **Satellite Town**: an older estate (around 1960) south of the expressway. It has creekside hotels on Badagry Creek and has partly become a slum.
- **Amuwo**: a mixed informal industrial and residential district.
- **Kirikiri**: the eastern end, with port and fuel storage plus the prison complex.
- **Oluti**: a planned area on Old Ojo Road near Festac's First Gate.
- **Abule-Ado, Alakija, Agboju, Abule-Osun, Ijegun**: dense expressway-side and pipeline-side neighbourhoods.
- **Ibeshe and Ilado**: semi-rural waterside wards. Of the LGA's 67 communities, 47 are rural.

#### 3. Landmarks and monuments
- **Lagos International Trade Fair Complex**: 350 hectares, designed by the Yugoslav architect Zoran Bojović, opened in 1977. Its markets are ASPAMDA (auto parts and machinery), BBA (Balogun Business Association: textiles and household goods) and a jewellery market.
- **Festival Mall** (with Shoprite) and **Ahonsi (23rd) Market**, Festac's largest market.
- **Festac's First, Second and Third Gates** and the **Festac Link Bridge**.
- **Kirikiri Maximum Security Prison** (1955), which includes Nigeria's only dedicated female prison.
- **Abule-Ado Mechanic Village** and **Bethlehem Girls College**, both hit by the 2020 blast.
- **Fire Junction, Ijegun**, a pipeline site.
- **Nigerian Navy Reference Hospital**.
- **Mile 2 rail station**, currently the last stop of the Blue Line.

#### 4. Buildings and streets
- Festac was built as about 5,000 identical government flats and duplexes for 45,000 people. It now holds over 300,000 (Catholic Herald estimate).
- Almost every house has been converted into something else: shops, salons, pharmacies, clinics, hotels and nightclubs. Gardens have become shops and drinking shacks.
- Some 5th Avenue blocks "looked like multi-storey IDP camps."
- The original design gave wide avenues, wide gutters, tree-lined pavements and blocks of flats. Today the roads are cratered, there is no central sewer, open gutters block up, and some avenues have no streetlights.
- Satellite Town has illegal structures and septic tanks.
- Trade Fair has half-built plazas. Lagos State bulldozed some of them in September 2025.

#### 5. Roads, junctions, bridges, traffic
- **Lagos-Badagry Expressway**: the main artery. A 10-lane-plus-BRT-plus-rail upgrade was awarded in 2009 and was still stalled in 2018.
  - Bottlenecks: Mile 2, Alakija, Abule-Ado, the Trade Fair under-bridge, Volkswagen, Iyana-Iba.
  - The stretch from Abule-Osun to the Trade Fair under-bridge "has failed completely."
  - Delays of 4 to 6 hours were reported.
- **Mile 2 to Apapa / Tin Can**: tankers queue for days to reach the tank farms, often down to a single usable lane. Cele bus stop is named in reporting.
- **Mile 2 bus stop** floods when the Signal Barracks Collector and Festac Channel drains clog with refuse.

#### 6. Economy and markets
- **Wholesale at Trade Fair**: auto parts, machinery and jewellery for buyers across West Africa. Counterfeit designer goods are common in local markets.
- **Port and tank-farm logistics**: haulage, tanker driving and mechanic work.
- **Informal home businesses** throughout Festac. A resident: "Everybody is just doing things on their own."
- **Cybercrime**: in 2005 the LA Times called Festac "at the center of the cyber-scam universe." Scammers worked out of internet cafés, sold foreign phone lines and sold email-harvesting software.
- **Fuel theft**: pipeline tapping and fuel scooping.

#### 7. Transport
- **Danfo** minibuses, LAMATA-regulated buses, and long-distance GIGM coaches from 1st Avenue.
- **Okada and keke** are still allowed here. The 2022 okada bans covered other LGAs but not Amuwo-Odofin or Oriade, so Tunde's trade is legal in this kind of district.
- **Blue Line rail**: Marina to Mile 2 opened on 4 Sept 2023. Planned stations at Festac, Alakija and Trade Fair are still under construction.
- **Wooden boats** run from jetties, and robbers have used the waterways as escape routes.
- **Trucks and tankers** dominate the road.

#### 8. Sound, smell, look
This section is my inference from the sources, with little direct description.
- Look: tanker queues, cratered roads, avenues gone dark when the power is out.
- Smell: open gutters ("swimming in sewage") and refuse burning. Lagos sealed the Trade Fair markets in 2023 over burnt waste.
- Morning: an expressway crawl.
- Night: bars, hotels and brothels along 1st Avenue.

#### 9. People, languages, religion
- The indigenous people are Awori Yoruba (riverine wards like Ibeshe, Ilado and Kirikiri). This is general knowledge; I did not open a page confirming it.
- Festac has a large Igbo trading population. Ethnic tension shows up in local politics and in suspicious questions about who is Igbo.
- Trade Fair traders quoted in reporting are mostly Igbo.
- Catholic presence is visible (Bethlehem Girls College). The area is mixed Christian and Muslim; one resident quoted is an Alhaja.
- Languages: Pidgin, Yoruba, Igbo.

#### 10. Food and nightlife
- Mama-named canteens and bukas (Mama Ada, Mama Do Good), shawarma on 41 Road, De Tastee chicken.
- Bars: Chillers, Adanma Bar, La-Vida Lounge.
- Clubs and sports bars: Ibiza Outdoor Bar & Night Club, Full House, sports bars in Festival Mall.
- Festac has a reputation for hotels and hangout spots.

#### 11. History
- About 1960: Satellite Town estate is founded.
- 1955: Kirikiri prison opens.
- 1977: FESTAC '77 (the Second World Black and African Festival of Arts and Culture). Festac Town was built to house participants and then allocated by ballot, with an early ban on renting or selling. The Trade Fair Complex also opened that year.
- 2002: Balogun traders relocate to Trade Fair.
- 2000s: the "419" reputation forms.
- 2019: Ijegun pipeline fire.
- 15 March 2020: the Abule-Ado explosion.
- 2025: Trade Fair demolitions.
- Federal, state and local government all claim control of Festac and levy overlapping charges.

#### 12. Danger and law
- **Bank robberies**: one at 23 Road; the robbers fled by waterway. During the chaos, soldiers on parade shot dead two men whom residents had shouted "thief" at, and police said they had been mistaken for robbers.
- **Land-grabber shootouts** (July 2016) around 4th and 5th Avenue. Residents mistook the gunfire for robberies at Diamond Bank and FCMB, and police sent the Rapid Response Squad with air and land units to the canal.
- **Ijegun, 4 July 2019**: vandals tapping a pipeline set fire to spilled fuel to escape arrest. Two or three people died and 31 cars burned.
- **Abule-Ado, 15 March 2020, around 9am**: 20 dead, over 100 buildings destroyed, about 10,000 displaced. Investigators blamed a granite-laden tipper parked on an NNPC pipeline; encroachment on the pipeline route was also cited. The headmistress, Rev. Sr. Henrietta Alokha, died getting her students out.
- **Kirikiri prison**: 69% of inmates were awaiting trial in 2018. There is a "VIP section" with cooks and a generator, and prisoners cook their own food on kerosene stoves.
- I did not find specific cult reporting for this LGA.

#### 13. Environment
- Badagry Creek and canals border the area.
- Seasonal flooding, blocked collector drains, no central sewer.
- Buried petroleum pipelines run under housing. Tank farms add fume and fire risk.

#### 14. Festivals and sacred things
- **FLAG**: Awori obas, chieftaincy rites, masquerades and shrines in Ibeshe, Ilado and Kirikiri exist, but I could not open a source that details them. Treat any traditional ruler, shrine or masquerade as sacred and never as criminal.
- **FLAG**: the Abule-Ado victims and Catholic institutions are real recent tragedy. Do not exploit them.

#### 15. In pop culture
- A 2014 ROK Studios TV series set in Festac shows dusty, run-down flats and sex work. The source did not name the series.
- *'77: The Festac Conspiracy* by Izu Ojukwu (2025, with Ramsey Nouah and Rita Dominic) is set around FESTAC '77. The Deadline summary said 1976, which looks like an error.
- *Law & Order: SVU* references Kirikiri prison.

#### 16. What Gidi can borrow
1. **A "Fest Town" district.** A decaying grid of numbered avenues. Each house hides a shop, bar or scam den. Gated enclaves (the "400 Roads" type) sit beside dark slum avenues. Fixers wait in internet cafés.
2. **A tanker-queue mission.** A 3-day jam to the tank farms. Weave an okada through one usable lane to deliver oja to a stuck driver. A fuel-scooping side hustle can go up in flames.
3. **A pipeline-blast set piece.** A disaster level inspired by Abule-Ado: rescue civilians from rubble at a mechanic village. Make it fictional, with no real school or victims.
4. **A wholesale mega-market.** A spare-parts bazaar where bulldozers arrive at 8am and players race to save stock.
5. **A creek getaway.** Robbers fleeing by canal boat, and a prison island with a "VIP wing."
6. **Characters.** A land-grabber gang boss; an Igbo spare-parts magnate; a drainage task-force crew.

#### 17. Things to avoid
- Real names: Festac, ASPAMDA, BBA, Kirikiri, Abule-Ado, Bethlehem Girls College, NNPC, Diamond Bank, FCMB, Shoprite, Festival Mall, GIGM, ROK.
- Real people: Sr. Alokha, officials named in reporting.
- Ethnic profiling: don't make Igbo traders into scammers.
- Real bar and restaurant names.

## Ojo LGA: research brief for Gidi

#### 1. In one line
Ojo is Lagos's far-west trading corridor. It is a dense, mostly residential sprawl split down the middle by the Lagos–Badagry Expressway, and it holds West Africa's biggest electronics market, a barracks, a university and mangrove creeks.

#### 2. Areas inside it
- **North of Badagry Creek:** mostly residential, cut in half by the expressway.
- **South of the creek:** few people, with mangrove swamps and sandy beaches. Some of the beaches fill up as holiday spots in December.
- **Iba, Oto-Awori and Igbo-Elerin:** old Awori settlements. Iba and Oto-Awori are now separate council areas (LCDAs).
- **Ijanikin (Oto-Awori LCDA):** a quieter town full of schools and colleges: Federal Government College Lagos, Lagos State University of Education (formerly Adeniran Ogunsanya College of Education), the National Postgraduate Medical College, Eko University of Medical and Health Sciences, and the Foreign Service Academy.
- **Okokomaiko and Iyana-Iba:** transit nodes on the expressway. **Alaba and Alaba Rago** are the market zones.

#### 3. Landmarks and monuments
- Alaba International Market
- Alaba Rago (a livestock market that grew into a general market)
- Iyana-Iba Market
- The former Lagos International Trade Fair Complex
- LASU main campus
- Ojo Military Cantonment: 81 Division HQ per Wikipedia, and the 149 Battalion per Security Force Monitor
- Navy Town
- The Trade Fair under-bridge
- The Iyana-Iba interchange bridge
- Ferry jetties at Muwo, Shibiri and Olojo Drive
- An NRC railway station

#### 4. Buildings and streets
- **Alaba lock-ups:** single-storey concrete-block shops of 10×10 or 10×20 ft. Newer ones stack a warehouse over an office.
- **Street furniture in Alaba:** generators sit on tyres or in welded rebar cages in the street. Private radio masts rise above rooftop satellite dishes.
- **Alaba roads:** no pavements or kerbs, so people, handcarts and trucks share one surface.
- **Alaba's own justice system:** the "prison" is a shipping container, and the market court is a shop across from it.
- **Alaba Rago:** run-down stalls sinking into marshy ground, with buildings bolted on at random, shallow clogged drains and some abandoned shops.
- **Housing:** the market has none. Most housing nearby is residential blocks. I have no source on house types, so face-me-I-face-you and uncompleted buildings are likely but unverified.

#### 5. Roads, junctions, bridges, traffic
- **The expressway:** the Lagos–Badagry Expressway (part of the Trans-West African Coastal Highway) runs east–west through Ojo.
- **Choke points (BusinessDay):** Trade Fair under-bridge, Ojo Barracks, Volkswagen, Iyana-Iba, and Abule-Oshun, where the road "failed completely".
- **Diversions and trucks:** drivers escaping the expressway pour into Old Ojo Road, which is already full of heavy trucks.
- **Effect on trade:** Alaba traders reported customers stuck in traffic for four to six hours.
- **Iyana-Iba works:** an 18-week diversion for bridge repair began on 19 January 2026, sending traffic onto the service lane.
- **Other roads:** Olojo Drive, Old Ojo Road, Kemberi Road and Alaba Road in the south; Chief Esan Way, Iyana-Ipaja Road and Igbo-Elerin Road in the north.

#### 6. Economy and markets
**Alaba International Market (UrbanNext):**
- Started as speaker sellers in the 1970s. FESTAC 1977 pushed them out to Mile 2 and then to Ojo, and the Badagry council adopted the market in 1979 with about 2,800 traders.
- Claims about 50,000 merchants and over $2bn a year in trade. The association says it supports about 1 million jobs, directly or indirectly. These are self-reported figures.
- About half the stock is secondhand ("tokunbo"), arriving in 30–50 containers a day. Workers strip dead units for parts and build hybrid TVs, radios and generators from them.
- The market runs its own lending institutions (21 of them), a task force, a tax committee and an electricity committee.

**Alaba Rago (Guardian, 2022):**
- Grain, yams, scrap metal and car parts.
- Contraband imported rice sold at the entrance.
- Open drug sales in its Kango section.

#### 7. Transport
- Roads dominate. Danfo minibuses and trucks rule the expressway.
- **Okada:** Ojo was **not** in the 2022 okada (motorbike taxi) ban phases, which covered Ikeja, Surulere, Mushin and others, so okadas still operate legally there. Check whether this has changed since 2022.
- **Rail:** Blue Line Phase 2 runs from Mile 2 to Okokomaiko along the expressway median, with a train depot at Okokomaiko. Completion is expected in 2027.
- **Water:** ferries and speedboats cross the creek.

#### 8. Sound, smell, look
- **From sources:** Alaba trades 8am–6pm, shuts on Sundays and holds court on Fridays. It is almost entirely men. Alaba Rago's drains spill rotting food, faeces and plastic, mixed with slaughter-yard waste.
- **Unverified:** speaker-testing music blaring and generator drone are likely, but no source I opened describes them.

#### 9. People, languages, religion
- **Original people:** the Awori (Yoruba).
- **Alaba:** strongly Igbo, with traders who fly between Lagos and Dubai.
- **Alaba Rago:** Hausa and Fulani, led by figures titled Seriki Hausa and Seriki Fulani. About 40 mosques were reportedly lost when it was demolished.
- **Population:** about 609,000 in 2006 and about 839,000 estimated in 2016. A very young population.

#### 10. Food and nightlife
Not covered by my sources. Alaba Rago reportedly has informal film-viewing centres and open drinking in Kango. Anything else, such as suya, buka joints or LASU-student bars, would be my guess.

#### 11. History
- **16th century:** the hunter Esugbemi founds "Ilufe", according to oral tradition.
- **Later:** Awori migrants found Iba, Oto-Awori and Igbo-Elerin. A dispute over the oracle splits Oto-Awori from Otto-Iddo.
- **1979:** Alaba market is adopted by the Badagry council.
- **1980s:** a major fire hits the market.
- **May 1989:** Ojo LGA is created out of Badagry.
- **2009:** a 10-lane rebuild of the expressway is awarded and then stalls.

#### 12. Danger and law
- **Alaba, 19 October 2022:** traders fought "hoodlums" armed with machetes. The cause is officially unknown, though some outlets blamed extortion.
- **Alaba, June 2023:** the state building control agency marked 17 market buildings "beyond redemption" for demolition.
- **Alaba Rago, December 2021:** the drug agency (NDLEA) seized about 8.4 million tramadol doses and 56,800 bottles of codeine.
- **Alaba Rago, 2022:** police recovered guns and arms caches. The state issued a 14-day quit notice, then withdrew it.
- **Alaba Rago, January 2023:** police from Okokomaiko division tried to arrest a suspect, traders resisted, and one attacker was killed. Police said the officer fired while retreating.
- **Alaba Rago, demolitions:** in May 2024 and 17–20 August 2025, more than 3,000 shops were destroyed. Traders say there was no notice.
- **Piracy:** Alaba's film pirates got tip-offs before raids and bribed their way out of arrests.
- **LASU:** cultism warnings recur. This is unverified in detail.

#### 13. Environment
- Badagry Creek, mangroves and a marshy coastline.
- A state flash-flood alert named Ojo in August 2023.
- The expressway road surface has failed in stretches.
- Alaba relies on generators because the power supply is poor.

#### 14. Festivals and sacred things
- **The ruler:** the Olojo of Ojo is the traditional ruler, and the Olojo festival is when he wears his crown. The Onijanikin rules Ijanikin.
- **Oro:** held when an Olojo or a baale (village chief) dies.
- **Other festivals:** Egungun, Gelede, Sango, Ogun, Osun, Iyemoja and Obaluwaye.
- **FLAG:** the game must never portray Oro, Egungun, Gelede, the Olojo's crown, oracles or orisa shrines as criminal fronts or cult gangs. Real campus cults (confraternities) are a separate thing and must not be blurred with traditional religion. Mosques at Alaba Rago are also sensitive.

#### 15. In pop culture
Alaba was the hub for distributing Nollywood films, both legitimately and as pirated copies (The Conversation). No specific songs or films were verified.

#### 16. What Gidi can borrow
1. **The "Alaba" district:** a self-governing electronics bazaar with its own court, container jail, task force and lenders. The player answers to the market association, not the police.
2. **"Green face" mission chain:** Tunde runs bootleg discs on release day. A tip-off system warns of raids, and he can bribe or flee.
3. **Tokunbo chop-shop:** a minigame where you strip dead goods and assemble hybrid generators and TVs to sell.
4. **"Rago" zone:** a sinking livestock-and-scrap market with a back-alley drug trade. A demolition-day set piece has bulldozers, armed police and traders scrambling to save their goods.
5. **Broken expressway:** a dug-up 10-lane highway with an unfinished rail median, diversions onto truck-choked old roads, and the okada as the only fast way through.
6. **Creek-side jetties:** speedboat smuggling across the lagoon.

#### 17. Things to avoid
- Real names: Alaba, Okokomaiko, LASU, Ojo Barracks and the 81 Division.
- The titles Olojo and Onijanikin, and the names of real market chiefs (Magnus Ubochi) and police spokespeople.
- Sacred Awori festivals shown as crime.
- Framing whole ethnic groups (Igbo or Hausa traders) as criminals. Keep crime at the level of individuals.

## Badagry LGA, Lagos State: research notes for Gidi

#### 1. In one line
Badagry is Lagos's far-west frontier: an old Ogu (Egun) slave port and mission town of coconut beaches, lagoon creeks and heritage museums, sitting on a long, checkpoint-heavy highway to the Benin border at Seme. Smuggling is everyday economics here.

#### 2. Areas inside it
- **Badagry town** has eight traditional wards: Jegba (the Akran's ruling house), Asago, Ganho, Posuko, Boeko, Ahoviko, Ahwanjigo and Wharakoh. Each has its own traditional head.
- **Marina Road** is the heritage strip, with the museums and the First Storey Building.
- **Gberefu Island** sits across the water. It has the slave route, the "Point of No Return" and the open Atlantic beach.
- **Ajido** is a quiet kingdom on the Badagry estuary, home of the Zangbeto festival. **Dale-Whedakoh** is a separate kingdom.
- **Badagry West LCDA** covers Asakpo (Coconut Beach), Ganyingbo, Gbaji-Yeketome and Irosu. These are border-side fishing and coconut villages.
- **Seme** is the border town, 23 km west of Badagry.
- Other named spots: Onireke, Isashi, Agbovipe, Oko-Afo, Mowo, Ajara-Vetho, Topo Island.

#### 3. Landmarks and monuments
- **Seriki Faremi Williams Abass slave museum (barracoon):** built in the 1840s, about 40 rooms around a courtyard with a well. Each room is roughly 3×3 m with one small high window and is said to have held up to 40 captives. Some rooms are now lived in by families.
- **Mobee Royal Family Slave Relics Museum:** a single room on Marina Road. It shows chains and mouth locks, and has bas-reliefs on the outer walls of captives being sold.
- **First Storey Building (1845):** a mission house now owned by the Anglican Church.
- **Agia Tree Monument (1992):** near the Town Hall, with busts of the missionaries Freeman and Townsend. The original tree, about 49 m tall, fell in a storm in 1959. A new one was planted in 2016.
- **Vlekete slave market and museum** on Vlekete Road. The "built 1502" date comes from one article and is doubtful.
- **Statues:** a brass Sato drummer at Roundabout bus stop, and a 12-ft fibreglass fisherman at the Soglo Way roundabout.
- **Point of No Return:** two leaning concrete posts, a thatched "spirit attenuation" well, an unfinished slave tunnel and an abandoned larger memorial.
- **Others:** Badagry Heritage Museum (an 1863 colonial building), Freeman's Methodist Church, the Thangbe python temple, and Gbaji Bridge (described as the only bridge linking Nigeria to Benin).

#### 4. Buildings and streets
- The heritage core has colonial two-storey buildings with long shaded balconies, low compound houses around courtyards, and family homes built into old barracoon rooms.
- On Gberefu, the slave route is a 1 km path of deep sand through scrub grass, with only the last 100–200 m paved in brick. Behind the palms are holiday homes left unfinished for years.
- Out of town you get palm-lined beach resorts, fishing villages and roadside filling stations.
- I did not find sources on density or on Brazilian-style houses. Treat any claims about those as unverified.

#### 5. Roads, junctions, bridges, traffic
- **Lagos–Badagry Expressway** is 61 km to Mile 2, and gridlock on it is long-standing. Container trucks heading to Apapa make it worse. The Okokomaiko, Agbara and Badagry areas all depend on this road.
- Flooding stalls it too. On 16 September 2023 it was flooded at Abule-Ado, around the Barracks in Ojo and at the trade-fair stretch, and commuters got out and walked.
- **Badagry–Seme Expressway** runs to the border.
- **Checkpoints:** Customs says only two are official, at Gbagi (Gbaji) and Agbara. Its Seme comptroller says "far too many" others are run by various agencies.
- A travel writer timed the drive from central Lagos at 2–2.7 hours. A 2016–17 article said the airport road was "riddled with potholes", so it may have changed.
- Named stops: Roundabout, Oko-Afo, Mile 2, Okokomaiko, Coconut junction.

#### 6. Economy and markets
- The traditional trades are fishing, salt-making, coconuts (reportedly about 2 million trees) and trading.
- Heritage tourism is a business of its own: boat charters to Gberefu for $1–3, horse rides for about ₦500, guides, and crafts like coconut cups.
- **Smuggling is the big informal economy.** Rice and fuel move along more than 50 bush paths between Seme and Gbaji Bridge, according to a clearing agent. Motorcycles carry the rice.
- Clearing agents and freight forwarders work around Seme. The Seme Customs command reported ₦1.5 billion in revenue for one month in 2025.

#### 7. Transport
- **Okada:** I found no evidence Badagry is under Lagos's okada ban. The ban list published in October 2022 covered 10 LGAs, and Badagry was not one of them. Okadas are the normal way to get around town and are used for rice runs.
- **Buses:** danfos and commercial buses run to Mile 2.
- **Rail:** the Blue Line's first phase runs Marina–Mile 2, and it is planned to reach Okokomaiko. It does not reach Badagry.
- **Ferries:** a 26-seat ferry service ran from Badagry Waterside to CMS (about 1 hour, ₦1,500 in 2012) and to Mile 2 (₦1,000). Police and Customs marine units rode along as a guard against "sea pirates".
- Small motorboats with outboard engines run the creek crossings.

#### 8. Sound, smell, look
- Sato drums and Ogu songs. Coconut palms in rows. Open sea, with white foam on modest waves.
- Beaches littered with plastic bottles and seaweed. Heat and deep sand.
- Unreliable boat motors and crumbling concrete docks.
- At night the Zangbeto, the "people of the night", patrol the streets.
- Smuggler chases happen in the evening: the Oko-Afo shooting was around 7:30–8 pm.

#### 9. People, languages, religion
- Ogu/Egun people, who are culturally close to those in Benin and Togo, plus Awori Yoruba. Languages are Egun (Gun) and Yoruba.
- The 2006 population was about 241,000.
- Islam and Christianity dominate today, but traditional religion is strong: Vodún, with shrines at Nabruku, Vlekete and Mathe.
- The town calls itself the "cradle" of Christianity and Western education in Nigeria.

#### 10. Food and nightlife
- My sources were thin here. What they confirm: fresh fish, coconuts, and beach resorts at Coconut Beach in Asakpo. Weekend beach outings are implied by the resorts, not confirmed directly.
- I found no reporting on clubs. **Mark as unverified.**

#### 11. History
- 1736: a European trader is granted land, and Badagry becomes a slave port serving Oyo.
- 1784: the town is sacked by Ouidah, Oyo and Lagos.
- 1842: first Christian sermon under the Agia tree, and the first Christmas celebrated in Nigeria.
- 1845: the CMS mission builds the First Storey Building.
- 1863: Badagry's chiefs sign a treaty ceding it to Lagos.
- The slave trade continued locally until about 1886. Seriki Abass, himself a slave merchant, was made Seriki of Badagry in 1895.
- One source says 3.5 million people were shipped from here. That figure is doubtful and should not be used.

#### 12. Danger and law
- **Cults:** Eiye and Aiye fight reprisal wars. In Ganho in May 2022 a 26-year-old was shot dead. In Onireke and Isashi in October 2025, police recovered pump-action guns, a locally made pistol and 52 cartridges after a clash; one person died.
- **Border violence:** in Irosu in 2021, an 18-year-old student was killed by a stray bullet in a clash between the joint border patrol and rice smugglers. Youths burned tyres and tried to burn the patrol's Hilux.
- At Oko-Afo in July 2026, Customs officers and soldiers chasing a bus with three bags of rice allegedly opened fire, killing one resident and injuring two.
- At Gbaji, smugglers reportedly met a Customs patrol with machetes, local guns and charms, and threatened to set fuel alight.
- **Zangbeto as community justice:** it acts as an informal police force and court, and some residents prefer it to the police.

#### 13. Environment
- Flooding in Mowo, Ikoga, Ajara-Vetho and nearby areas, made worse by drains blocked with plastic.
- Illegal dredging at Ganyingbo and Gbaji-Yeketome: residents say it threatens Gbaji Bridge and drives fishermen away, and floodwater there has stayed for weeks.
- Salt water has eroded the memorial at the Point of No Return.
- A 2017 report said filming in Badagry was hard because electricity was unreliable.

#### 14. Festivals and sacred things
- **Zangbeto:** a raffia-cone masquerade of night guardians. The Ajido festival is held every three years and includes a regatta.
- The annual **Badagry Diaspora Festival** (since 1999) has Sato drumming and masquerades.
- The **Igunnuko** festival is also held here.
- **Chiefs:** the Akran of Badagry and his white-cap high chiefs, and the Aholu (king) of Ajido.
- **FLAG:** never show Zangbeto as a gang, villain or costume a criminal hides in. Touching its robes is a grave offence, and Ogu belief holds there is no human inside. The same goes for the Sato drum, the Thangbe pythons, Vodún shrines and the slave-route sites. Never use them as crime locations or for comedy.

#### 15. In pop culture
- **Jemeji** (DSTV, from 2016) is a soap about Badagry, shot in Iyana Ipaja because filming in Badagry was costly, short of power and had security concerns.
- In 2025 a crime film titled *Badagry (I Bad I Gree)* was forced to rename itself *I Bad* after residents petitioned the censors board.
- The drummer Babatunde Olatunji came from Ajido.

#### 16. What Gidi can borrow
1. **Border-district "rice run" missions:** Tunde carries bags of rice on an okada through bush paths around official checkpoints. Heat rises as agents leave the checkpoints to chase him, and the run ends in a chase into residential streets.
2. **A highway checkpoint gauntlet:** one long expressway to the border with many checkpoints of different "agencies" (made up for Gidi). Each one is a choice: pay, talk your way through, or detour into flood or bush.
3. **A creek ferry and speedboat layer:** waterside jetties, ferry routes as a way around the traffic, "pirate" risk, and outboard-motor boats that cut out mid-chase.
4. **A coastal-frontier district feel:** coconut-palm beaches, unfinished villas, and a colonial heritage strip with balconied two-storey buildings. Keep it as quiet scenery, not crime turf.
5. **Night-guardian folklore as ambient world rules:** a fictional, non-sacred "night watch" curfew mechanic where locals clear the streets. Invent it from scratch and do not borrow Zangbeto imagery.
6. **Characters:** a clearing agent fixer, a fisherman whose waters are being ruined by dredgers, a fuel smuggler who threatens to burn the fuel rather than give it up, and a Customs patrol leader.

#### 17. Things to avoid
- Real names: Badagry, Seme, Gbaji, Ajido, Gberefu, Seriki Abass, Mobee, the Akran, the Nigeria Customs Service, the 243 Recce Battalion, Eiye and Aiye.
- Zangbeto, Sato, Vodún shrines and pythons, and any slave-trade site or artefact used as a backdrop for crime.
- Real people named in the reporting, and real victims.
- A Badagry community has already forced a film to change its title over image concerns, and *Gangs of Lagos* was sued over its Eyo portrayal.

## Ikorodu LGA: research brief for Gidi

#### 1. In one line
Ikorodu is Lagos's huge north-eastern frontier town on the lagoon. Its royal and masquerade traditions are much older than the city that has grown around it. Every morning it sends a million-odd commuters down one choked road to the city, and when its own traditions call a curfew, the town shuts down.

#### 2. Areas inside it
- **Ikorodu town (Ilu Ikorodu):** the old core, split into 16–17 "Ituns" (quarters). It has the Ayangburen's palace, Ayangburen Road, T.O.S. Benson Road and Ladega Market.
- **Ebute / Ipakodo:** the lagoon edge, with the ferry jetty, the Customs Lighter Terminal and the recreational park.
- **Agric, Ogolonto, Majidun, Haruna:** stops along the Ikorodu Road corridor that are getting more suburban.
- **Igbogbo/Bayeku, Ijede, Imota:** separate towns, each with its own Oba and its own council (LCDA). Ijede has the Egbin power station and hot springs. Imota has a rice mill and Caleb University. They feel semi-rural.
- **Ibeshe, Isiu, Aleke, Itamaga, Odongunyan, Owutu:** outer settlements with poor infrastructure. Most of the Badoo attacks happened here.

#### 3. Landmarks and monuments
- **Ikorodu Oga Statue** at the garage roundabout. It shows a hunter with a rifle standing on an elephant, more than 15 ft tall, in concrete and bronze. It was unveiled on 2 February 1991 and designed by Samuel Oduyebo.
- **Palace of the Ayangburen** on Ayangburen Road.
- **Iledi Oshugbo Abalaiye**, the sacred grove where Ikorodu's kings are installed.
- **Omitunmishe (Osun) Temple**, Ikorodu Town Hall Library, Ebute ferry terminal, Egbin Thermal Station (Nigeria's largest power plant), Imota Rice Mill (opened 2021), Ikorodu Recreational Park, Cradoo Lake Waterfront.
- **Old markets:** Ajina (the oldest), Obu Ale (a night market) and Oburoke (a widows' market).

#### 4. Buildings and streets
*Mostly unverified general knowledge, so treat it with care.* The old town has bungalows and older storey houses with corrugated roofs packed close together. The growth areas have plastered-block "face-me-I-face-you" compounds, half-built houses where people live behind unrendered walls, gated estates (the Wikivoyage page mentions a "Tinubu Estate Road") and churches in converted warehouses. Main roads are dual carriageways. Side streets are laterite (red dirt) with open gutters. There are hardly any high-rises. The town is low-rise and sprawls outward.

#### 5. Roads, junctions, traffic
- **Ikorodu Road (Mile 12 to Ikorodu)** is the spine. The 13.5 km upgrade, built by CCECC with World Bank money, added BRT lanes. Its stations are Mile 12, Owode Onirin, Owode, Irawo, Majidun, Ogolonto, Agric and Haruna, with terminals at Mile 12, Agric and Ikorodu, plus 10 pedestrian bridges.
- The famous names are the **Ikorodu Garage roundabout**, Agric, Ogolonto, Majidun and Owode Onirin (a spare-parts area; *it may actually sit in Kosofe LGA*).
- The other roads run to Sagamu and to Itokin/Epe.

#### 6. Economy and markets
Trading, farming and factories. Historically the town gathered fish, poultry, cassava, palm oil, vegetables and fruit for Lagos, and it has pottery and truck farms. The industrial belt is around Ikorodu and the rice mill is at Imota. The informal economy runs on market traders (Ladega, Imota Big Market, Obada Morning Market), transport workers and spare-parts sellers. Many residents commute to Lagos for work.

#### 7. Transport
- **BRT:** the BRT runs to TBS, Ikeja and Oshodi. Buses have AC, phone charging and sometimes films. The trip is about 45 minutes in light traffic and much longer in heavy traffic.
- **Ferries:** the Ebute jetty is government-run and the best organised. Private jetties at Bayeku, Ijede and Badore are, according to reporting, run by untrained staff and allegedly controlled by a cartel. Boats are overloaded, life jackets are dirty, and water hyacinth slows the routes.
- **Local trips:** danfo, keke and okada.
- **Okada ban:** the 2022 phases named Ikeja, the Island areas and Kosofe/Oshodi/Shomolu/Mushin. **I could not confirm whether Ikorodu was included.**

#### 8. Sound, smell, look
*Partly inferred.* The day starts with a pre-dawn rush to the garage and BRT, with conductors shouting "Mile 12! Obalende!". In the afternoon the markets are busy. In rain the red-mud side streets flood. The lagoon smells of fish and engine fuel. At night the outskirts go dark and it is hard to find a ride, which Wikivoyage confirms. On Oro days the town goes eerily empty and the markets are shuttered.

#### 9. People, languages, religion
The population was about 536k in 2006 and is now estimated at over 1 million. People speak Yoruba, with the Ijebu dialect common, plus Pidgin, and Igbo traders work in the markets (for example Ladega). Founding families trace back to the Remo/Ijebu people and to Benin migrants (the Olisa line, from about 1630). Christianity, Islam and traditional worship live side by side. The Ogboni/Osugbo, Oro, Egungun and Obaluaye groups are active.

#### 10. Food and nightlife
I found little that is verified. There are fast-food chains (Sweet Sensation, Domino's) on Ayangburen and T.O.S. Benson Roads, local bukas and canteens such as "Iya Greatest Food Canteen" in Imota, and evening markets like Ajina. Bars include "F1 Bar & Club" and 24-hour "Pegrite Place". Nightlife is modest compared with Island Lagos.

#### 11. History
- The founder Oga, a hunter-prince of Remo, killed an elephant and took the title "Oga" (leader). The name comes from "Oko Odu", a dye-plant farm. *This is oral tradition.*
- In the mid-1800s Ikorodu became a Remo trading post on the Lagos–Ibadan route.
- During the Ibadan–Ijaiye wars of 1860–65, Ikorodu smuggled arms past the Ijebu–Egba blockade. In 1865 British forces from Lagos defeated the Egba army at Ikorodu.
- Over the last 40 years, rapid sprawl has turned it into a satellite city of Lagos.

#### 12. Danger and law
- **The Badoo attacks, June 2016 to July 2017:** at least 26 people were killed in 15 night attacks. The killers entered through windows at 1–2am and crushed heads with grinding stones and pestles. Reporting claims they wiped up blood with white cloths for ritual use. One attack hit a Cherubim and Seraphim church after a vigil.
- **The response:** residents protested at the Oba's palace. The traditional council held a three-night spiritual ritual. Vigilantes (OPC, Onyabo, Neighbourhood Watch) formed patrols, and about a dozen suspects were lynched. Police announced 15, then 87, then "100+" arrests.
- **Other crime:** in 2016 reporting, militants and kidnappers were active around Isawo/Agric.
- **Trust:** reporters doubted that the police could cope.

#### 13. Environment
The town sits on the lagoon edge. Floods are recurrent: in August 2025, Oko-Ope in Ijede was submerged after 12 hours of rain and residents were trapped. Water hyacinth chokes the boat routes. The Egbin plant sits in the area, yet power cuts are still common *(general knowledge)*.

#### 14. Festivals and sacred things
- Oro (Magbo and Liwe/Live) is held in May and June. During Oro, women and non-natives must stay indoors and markets close. Tradition says breaking this is punishable by death.
- Other festivals: Ikorodu-Oga Day, Odun Osu, Eyibi, Ogun (September–October), and Egungun.
- **FLAG:** never portray these as criminal or cult activity, never show what Oro looks like, and never make it a joke: Oro, the Osugbo/Ogboni sacred grove, Egungun masquerades, Osun shrines, or the Oba's office. Badoo was a criminal gang, not a traditional rite, and the game must keep the two clearly separate.

#### 15. In pop culture
The **Ikorodu Bois** (Muiz and Malik Sanni and Fawas Aina) went viral remaking film trailers with household items. Netflix sent them production equipment in 2020. Ikorodu is also a common setting for Nollywood and Yoruba films and a stock "far away" joke in Lagos slang *(not sourced)*.

#### 16. What Gidi can borrow
1. **A hunter-on-elephant roundabout statue** as a district landmark and okada meeting point. Invent a different founder myth for it.
2. **A curfew day:** on a random day a traditional curfew empties the district. Markets shut, traffic disappears, and Tunde has to reroute deliveries or wait it out. NPCs warn him and the game never shows what is behind the curfew. It changes gameplay without turning the tradition into the enemy.
3. **Ferry-jetty runs:** overloaded speedboats, hyacinth slowing the boat, and a jetty cartel deciding who rides. This is a good water route for moving oja (goods).
4. **A night-terror storyline** inspired by Badoo: an invented gang, rumours spreading in the neighbourhood, vigilante checkpoints at night, and the danger of a mob mistaking Tunde for a killer.
5. **A corridor commute:** a long BRT road with named stations, footbridges and mid-road go-slows.
6. **Flood-zone deliveries** on red-mud side streets during rainy season.

#### 17. Things to avoid
- The real names Ikorodu, Ayangburen, Oga/Ogaremade, Badoo, Egbin and Caleb.
- Real people: Oba Shotobi, the Ikorodu Bois, Gani Adams, and the named victims.
- The OPC as a faction name.
- Real brands: Sweet Sensation, Domino's, Justrite.
- Any copy or depiction of the Oro bullroarer, the Osugbo grove, or Ogboni symbols.
