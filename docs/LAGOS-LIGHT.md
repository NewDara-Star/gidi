# Lagos light: why the city looks different

The owner's observation: photos taken in Nigeria look different from photos taken abroad. The vibrancy and the feel of the light aren't the same. Most of it is physics. This is the lighting brief for Gidi's look, in the browser now and in Unreal later (`docs/UNREAL-PORT-PLAN.md`).

## 1. A high sun
- Lagos is about 6° north of the equator, so for most of the day the sun is nearly overhead.
- **The result:**
  - short, hard shadows
  - deep shade under eaves, canopies and trees
  - faces lit from above, with dark eye sockets
  - colours that read harsh rather than rich
- **Abroad:** further north the sun sits lower for much of the year. Low, raking light passes through more atmosphere, turns warmer and casts long soft shadows. That is the light most people think of as "good photo light".

## 2. A short golden hour
- Near the equator the sun rises and sets almost straight up and down. The warm, flattering light at dawn and dusk lasts only about 20–30 minutes.
- Further north it lasts an hour or more, and in summer much longer.
- Dusk in Lagos arrives fast: bright day, a brief orange, then night.

## 3. Thick air
- **Humidity:** water vapour scatters light. Distant buildings lose contrast, and the sky is pale or milky more often than deep blue.
- **Harmattan (roughly December to February):** Saharan dust fills the air.
  - The sun becomes a soft orange disc you can look at.
  - Everything takes on a beige-orange haze.
  - Contrast drops, and the horizon disappears.
- **Smoke and pollution:** traffic, generators and burning add particles, and more haze.
- **Abroad:** cold, dry, clean air gives the crisp, saturated, "high-definition" look.

## 4. The ground colours the light
- Red laterite soil and its dust coat roads, walls, kerbs, cars and feet. Rusty corrugated roofs are everywhere.
- Light bouncing off all that warm, reddish ground tints the shadows and the people (warm bounce light).
- Against the haze sits a loud palette:
  - painted walls and hand-painted signs
  - posters and billboards
  - ankara fabrics
  - the yellow danfos and kekes
  - umbrellas over the stalls

## 5. Cameras struggle with it
- Phone cameras are tuned for average scenes. Lagos gives them blinding sun next to deep shade, and dark skin tones in harsh light.
- Auto exposure and white balance often get it wrong: blown-out highlights, crushed shadows, odd colour casts.
- Overcast light abroad is a soft, even scene that cameras handle well and that flatters skin.

## 6. Seasons and times of day are different looks
| State | Look |
|---|---|
| **Midday, dry season** | white-hot, overhead, hard short shadows, bleached colour, heat shimmer on the tarmac |
| **Harmattan** | beige-orange haze, orange sun disc, low contrast, dust on everything, cool mornings |
| **Rainy season, just after a downpour** | the air briefly clean, greens intensely saturated, wet roads mirroring the sky, puddles and floods in the low spots, dramatic tropical clouds |
| **Overcast rainy-season day** | flat, grey-white, humid, soft shadows |
| **The short golden hour** | 20–30 minutes of warm orange, long shadows at last, then fast blue dusk |
| **Night** | patchy: generator-lit shops and kiosks, sodium and LED streetlights where they work, dark gaps where they don't, headlights in the haze, phone and lantern light at the stalls |

## For Gidi (lighting brief)
- **Sun:** high overhead for most of the day; short, hard shadows; a strong sky light filling the shade.
- **Atmosphere:** a little haze always, more over distance; Harmattan as a weather state with an orange cast, low contrast and a soft sun disc.
- **Bounce:** warm, reddish ground light from laterite and rusty roofs, tinting shadows and the undersides of things.
- **Golden hour:** short and intense; dusk fast.
- **After rain:** clean air, saturated greens, wet reflective roads, standing water in the flood-prone low ground (tie to the terrain and the "floods and low spots" Lagos-ism).
- **Night:** uneven, generator-driven light; whole streets dark during a blackout (the Maze's Transformer 44 event).
- **Colour:** keep the loud palette (paint, signs, fabrics, yellow danfos) against hazy, slightly desaturated backgrounds. The contrast between the two is the Lagos look.
