# Civic and public-service buildings: a catalogue for the Gidi generator

Scope: hospitals, clinics, chemists, police (stations, command, posts, checkpoints, barracks), fire, government offices, customs, prison, army, courts, cemeteries, post offices, traffic/task-force posts, and power-company offices and transformers. This builds on `docs/look/research-buildings.md` sections 10.7, 10.8, 10.11 and 10.12, and on `docs/look/research-street.md` sections 2.2 and 3.8. It goes further and does not repeat those sections.

Rule zero holds throughout. Agencies are described by role and a generic livery, never by real name, crest or logo. Gidi must invent its own crests, acronyms and slogans. Real places are cited only as examples of a pattern.

Tags: **[src]** means the number comes from a listed source (see the numbered list at the end, e.g. [src S4]). **[est]** means our own estimate from photo knowledge of Lagos. Most dimensions and every hex value are [est]. Public reporting on these buildings is about condition and money, not geometry.

## 0. Shared civic kit (the grammar every public building uses)

Lagos public buildings share a kit, whatever the agency. Model these once and reuse them everywhere. Each building then differs mainly in its **agency livery**, **massing** and **props**.

### 0.1 The 1970s–80s concrete government block
- **Looks like:**
  - A rectangular slab, 3–8 storeys, 12–16 m deep and 30–80 m long [est]. Exposed concrete frame grid with infill panels.
  - Horizontal sunshade fins or "egg-crate" brise-soleil over windows. Long corridor galleries on one side.
  - Steel-framed glass louvre windows (now often swapped for aluminium sliders with tinted glass). Split-AC units bolted below the sills.
  - A flat roof with a parapet, water tanks and an HF/VHF mast. One stair tower expressed as a vertical slab with ventilation blocks.
- **Kit parts:**
  - Frame bay 3.6–4.5 m wide, floor-to-floor 3.3–3.6 m [est].
  - Fin module: 0.6 m projecting horizontal slab every floor, plus vertical fins at 1.2 m spacing for egg-crate variants.
  - Breeze-block (decorative perforated block) panels 0.4×0.4 m at stairwells.
  - Entrance canopy: a cantilevered slab 3–5 m deep on two columns, with the agency name in raised letters on the fascia.
- **Colours (hex) [est]:** off-white #E8E2D2, cream #E6D6A8, government ivory #EDE3C8. Agency bands as in 0.6. Stained concrete #8E8A80. Black rain-streaks #3B3A36 under every sill.
- **Materials:** in-situ reinforced concrete, sandcrete blockwork, cement render, terrazzo floors, steel louvre frames.
- **Condition states:**
  - *New/renovated*: fresh paint, blue-tinted or reflective glass, aluminium cladding (ACP) on the ground floor or entrance, a new granite stair.
  - *Worn*: sun-faded paint, AC drip stains, louvres missing a blade or two, some windows with cardboard.
  - *Decayed*: black mould streaks full-height, spalling concrete showing rusting rebar, plants growing from gutters, broken louvres. An abandoned top floor.
  - *Half-renovated* (very common): one block freshly clad and the next one decayed, both on the same compound.
- **SELECT BY:** any `civic` kind; age bucket from the district (old_island and civic central = older); wealth plus "showcase" flag (near the governor's office = renovated).

### 0.2 Perimeter wall
- 2.1–2.7 m sandcrete wall in 3 m bays between 0.23 m piers, with a coping [est].
- Topped with one of: broken bottles set in cement (older), 3–5 strands of barbed wire on angled steel brackets, razor-wire coil (0.45–0.6 m), or electric fence on standoffs (elite or HQ).
- Painted in the agency's base colour with a band at 0.3–0.6 m (dark plinth to hide splash), or bare grey.
- Stencils: "POST NO BILL", "NO PARKING", "THIS PROPERTY IS NOT FOR SALE — BEWARE" (on contested land), and the government demolition "X". Posters on the outside, never the inside.
- Shops and kiosks lean against the outside of the wall on busy roads (traders exploit the blank frontage). Agencies periodically demolish them, so this is a seeded state: `wall_shacks: none / few / lined` [est 30% / 45% / 25% on main roads].

### 0.3 Gate, barrier, sentry box
- **Main gate:** a pair of steel leaves, 2×2.5 m, or a 6–8 m sliding gate, set between two thick piers 0.6–1.2 m square and 3–4 m tall. Often a concrete arch or portal spans them with the agency name and crest [est].
- **Barrier arm:** 4–6 m steel pipe, painted red/white in 0.5 m stripes. Counterweighted, lifted by hand. On cheap sites it is a bamboo pole or a rope with rags [est].
- **Sentry box:** 1.2×1.2 to 2×2 m, 2.4 m tall, flat or hipped roof, window on three sides. Painted in agency colours with diagonal stripes on the corners. Often a plastic chair and a bucket beside it.
- **Speed bumps** just inside and outside: 2–3 humps, painted yellow/black or white/black.
- **Pedestrian wicket:** a 0.9 m steel door in the main gate. Queues form here, not at the vehicle gate.
- **Signage:** "ALL VISITORS REPORT AT GATE", "STOP FOR CHECK", "SLOW DOWN", "NO ENTRY WITHOUT AUTHORITY".

### 0.4 Crest signboard
- A freestanding board on two posts, 2.4–4.8 m wide × 1.2–2.4 m high, bottom 1–1.5 m above ground [est].
- Layout: crest at left (or centred above), agency name in capitals, a sub-line ("DIVISIONAL HEADQUARTERS", "MINISTRY OF ..."), sometimes a motto in a ribbon.
- Painted metal or flex print on a steel frame. Older boards are hand-painted and fade to pastel. A small roof cap on the board is common.
- Gidi: invent a state crest (e.g. a shield with waves, a palm and a crossed oar) and an agency palette per kind. Never use the real federal or Lagos State arms.

### 0.5 Ceremonial compound furniture
- **Flagpoles:** 1–3 steel poles 6–9 m on a raised plinth, outside the main block [est]. Flags are often faded or missing. One pole may be bent.
- **Painted kerbs:** white, or black-and-white 1 m alternation, along internal roads. Tree trunks whitewashed to 1–1.2 m. Painted stones lining paths (white, sometimes alternating with the agency colour). Strongest in army and police compounds, weaker elsewhere.
- **Car park:** herringbone or 90° bays under shade trees (almond, neem, flamboyant). Officials' SUVs in front, staff buses behind.
- **Generator house:** a 3×5 m block hut with a louvred door and a stack, with a 0.5–1 MVA diesel set (see the street doc for small sets). Diesel drums and a black oil stain around it.
- **Water:** an elevated tank on a steel stand (4–8 m) or a row of plastic tanks on a concrete plinth. A borehole with a "WATER" stencil.
- **Shade sheds:** open steel canopies 6×12 m with plastic chairs or benches for waiting.
- **Notice boards:** glass-fronted or plain boards with A4 sheets, near the entrance.
- **Mammy shed / canteen:** a zinc-roofed open structure at the back with women cooking (every large compound).

### 0.7 Queues and crowds (life rules for all civic items)
- Queues form at: the pedestrian wicket, the cashier or revenue window, the records counter, the pharmacy hatch, the customer-service hall door.
- Queue length scales with `importance × time_of_day`. Peak 08:00–11:00 [est]. A queue of 10–60 people in a line along the wall, with umbrellas and fans.
- Hawkers cluster within 20 m of every public gate: pure-water sachets, snacks, phone credit, photocopy and passport-photo stands (on a table with a generator), "business centre" kiosks typing forms.
- Okada and keke drop-off clumps at the gate. Danfos stop on the road in front ("bus stop" named after the building).
- Night: the compound is lit by 2–6 floodlights on poles or on the gate piers, plus the generator hum. Sentries sit under one bulb. The street outside goes dark if the grid is off.

## 1. Teaching hospital campus

- **Looks like:**
  - A large walled campus on the mainland with a mix of buildings from four eras. The real pattern: a teaching hospital set up in 1961–62 by adapting an existing mainland hospital's buildings, on a former farmland site of about 92 acres (37 ha) [src S23].
  - 1960s 2–4-storey ward blocks linked by covered walkways. 1970s–80s 5–8-storey slab blocks (the main clinical tower). Newer glass-fronted specialist centres (cancer, cardiac) funded by donors. Long-abandoned or "under reconstruction" wings [src S23 reports a shut diagnostic block and decommissioned centre].
  - A main gate on a congested road. The access road floods in heavy rain. In the real pattern, residents near a teaching hospital in Mushin complain of repeated flooding along the hospital road, blamed on blocked drains and bad surfacing [src S1, S2].
  - Inside: a ring road, staff quarters at the back, a medical school with lecture theatres, a mortuary with a separate gate, a mosque and a chapel, banks and food courts near the gate.
- **Kit parts:**
  - Ward block: 2–3 storeys, 12 m deep, 60–90 m long, with open-sided corridors, louvre windows, an asbestos or long-span roof [est].
  - Tower block: 6–8 storeys, 15×60 m, egg-crate fins, lift core (lifts often broken), a big signboard on the roof parapet [est].
  - Covered walkway: 2.5–3 m wide, steel or concrete posts every 4 m, corrugated roof at 2.7 m; runs 50–300 m between blocks [est].
  - Emergency entrance: a canopy 8×6 m with an ambulance bay, a ramp at 1:12, a red "ACCIDENT & EMERGENCY" sign.
  - Gate complex: twin gates (in/out) with a barrier and a sentry box each, and a pedestrian gate. A pharmacy and bank cluster just inside.
  - Specialist centre: a 3–5-storey block with ACP cladding and glass curtain wall, a donor name in big letters [est].
  - Mortuary: a single-storey block at the back with a separate gate, hearses parked, and mourners in matching *aso-ebi* cloth.
- **Dimensions [est]:** campus 20–40 ha, gate frontage 150–400 m; 30–60 buildings; total beds 700–1,000.
- **Colours [est]:** ward blocks cream #E6D6A8 with sea-green band #3E8E6E; towers off-white #E8E2D2 with black rain streaks; donor centres silver ACP #BFC3C6 plus blue glass #4D6E8C.
- **Materials:** concrete frame, sandcrete, terrazzo, steel louvres, asbestos sheets (old wards), long-span aluminium (re-roofed wards).
- **Variants:** whole campus is one item, but block states vary inside it: 40% worn, 25% renovated, 20% decayed, 10% new donor block, 5% abandoned or under reconstruction [est].
- **Condition states:**
  - Worn: patched roofs, stained walls, tired paint, a ward with a tarpaulin over a leak.
  - Renovated: fresh paint, new aluminium windows, an AC on every window.
  - Decayed: closed wing with a chained gate, broken glass, shrubs on the roof.
  - Flood state: water 0.2–0.6 m deep across the access road and gate approach; vehicles crawl; people wade with shoes in hand [est].
- **Props and life:**
  - Day: crowds at the gate (hundreds), queues at the records and cashier windows, relatives sleeping on mats and benches outside wards, nurses in white or blue, doctors in white coats, students with notes, tricycles, private ambulances, police at the A&E.
  - Food vendors and photocopy shops along the outside wall. Herbal sellers and blood-donor touts near the gate [est].
  - Night: emergency lit, rest dark; relatives sleeping outside; generator roar; the gate road lined with okada and food stalls lit by lamps.
- **SELECT BY:** unique (one per city); `civic` or `dense_mainland` edge; on a main road with a known low point (floods); big land parcel.
- **Gidi mapping:** Gidi Teaching Hospital (and its flooding road).

## 2. General hospital (state)

- **Looks like:**
  - A walled compound of 2–5 buildings on a main road: an older 2–3-storey ward block (1950s–70s), a 3–5-storey outpatient/admin block (1980s), and often a new glass-and-cladding block for a specialist unit (maternity, trauma) [est].
  - The real pattern: a Lagos Island general hospital where several billion naira of renovations still left many structures in disrepair and a faulty lift became notorious [src S13]. Use "renovation in progress" as a normal state, not an exception.
  - A big crest signboard at the gate. "GENERAL HOSPITAL" lettering on the roof parapet of the main block.
- **Kit parts:**
  - Main block: 3–5 storeys, 14×50 m, a ramp along the frontage, an entrance canopy [est].
  - Ward block: 2 storeys, 12×40 m, with an open veranda corridor and louvres.
  - Ambulance bay with a red "EMERGENCY" sign and a plastic-chair waiting shed.
  - Pharmacy hatch: a window 1.2×0.8 m with a steel grille and a queue.
  - Mortuary at the back; a waste incinerator chimney (2–3 m brick stack, sooty) [est]; oxygen-cylinder cage.
  - Car park with 20–60 bays, half taken by staff and half by touts' cars.
- **Dimensions [est]:** site 1–4 ha; 100–300 beds; frontage 80–200 m.
- **Colours [est]:** cream #E6D6A8 or white #F3F1EA; band sea green #3E8E6E or hospital blue #3C78B4; red cross #D2232A on signs.
- **Variants:** general mainland (40%), island old-town (20%), rail-side (10%), waterfront/shore (10%), new-build sprawl (20%) [est].
  - *Railside*: a long side boundary along the track; a footpath gap in the wall where people cross the rails.
  - *Island (old town)*: denser, taller, hemmed in by the market; the compound has no setback.
  - *Shore*: raised plinth (0.6–1 m) for flood; salt-faded paint; a jetty or boat landing nearby for patients from water communities [est].
- **Condition states:** new (glass block, fresh paint), worn (faded, AC drips), decayed (closed blocks, roof leaks), half-built (a new block's frame with starter bars beside the old one).
- **Props and life:** queues from 07:00; relatives on benches; nurse uniforms in white or light blue; a chapel or prayer room; a police post at the emergency; private ambulances; food sellers under umbrellas outside the gate; a burial-services shop across the road (coffins on display) [est].
- **SELECT BY:** `kind=hospital` with `tier=general`; main road; one per 1–3 districts; variant by district (railside if a rail line borders the plot; shore if waterfront; island if old_island).
- **Gidi mapping:** State General Hospital, Railside General Hospital, Island General Hospital, Shore General Hospital.

## 3. Maternity home and primary health centre

- **Looks like:**
  - *Older maternity home / PHC*: a single-storey long block (25–40 m) with a 2–3 m deep veranda along the front on square columns, a low hipped roof, louvre windows, a signboard on two posts [est].
  - *New maternal centre*: a 3–4-storey block. In the real pattern, new state maternal-and-child centres are four-storey buildings with 100–160 beds, ramped entrances, a fire stair, and two waiting sheds [src S20]; a similar centre is three floors with 149 beds [src S20].
  - Walls painted with health murals: a mother and baby, mosquito nets, vaccination days, hand-washing. Painted by hand in bright flat colours.
  - Some PHCs are unfinished for years or look abandoned [src S20].
- **Kit parts:**
  - Veranda block: 8–10 m deep, 3 m storey height, veranda 2.5 m with a 0.45 m plinth and 3 steps [est].
  - Waiting shed: an open steel frame 6×10 m with long-span roof and 6–10 benches [est].
  - Ramp 1:12 with a steel rail.
  - Mural panels 2×3 m on the end walls.
  - Incinerator pit, a placenta pit with a concrete slab (rural/older) [est].
- **Colours [est]:** white #F3F1EA walls; band and columns sky blue #6BA6D6 or green #4E9A5E; murals in primary colours (#E43F3F, #F2C230, #2F7FC1, #3C9A4A).
- **Variants:** old veranda PHC 55%, converted house 20%, new 3–4-storey centre 15%, abandoned/unfinished PHC 10% [est].
- **Condition states:** new (bright paint, signboard with a donor logo, solar panels on the roof); worn (faded mural, missing louvres); abandoned (weeds in the yard, roof sheets lifting, a padlocked door, goats).
- **Props and life:** day: mothers with babies on their backs sitting on benches, a nurse with a register, immunisation-day crowds, keke drop-offs. Night: one bulb on the veranda, a security man on a bench, a birth in progress lit by a lantern when the power fails.
- **SELECT BY:** `kind=clinic` with `tier=public`; any district, more in `dense_mainland` and `sprawl`; back streets as often as main roads. Jungle variant: dense trees, red earth, a footpath approach on the urban fringe.
- **Gidi mapping:** Jungle Maternity Home, public clinics.

## 4. Elite private hospital

- **Looks like:**
  - A 3–8-storey contemporary block on a corner plot in the elite district: glass curtain wall, aluminium composite cladding in white and a brand accent, a projecting porte-cochère [est].
  - A clean painted wall with railings so you can see in (the elite wall is a display, not a fortress), lush lawn strips, palms in planters.
  - The brand name in backlit 3D letters on the parapet and on a monolith at the gate.
- **Kit parts:**
  - Block: 20×30 m footprint, floor-to-floor 3.6 m; curtain wall in 1.5 m modules; a glass lobby double height [est].
  - Porte-cochère: a steel and glass canopy 8×10 m at 4.5 m clearance [est].
  - Gate monolith: 1×3 m stone-clad slab with the name.
  - Car park with interlocking paving (grey and red), 20–40 bays, a valet stand, an ambulance bay with a white ambulance.
  - Rooftop: helipad marking (rare, top tier), AC condensers, a soundproofed generator enclosure (container-style, 2.5×6 m).
- **Colours [est]:** white #F5F5F2, warm grey #A9A69E, glass #5C7A8F, brand accent (seeded teal #1F8A8A or burgundy #7A1F3D).
- **Variants:** purpose-built tower 50%, converted villa (2–3 storeys with a glass extension) 40%, medical "mall" with labs and pharmacy 10% [est].
- **Condition:** new or renovated almost always; worn means slightly dated 2000s blue glass and green ACP.
- **Props and life:** SUVs with drivers waiting, uniformed private guards (not police), a reception visible through glass, potted plants. Night: fully lit, no generator noise audible, a sentry in a smart booth.
- **SELECT BY:** `elite` district; wealth high; main road or prestige avenue.
- **Gidi mapping:** Heights Private Hospital.

## 5. Private clinic and dockworkers' clinic

- **Looks like:** (extends research-buildings 10.7)
  - A converted 2–3-storey house. The ground floor is reception and consulting; upper floors are wards. A big signboard (2.4×1.2 m) mounted on the fence or the balcony: red cross, clinic name, services list ("SCAN • LAB • MATERNITY • 24 HRS").
  - A ramp added to the steps in raw concrete. A small generator in a cage. A "NO PARKING — AMBULANCE" stencil.
  - Clinics with a scan/lab add a printed flex banner listing tests.
- **Dockworkers' clinic (port):** a 1–2-storey block inside or at the edge of the port zone, run by a union or company. Looks like a cut-down general hospital: cream with a blue band, a stencilled name, a first-aid ambulance (often a converted bus), queues of men in boots and reflective vests at shift change [est].
- **Kit parts:** house shell (from the residential kit) + signboard + ramp + generator cage + red cross light box + flex banners.
- **Colours [est]:** white #F3F1EA; green band #3E8E6E or blue #3C78B4; red cross #D2232A.
- **Variants:** converted house 70%, purpose-built 2–3-storey clinic 20%, dockworkers'/company clinic 10% (port only) [est].
- **Condition:** worn is the norm; decayed (faded sign, broken light box) common on back streets.
- **Props:** a nurse at the gate, a bench of patients, a keke parked, a pharmacy in the front room. Night: red cross light box lit, "24 HRS" painted.
- **SELECT BY:** `kind=clinic` `tier=private`; any district; frequency high in `dense_mainland` and `sprawl`; dockworkers' clinic only in `port`.
- **Gidi mapping:** clinics, dockworkers' clinic.

## 6. Chemist, pharmacy, patent medicine store

- **Looks like:** (extends research-buildings 10.8)
  - *Patent medicine store (PMS)*: a single shop bay 3×4 m or a kiosk. A wooden counter or a glass display case at the front, shelves behind packed with coloured cartons to the ceiling, a fan, a plastic stool. Often a small hand-painted sign; in the real pattern, a study of drug shops in two Lagos areas found only 1.7% had the required sign-posts [src S15]. So most PMS have no proper sign, or just "CHEMIST" painted on the wall.
  - *Pharmacy*: a glass-fronted shop 4–8 m wide, a green cross light box (0.6–1 m) projecting from the fascia, a fascia sign with "PHARMACY" and the name, steel roller shutter, a burglar-proof door.
  - Rack of "MEDICINE" posters and branded flex banners from drug makers (invent).
- **Kit parts:** shop bay from the shop kit; green cross light box (square or plus-shaped, 0.6–1.0 m, LED-lit); glass display counter 1.8×0.6×1 m; shelving 2.4 m high; posters.
- **Colours [est]:** cross green #1FA05A; fascia white #FFFFFF with green text; kiosk walls painted pale green #9BD4A8 or white.
- **Variants:** PMS shop bay 55%, PMS kiosk 20%, registered pharmacy 20%, chain pharmacy (elite, mall) 5% [est].
- **Condition:** cross box dark or flickering in decayed states; shutters rusty; faded drug-maker posters.
- **Props and life:** a customer at the counter, a nurse-type attendant in a white coat, a bench for injections inside (PMS often give injections). Night: the green cross is one of the brightest lights on the street; the chemist is open late.
- **SELECT BY:** front shops on main/collector roads; every 150–400 m in `dense_mainland` [est]; near any hospital gate (cluster 3–8 within 100 m); pharmacy tier by wealth.
- **Gidi mapping:** chemists, pharmacies, patent medicine stores.

## 7. Divisional police station plus vehicle and okada pound

## 22. Power company offices and transformers

### 22.1 Power company district office ("NEPA office")
- **Looks like:**
  - The old national utility's district offices still exist as the regional distribution company's business-unit offices: a 2–3-storey block on a compound full of utility vans, cable drums, poles and old transformers [est].
  - A customer-service hall with a queue of people holding bills and meters (complaints about estimated billing, prepaid meters); in the real pattern, communities without power for months go to the local office and find officials avoiding them [src S19].
  - The front gate often has posters about vandalism and theft of cables; communities lose supply when transformer cables are stolen [src S19].
- **Kit parts:** office block (0.1 kit, 2–3 storeys, 12×30 m) [est]; customer hall with a glass front and a queue shed; yard 30×40 m with cable drums (1.2–2 m diameter, wooden), spare concrete poles lying in stacks, scrap transformers rusting, a crane truck [est]; injection substation next door (see 22.2).
- **Colours [est]:** older offices cream #E2D6B4 with green #2E6B3A trim (legacy utility); new branding white with a seeded corporate colour; yellow #F4C20D danger signs.
- **Variants:** legacy office 60%, rebranded office 30%, customer-service kiosk in a plaza 10% [est].

### 22.2 Transformers and substations (extends research-street 2.2)
- **Looks like:**
  - Community transformer: a tank on a plinth inside a cage (block or chain-link), often with a nickname or number painted on the cage (Gidi: "Transformer 44"). The cage is covered in community paint: the CDA name, "DO NOT URINATE HERE", "TOUCH AND DIE" [est].
  - Signs of theft and vandalism: missing cables, open terminals, a padlocked steel door, and community vigilantes who guard it at night [src S19].
  - Injection substation: a walled yard 30×40 m with switchgear, 2–3 large transformers (3×3×3.5 m), gantries of steel, a control room, gravel bed [est].
- **Kit parts:**
  - Transformer (ground): tank 1.2×0.8×1.5 m with radiator fins and 3 bushings, on a 2×2×0.6 m plinth [est].
  - Cage: 4×4 m, 2.1–2.4 m tall, block lower 1 m and mesh above, steel door; or all mesh [est].
  - Feeder pillar: 1.2×0.4×1.4 m steel cabinet next to it [est].
  - Pole-mounted (H-pole) variant from research-street 2.2.
  - Number/nickname: hand-painted on the cage in 0.3 m letters.
  - Substation: wall 2.4 m with razor wire; 2–3 large transformers; steel gantry 8 m; control room 6×10 m [est].
- **Colours [est]:** tank grey #8C8F8A; cage block painted community colours (e.g. green #3A7D44 or white) with black tar stains; rust #7A4A2A; danger yellow #F4C20D with black bolt.
- **Variants:** caged ground transformer 55%, H-pole 35%, substation 10% [est]. States: working (hum), vandalised (cables cut, door hanging), burnt (scorch marks, oil stain), new replacement (bright tank, donated by a politician with a plaque or banner).
- **Props and life:** a vigilante's chair, a bench where men gather (under the transformer's shade, despite the risk), a generator next to it at a market. Night: if working, the street has lights; if not, the area is dark except generators.
- **SELECT BY:** junctions and estate entrances; one per 2–6 streets; substations on main roads at district edges; a nicknamed transformer is a landmark at a junction.
