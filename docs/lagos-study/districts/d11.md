## 1. What the district is, and what it is not

- Members: Ijora, Ijora Badia (both pieces), Iganmu, Costain, Iponri. Borders: the railway (east), the Eko Bridge
  approach and Western Avenue [EC]. NAMES.md: Costa, "Cos-tain to Cos-ta (the old Gidi Costa Circle, after the Costain
  roundabout)".
- **Size**: the frame ring is one polygon, 87 points, 0.665 km² in game (5.98 km² real at 1:3), bbox x 12725-13808,
  y 6675-8242 [OB]. LIVING measures the real twin at 6.0 km², about 100,600 people (16,800/km²), 6,700 buildings with a
  mean footprint of 203 m² and **37.2% of roof area in buildings over 1,000 m²**; class D, "poor and packed" for
  Ijora-Badia [LIV]. Overture lists 44 churches to 0 mosques [LIV], but see section 4: mosques exist; Overture files
  some under "religious_organization".
- **Land use inside the ring** [OV]: industrial 27% of the area, residential 23%, railway 4%, grass (the theatre
  grounds) 1.7%, retail 0.9% (Iponri Market), small brownfield, farmland and commercial pieces 2%; the rest has no
  land-use polygon (roads, ramps, unmapped ground).
- **Truth check 1: Ijora is mostly not in it.** The ring's south edge is the Ijora Causeway line (`borders.csv`: Apapa
  GRA north-east | Ijora Badia, main road, Ijora Causeway and Apapa Road). The living heart of Ijora falls south of it,
  in d10 Apàpọ̀'s frame land: the OSM "Ijora" suburb point (6.4667, 3.3678; game 13381, 8172), the Ijora Badia Market,
  the Ijora Central Mosque, Ijora Oloye's school and health centre, and the Seven-Up plant (6.4678, 3.3642) [OSM][OV].
  What d11 holds at Ijora is the bridge knot, container depots and one residential pocket by the Eko Bridge foot
  (about x 13506-13630, y 8094-8222) [OV]. EARTH-CHECK row 8 sent "Ijora Badia goes north with Ijora" (corrected on check: was quoted as "Ijora Badia north with Ijora") [EC]; the frame ring
  did that by name but cut along the causeway. Whether to move that line is a call for the owner when d10 is drawn.
- **Truth check 2: the north tip is railway land, not Costain.** From about y 6675 to 7280 the ring holds the Ebute
  Metta junction: the standard-gauge Mobolaji Johnson station, the railway hospital, a railway sports ground and a
  "railway" land-use polygon of 29,288 game m² [OSM][OV]. The frame's "Costain" place point (13474, 7194) sits up
  here among the Oke-Ira churches [OB][OV]; the real Costain junction is 420 m further south (13436, 7612) [OSM].
- **Truth check 3: Iponri is split.** The Iponri Housing Estate (Overture point 6.4883, 3.3612) and most of Iponri
  Estate Road fall in d13 Sọ̀rọ̀lérè's ring; Iponri Market, the Iponri bus stop and the estate's high school fall in
  d11 [OV][OSM].
- **Truth check 4: Jaekel House is not here.** Wikipedia's coordinates (6.4890, 3.3783, 17 Federal Road) put it in d16
  [W-JH][D16]; Overture's "Legacy Nigerian Railway Museum" point (6.4946, 3.3762; game 13690, 7143) falls in d11. The
  sources disagree; Wikipedia's address-backed point is the better one (*guess* that Overture's point is misplaced).
- **What it is, then**: the knot where the old mainland meets the bridges and the port road, an industrial belt
  (brewery, depots, the theatre's grounds), a strip of 1950s-70s housing (Iponri, Alaka, Ebute Metta west: Abule-Nla,
  Ijero, Oke-Ira) between Western Avenue and the railway, and the railway junction at the top [OV][OSM][W-SU][LGA].

## 5. What it is like

- **Fabric** (whole real places, from `places.csv`; each spills past the ring) [OB]: Iganmu, "industry", 1,174
  buildings/km², median footprint 83 m², **55% big roofs**, mean height 10.3 m; Ijora,
  "industry", 1,161/km², 53 m²; Ijora Badia, "port & tank farm", 868/km², 62% big roofs; its piece north-east of the
  Blue Line, "industry", 833/km², 73% big roofs; Iponri, "bungalow suburb", 2,685/km², 104 m², 6.2 m; Costain,
  "bungalow suburb", 1,210/km², 68 m², 7.6 m. So: sheds and depots in the south and west, a band of low housing in
  the middle and north between Western Avenue and the railway. [ST] counts "Iganmu-Ijora" as a 2.4 km² industrial
  piece and "Ijora-Otto" as a 1.2 km² port.
- **Houses**: Iponri Housing Estate is two-storey blocks of flats, 15 condemned as distressed in Oct 2024 [NM] (in d13's
  part); "1950s-70s storey buildings and bungalows" for Surulere is unverified [LGA]. Badia's multi-room houses were
  torn down in 2015 and replaced by zinc, tarpaulin and tents [LGA][SDI] (Badia's exact place not found).
- **People**: Ijora began as a swampy village reached by canoe from the Island; it grew with the Iddo terminus, the
  1919 coal wharf, the 1923 power station and the 1960s industrial zoning [W-IJ]. Many of Badia's first residents were
  resettled from Oluwole Village when it was taken for the National Theatre [W-IJ]. Port and factory workers [LIV][LGA].
  The landowning ruler is the Ojora of Ijora; his family carried out the 2015 Badia demolitions [LGA][SDI]. Careful:
  the residents are never the villains [LIV].
- **Trades** [OV][W-IJ][W-NB][S-7UP]: brewing (Nigerian Breweries since 1949); bottling (Seven-Up at Ijora since 1
  Oct 1960, just over the d10 line); container depots and logistics (Sifax, Lilypond, many "logistics" listings);
  tyres and car dealers on the causeway; Iponri's household-goods market; event centres, hotels and churches in the
  housing band (Overture's most common listings: 24 churches, 22 religious organisations, 16 professional services,
  15 clothing shops, 10 schools, 10 hotels in the ring [OV]).
- **By hour**: trucks and tankers for the port at all hours (the Apapa corridor's traffic [LGA]); a tanker rolled back

## 6. What makes it unlike its neighbours

- **Big roofs**: 37.2% of roof area in buildings over 1,000 m², against 8.4% in Ìbùdó Mẹ́ta, 8.9% in Sọ̀rọ̀lérè and
  2.4% in Agunda [LIV]. It is the sheds-and-depots district among packed housing.
- **Stacked roads**: Costain is where Western Avenue, Eko Bridge and Apapa Road cross with pieces at OSM layer 2, two
  decks up [OSM]; the old mainland's "knot to the bridges" [ST]. Ìbùdó Mẹ́ta's Iddo knot is the Island bridges'
  landing; Costa's is the port road's and the Surulere avenue's.
- **Three railways**: the old narrow-gauge line on the east edge, the standard gauge with its big terminus at the top,
  the Blue Line on its viaduct at the bottom [OV][W-BL][S-MJ]. No neighbour has all three.
- **A national monument in a truck district**: the cap-shaped National Theatre in its grass grounds, ringed by the
  expressway, the Blue Line and depots [W-NT][OV].
- Against Sọ̀rọ̀lérè (west): Sọ̀rọ̀lérè is streets of houses and the stadium; Costa is the industrial edge Surulere's
  page names (Iganmu, Iponri) [W-SU]. Against Ìbùdó Mẹ́ta (east): the railway is the line [EC]; the old planned grid
  is over there, not here. Against Apàpọ̀ (south): the causeway line; real Ijora's houses are on that side.
