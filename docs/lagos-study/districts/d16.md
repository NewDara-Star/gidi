## 1. What the district is, and what it is not

- Members: Ebute Metta (both pieces: west of and east of Murtala Muhammed Way), Oyingbo (both: Oyingbo and Oyingbo
  north), Otto-Iddo. Borders: the railway west; the lagoon east; Iddo and the Carter Bridge head south; Yaba north [EC].
- **Iponri is not in it.** `districts.csv` groups "Otto-Iddo / Oyingbo / Iponri" (row 56 at threshold 65, 47 at 60,
  34 at 55: the CSV's own numbering, not NAMES.md's), but the 51-district list puts Iponri in district 11
  (Ijora-Iganmu-Costain) [EC]. NOW.md names no threshold; the EARTH-CHECK list is the one the drawn districts follow.
- The name means "the three harbours": Iddo, Otto and Oyingbo [W-EM]. Gidi's name Ìbùdó Mẹ́ta, "three camps, three
  stations" (NAMES.md), fits it.
- **It is small.** The frame outline is 0.48 km² in game (the real 4.3 km² at 1:3) [OB][LIV], bbox x 13558-14475,
  y 7175-8225, 71 points. Smaller than Lagoon University's outline; about the size of Makoro.
- **Truth check on its working picture:** the LIVING sheet's headline trade, the Oko-Baba sawmill, has **left**. The
  state moved the sawmillers to Timberville, Agbowa-Ikosi (204 band-saw sheds, 260 flats there); the old Ebute Metta
  site is to be "regenerated" [TRIB, 26 May 2025]. A commentary seen in search snippets also lists Oko Baba demolitions
  in September 2024 and Otto demolitions in March 2024 (snippet only; not read in full). The Otumara waterfront
  settlement was demolished on 7 and 10 March 2025, "over 500 houses" [GUA-H]. So today's real waterfront is cleared
  ground, rubble and what is left, not a working sawmill. Whether Gidi keeps the sawmill (LIVING's `saw_timber`
  activity) is a creative call for the owner: Gidi is its own city, but the note must say the real one is gone.

## 3. Main roads (from the frame and OSM; widths mostly not known)

Frame-study big roads inside the outline, game metres of line [OB]: rail 2,906; Eko Bridge 809 (motorway, bridge);
Third Mainland Bridge 754 (motorway, bridge); Iddo Road 817 (trunk); Clinic Road 529 (primary); Murtala Muhammed Way
399+36; Herbert Macaulay Way 293; Carter Bridge 257+169+18; Ijora Causeway 213+93; Oyingbo Street 152; Wright Street
81; plus about 1,330 m of unnamed motorway and trunk (the flyover ramps). 11.5 km in all.

- **Iddo Road**: trunk, ref A5, one-way pieces (so a split carriageway), asphalt [OSM]. Runs down to Iddo and Carter
  Bridge.
- **Herbert Macaulay Way**: trunk, ref F266, one-way pieces, paved [OSM]; runs north through Yaba. Third Mainland
  Bridge has a link midway to it [W-TMB].
- **Oyingbo Street**: trunk F266, one-way pieces, tagged unpaved [OSM] (a trunk tagged unpaved is likely stale;
  *guess*).
- **Murtala Muhammed Way**: trunk [OB]; the old Clifford Street [W-EM] (Vanguard says Denton Street [VAN]: the
  sources disagree). Lagos Terminus stands on it [W-LT].
- **Clinic Road (Lagos Mainland)**: primary, one-way pieces, maxspeed 80, with bridge pieces at about 6.4923, 3.3877
  [OSM]; on the seam with Makoro (`borders.csv`: Oyingbo north | Makoko, main road 1.05 km).
- **Carter Bridge**: built 1901, rebuilt late 1970s, starts at Iddo and lands in Idumota; the Alaka-Ijora flyover at
  the Iddo end, 1973; commercial vehicles banned from it since 2006 [W-CB]. Lane count not found.
- **Third Mainland Bridge**: 11.8 km, eight lanes, Oworonshoki to Adeniji Adele on the Island; its first phase was
  designed to end at Ebute Metta [W-TMB]. In the frame it runs along d16's water edge.
- **Eko Bridge and Ijora Causeway**: on the south-west seam with d11 (`borders.csv`: Ijora | Otto-Iddo, Eko Bridge and
  Ijora Causeway) [OB]. Ebute Metta is "a main route to Lagos Island, Ikoyi and VI via flyovers across the Iddo
  peninsula" [W-EM]: **the Iddo end is a knot of flyovers and ramps**, the thing that most sets the south apart.
- **Herbert Macaulay *Street*** (residential, near Oyingbo, 6.481-6.483 N) is a different road from Herbert Macaulay
  *Way*: the old King George V Street of the Ago Egba grid [W-EM][OSM]. Do not merge them when drawing.
- **Inner streets** [OSM]: Borno Way (unclassified, partly one-way; per [VAN] the old Griffith Street, yet OSM still has
  a separate Griffith Street near Otto: unresolved), Glover Street (paved), Coates Street, Osholake, Bola, Freeman,
  Denton, Oloto, Griffith; in the Oko-Baba quarter Oko-Baba Street, Ondo Street, Jebba Street, Kano Street, Abeokuta
  Street, Tapa Street. Most are tagged unpaved [OSM] (*guess* that many are worn tar, not earth: check in Street View).
- The Ago Egba grant ran "from Oyingbo (Coates Street) to just before the Yaba lands (Glover Street)" [W-EM]: Coates
  Street at the south, Glover Street at the north mark the old Egba town.
- Widths: OSM has no lanes or width tags on any of these [OSM]. Not found; to be read on Street View/Earth.

## 5. What it is like

- **Fabric** [OB]: Ebute Metta west, bungalow suburb, 1,790 buildings/km², median footprint 94 m², mean height 6.2 m;
  Ebute Metta east of Murtala Muhammed Way, **planned grid** (alignment 0.898), 2,520/km², 139 m²; Oyingbo, 2,486/km²,
  68 m², 6.5 m; Oyingbo north, waterfront, 2,755/km², 98 m²; Otto-Iddo, 2,971/km², 66 m², grid 0.379 (organic). So: a
  grid of bigger old houses in the middle, small packed houses at Otto-Iddo and Oyingbo, the waterfront at the north-east.
  [ST] reads the Ebute Metta / Oyingbo / Yaba railway grid as 2.4 km², grid 0.83.
- **Houses**: many colonial-era houses in "Brazilian" style [W-EM]; colonial railway bungalows and rail houses being
  turned into studio flats [LIV][LGA]; the area is called gentrifying, "the Brooklyn of Lagos" in one article [LGA].
- **People**: Egba Christian founders (Ago Egba, 1867), then railway families (engineers, stationmasters) [W-EM];
  the landowner and ruler is the Oloto of Otto [W-EM][LGA]. Careful: never a crime front [LIV]. Class C, "getting by"
  [LIV]. Okada banned (Lagos Mainland, since 1 June 2022) [LIV].
- **Trades**: food and cloth [W-EM]; the market (food, meat, herbs) [W-OM]; railway and office work [W-EM]; timber
  until 2025 [TRIB].
- **By hour** [LIV][LGA][W-OM]: the Oyingbo market morning; the Red Line from 6 a.m. [LGA]; the bus terminal and
  the flyover traffic to the Island at both rush hours (*guess* from the general day in [LIV]); Sunday church traffic
  round the old Anglican and Catholic churches (*guess*, from [LIV]'s general week). Sleeping in the market banned
  from January 2026 to curb crime [W-OM].
- **Water**: lagoon east and south; Ebute Metta Creek in the south-west towards Ijora [OSM]; the three old harbours of
  the name [W-EM]; the old ferry jetties disused [OSM].

## 6. What makes it unlike its neighbours

- Against Saba (north): Saba is the student and market town; Ìbùdó Mẹ́ta is the old rail town, with the railway
  compound, the terminus and the Red Line's end [W-EM][W-LT][KK].
- Against Makoro (north-east): Makoro is stilts on water; Ìbùdó Mẹ́ta is land, with the waterfront as sawmill ground
  (now cleared) [TRIB][EC].
- Against Costa (west, d11): the railway is the line [EC]; Costa is the port-and-causeway side.
- Its own: **every island bridge lands here**, so the south is flyovers, ramps and the Carter Bridge head over the old
  Iddo town [W-EM][W-CB][ST]; the oldest planned grid on the mainland [W-EM][OB].

8. Otto, along Oloto Street and Denton Street: the packed small houses by Iddo Road.
9. Herbert Macaulay Way at the Oyingbo end: the trunk road's width (two carriageways?) for the road record.
