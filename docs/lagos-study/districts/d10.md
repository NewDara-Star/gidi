## 1. What the district is, and what it is not

- Members by the plan: Apapa GRA (all pieces) and Apapa Wharf (all pieces); borders: the west canal, the harbour,
  Ijora to the north [EC]. Tin Can "goes with Kirikiri as the western port"; Ijora Badia "goes north with Ijora"
  (d11) [EC row 8].
- **The frame outline holds more than that.** Of its six place pieces [OB], two are not Apapa's GRA or Wharf at all,
  read from what lies inside them [OV][OSM]:
  - "Apapa GRA, south-west of Apapa-Oworonshoki Expressway" (12641, 9384) is **Tin Can Island port's east end**: the
    TICT container terminal (12638, 9342), "Tin-Can berth No.9" (12403, 9540), Overture's "Tincan Island" industrial land
    (186,000 m² of it inside d10) and the Tin Can Island interchange [OV][OSM]. So the frame **cuts Tin Can port in two**:
    its west end and first gate landing (12482, 9328) are in d08, its east quays in d10.
  - "Apapa Wharf south-east" (13739, 9642), the outline's separate south piece across the water, is **an island
    village, not wharf**: Ilado village (13816, 9438) and the Sagbokoji public landing (13719, 9422) [OSM]; 4,597 small
    buildings, median 42 m², 4.2 m high, grid 0.343 (organic) [OB]. Sagbokoji is a riverine fishing community reached
    by boat, opposite Tin Can, with no piped water or grid power [S-SAG]. Snake Island proper (Nigerdock, a cluster of ten resident
    communities (corrected on check: was "ten other communities"; [W-SI] names neither Sagbokoji nor Ilado)) lies to its south-west and is in d08's frame [W-SI][OSM].
  - "Apapa GRA north-east" (13412, 8399) is not GRA either: it is the **Ijora Oloye / tank depot / navy** belt north of
    the Marine Bridge (port & tank farm fabric [OB]; Western Naval Command HQ, oil depots, Ijora Oloye school and health
    centre, Ijora Central Mosque [OV]). The Ijora suburb point (13382, 8172) is inside d10 [OSM], though [EC] puts
    Ijora in d11. Lilypond container terminal (13235, 8066) falls just outside, in d11 [OV].
- Size: 1.48 km² in game [OB] (13.3 km² real at 1:3; the six pieces' land in places.csv sums to 13.3 km²): three
  polygons, the main one 1.299 km² (bbox x 12075-14392, y 8058-9558, 125 points), the island piece 0.177 km² (x
  13492-13992, y 9375-9925), and a 139 m² sliver at (12475-12492, 9575-9592), likely a frame artefact (*guess*).
- The name: Apapa was a small Awori fishing village; the town grew after the harbour moles of 1906-1913 let ocean
  ships in, and the colonial government gave it over to port and industry in the 1920s [W-AP][W-LPC]. Gidi's Àpapọ̀,
  "everything gathered together" (NAMES.md), fits a port.
- **Truth check on its working picture.** [LIV] grades it "B in the GRA, D in Badia and under the bridges". Badia is
  not in the frame's d10 (Badia East's point (13158, 7954) is in d12's frame [OSM]). The GRA is real but not the
  quiet garden suburb the label suggests: residents left houses empty on streets such as Marine Road, Oduduwa Road and
  Sola Ayo-Vaughan Street [S-GRA]; property prices crashed and the Lebanese restaurants and clubs moved on [TD17];
  tankers queue on Marine Road and Point Road [TD17]. And a good share of the "GRA" is military: Overture lists Ashanti,
  Tamandu, Queen and Tego Barracks, the Army School of Finance and Administration, the Army School of Signals and the
  navy base among the houses [OV] (Overture points only, confidence 0.28-0.89 (corrected on check: was 0.28-0.80; the navy base is 0.89): to read on the satellite). The area guide
  says "private security and long-term residents create stability" in the GRA (corrected on check: was 'still calls the
  GRA "relatively livable"', a phrase not on the page) [NHM].

## 3. Main roads (from the frame, Overture and OSM; widths mostly not known)

Frame-study big roads inside the outline, game metres of line [OB]: rail 5,490; Apapa-Oworonshoki Expressway 1,874
(motorway) + 1,431 (primary pieces) + 474 (bridge); unnamed motorway 1,748 and bridge 1,033 (the flyovers and ramps);
Apapa Road 1,149 (motorway) + 1,150 (bridge) + 336 (primary); Wharf Road 900; Malu Road 824; Liverpool Road 481;
Commercial Road 437; Warehouse Road 405; Ijora Causeway 284 + 181 (bridge); about 19.5 km in all. Overture adds
inside the outline 15.4 km of residential and 23.0 km of service road (game metres) [OV]: **service roads outnumber
house streets**, the port's and depots' yards.

- **Apapa-Oworonshoki Expressway** (the Oshodi-Apapa Expressway): built 1975-78 [LGA]; "for most of its portion, a
  six-lane expressway with two one-way service roads" [W-AOE]; [W-AP] calls it "a 10-lane corridor recently
  reconstructed with concrete pavement" (the two agree if each service road has two lanes: *guess*; OSM tags its
  service pieces 2 lanes, one-way [OSM]). The original road had three lanes in the main carriageway and two in the
  service lane each way [S-AOE]. Rebuilt in concrete by Dangote under the tax credit scheme (contractor Hitech), 36.02
  km, inaugurated 26 May 2024 [S-AOE]. Its end borders the port (corrected on check: was "it starts at the port") and it passes Tin Can Island [W-AOE]; its Mile 2-Tin Can
  stretch and the Liverpool roundabouts were long under works [TD21].
- **Apapa Road / Ijora Causeway**: the way in from Ijora and the bridges (the Ijora Bridge "into Apapa": a trip that
  should take five minutes took two hours on a good day, four to five on a bad one [TD17]). Overture marks two
  interchanges on it inside d10: the Neck (13242, 8197) and the Oval (13254-13278, 8476-8537), with Apapa Road's bridge pieces
  from y 8069 to 8675 (corrected on check: was 8130 to 8653) [OV]: **the north of the district is a flyover spine**, like Ìbùdó Mẹ́ta's south.
- **Wharf Road**: primary [OV], the road to the port gates [TD21]; one of the mid-rise office corridors, with Creek
  Road [W-AP]. Rebuilt in concrete by AG Dangote 2018-2019, reported as a four-kilometre dual carriageway, two
  kilometres each way (search summary only [S-WR]; the sources disagree on its length). Lane count not found.
- **Creek Road, Burma Road, Warehouse Road, Kofo Abayomi Avenue, Randle Road**: "tankers and trailers park on them
  indiscriminately" [TD17]; Creek Road has ThisDay's office at no. 35 [W-AP] and the "choking smell of urine" [LGA].
  Burma Road and Warehouse Road are in Overture (unclassified, secondary) [OV]; **Creek Road, Kofo Abayomi Avenue
  and Randle Road carry no name in Overture's segments or Nominatim inside the box** [OV][OSM]: their lines are not
  read yet (one Overture bank branch is "Kofo Abayomi Branch" at (13505, 8959) [OV], a hint only).
- **Liverpool Road, Marine Road, Point Road, Oduduwa Way, Park Lane**: where tankers queue for the tank farms
  [TD17][LGA]. Liverpool Road secondary, Marine Road and Park Lane tertiary, Point Road residential [OV].
- **Malu Road**: secondary, 824 m, 2 lanes one-way in OSM [OB][OSM; lanes not checked], with a bridge piece at (12950, 8597) [OV]; it crosses the d09 seam
  (the canal) at about (12825, 8601) (corrected on check: was "runs along the d09 seam's north end").
- **Commercial Road** secondary 437 m [OV]; **Dockyard Road** unclassified, 2 lanes [OSM].
- **Tin Can Island Port Road**: tertiary, 1,418 m inside d10, the spine of Tin Can's east quays [OV].
- **The GRA's streets** [OV]: Child Avenue, Ladipo Oluwole Road, Itapeju Avenue, North Avenue, Central Avenue,
  Hinderer, Bathurst ("Barthurst" in Overture), Forcados, Oranyan, Dakar, Randle Avenue, Bombay Crescent, Calcutta
  Crescent, Crowther Crescent, Caulcrick, Azare Crescent, Ogedengbe, Bornu Crescent, Idunganran, Lisabi, Obanta,
  Danfodio, Ashanti, Takoradi, Seaplane Road, Rhodes Crescent. Most tagged unpaved [OV] (*guess*, as in d16: worn tar
  more likely than earth; check in Street View).
- **Bridges** [LGA][OV]: Ijora Bridge, Marine (Marine Beach) Bridge, Liverpool Bridge, Coconut Bridge, the
  Apapa-Ijora-Costain bridge and Eko Bridge are named in [LGA]. The Liverpool Bridge links Tin Can and Apapa ports,
  carries at least 3,000 trucks a day, is over 30 years old, and drivers park on it [SP-LB]. The Marine Beach Bridge has
  an Apapa-bound and an Ijora-bound side; a fire, which the minister blamed on trading under it, damaged it; repaired 2012, 2017 and
  2020-21 [SP-MB].
- **Overture also has "Snake Island - Victoria Island Tunnel" (351 m) and "Apapa Tunnel Bypass"** as class unknown
  [OV]: no source read says either exists; treat as proposals, not to draw (*guess*).
- Widths: Overture has no width rules on any segment inside d10 [OV]; OSM lane tags only on the expressway's
  service pieces, Malu Road and Dockyard Road (2 each) [OSM]. Everything else to be read on Street View/Earth.

## 5. What it is like

- **Fabric** [OB] (the six pieces, places.csv): Apapa GRA, bungalow suburb, 5.08 km² real, 1,742 buildings/km²,
  median footprint 72 m², mean height 6.3 m, 8% four storeys and up; GRA north-east (the Ijora/depot belt), port &
  tank farm, 1,340/km², 106 m², 8.6 m; Apapa Wharf, port & tank farm, 1,208/km², **139 m²**, 7.4 m, and the highest
  places density of the six (110/km²); Wharf east of the expressway, port & tank farm, 740/km², 95 m², **13.7 m mean,
  55% four storeys and up, 58% big roofs** (the quays' sheds, silos and towers); Tin Can's east end, 944/km², 12.1 m,
  47% four storeys and up; the island piece, waterfront, 5,603/km², 42 m², 4.2 m. So: **big roofs and tall sheds on
  the water, a low leafy grid inland, and one packed island village across the channel.**
- **Land** [OV]: industrial 690,000 m² of the 1.48 km² in game (47%), residential 488,000 m² (33%), garages and yards
  60,000 m² (the truck yards at Tin Can (12749, 9397) and (12375, 9467)), railway 14,000 m².
- **Houses**: the GRA was laid out on Garden City lines: a grid, wide tree-lined avenues, large plots; colonial
  bungalows with high ceilings and deep verandas, Tropical Modernist villas, newer flats [W-AP]; executive housing on
  Marine Road [W-AP]. A 1,000-acre industrial and housing estate was built 1950-58 [W-AP]. Detached and
  semi-detached houses, low-rise flats and converted houses today [NHM].
- **People**: 217,362 in the LGA in 2006 [W-AP]; Awori first, then port workers and migrants [W-AP][LGA]; maritime
  executives in the GRA [LGA]; on the island, Egun (sources also say Awori) fishing families: men fish, women smoke the
  fish [S-SAG].
- **Trades**: the two ports, Customs and clearing agents, shipping, insurance and logistics offices, tank farms,
  flour and grain mills [W-AP][W-LPC][LGA]; 86 of Overture's 648 places inside d10 are transport and storage firms,
  14 more shipping or delivery, 10 fuel stations [OV]. Informal: roadside food, mechanics, touts, "traffic managers" [LGA],
  and the motor boys who sleep in the trucks [NM-MB] (corrected on check: the motor boys are not in [LGA]).

## 6. What makes it unlike its neighbours

- Against Ayégúnlẹ̀ (west, across the canal): Ayégúnlẹ̀ is the densest tenement grid (50k/km² [LIV]); Àpapọ̀ is
  quays, depots and a low garden grid: the GRA has 1,742 buildings/km² to Ajegunle's 2,976, and its houses are smaller
  (72 m² median to 127 m²) [OB]. The canal and its landings are the line
  [EC][OSM].
- Against Kẹ́rẹ́kẹ́rẹ́-Tin Pan (south-west): both are port; the frame splits Tin Can's quays between them [OV].
  Kẹ́rẹ́kẹ́rẹ́ has the creek villages and the prison [LIV]; Àpapọ̀ has the old Wharf and the GRA.
- Against Costa and Agunda (north): the Ijora flyovers and the railway mark the line [OB].
- Its own: **the trucks**, parked on every shoulder and bridge [TD17][TD21]; **the GRA's avenues behind the trailers**
  [W-AP][S-GRA]; **the sheds and silos on the water** [OB][W-LPC]; the Arabian-style amusement park [W-AAP].
