## What the precedents show [F]

- **PlayStation Home** (2008-2015): 41 million members at closure; avatars, apartments, clubs, mini-games, events.
  Critics: "nothing... other than creating an avatar and hanging out", customisation behind a paywall.
- **Meta Horizon Worlds** (2022): most visitors did not come back after the first month; 9% of the worlds people built
  were ever visited by 50 people. Meta's own words: "An empty world is a sad world".
- **Second Life**: concurrency about halved by 2010; everyone had to be online at once, and nobody was sure whether it
  was a game or a social network (FourWeekMBA is a weak source).
- **FC 27's The Grounds**: up to 100 in a hub with three districts; walk up to a pitch to join 1v1 to 5v5; others
  watch. The owner's observation (not verified): small, invisible walls, water you can't enter.
- **Sea of Thieves**: built for "players creating stories together"; a common enemy halved fights between ships;
  players asked for "more things to do in between".
- **Animal Crossing in lockdown** (Frontiers 2021, n=1,053): having visitors on your own island went with lower
  loneliness (β -0.14, p<0.001); visiting others did not (the abstract implies it did).
- **Brookhaven RP** (Roblox): the most-visited Roblox experience, no missions, players pick roles and make stories.
- **FiveM roleplay**: the big servers rest on whitelists, strict staff and streamers.
- **GTA Online**: griefing handled with passive mode, invite-only sessions, Bad Sport lobbies.
- **VRChat**: strangers need trust ranks, shields, a block that hides both sides, mute, reports.
- **Lagos Life** (launched 1 Oct 2026): 1.2 to 9.3 million players claimed (unaudited); a cash exploit, sold
  "Game Naira", real-money deals, a thin privacy policy. The owner: 30 minutes for one conversation, then nothing to do.
- **Small teams and multiplayer**: Dan Marshall (Gun Monkeys): "My advice remains: don't"; Disintegration's servers
  shut 5 months after launch; Ballistic Overkill ended servers over update and anti-cheat costs.
- **Tools**: Epic Online Services is free (lobbies, sessions, voice, cross-platform login, PlayStation included);
  PS5 has party voice chat with reporting built in.
- **The owner's games are documented**: police and thief, the biro game, mineral cork ball, table soccer with biro
  covers and buttons, suwe, boju-boju (Culture Custodian, BellaNaija).

## Lessons [D]

1. Friends bring friends; strangers bring a moderation team. Start friends-only, voice through the PS5 or Steam party.
2. Never empty, even with two people: the city's own life fills the street.
3. Hanging out needs something to do with your hands: the talk happens during Ludo, not instead of it.
4. Short rounds of 5-15 minutes, a short walk or ride apart.
5. Your own place: hosting friends is what went with less loneliness.
6. Never paywall the self: no trading, nothing that touches real money.

## The hour [D]

**With friends:**
- 0-5 min: meet at the base (the host's compound, or "the joint": a beer parlour or buka with a table, radio and
  suya). Early arrivals play Ludo or Whot! while the rest log on.
- 5-10 min: the chalkboard shows what's on (the games, plus one city event: match day, an owambe, rain).
- 10-50 min: three or four rounds (five-a-side with captains picking, police and thief, a keke race). The danfo ride
  between them is play too: one friend as conductor, maybe a Lagos moment on the way.
- 50-60 min: back at the suya spot; the chalkboard tallies the day's scores and rivalries; "same time tomorrow?"

**Alone:** the story films; 10-minute hustles (conductor, okada, hawking) paid in standing, not tradeable things;
friends' ghost times and chalkboard scores to beat, notes left on the wall; free roam; drop-in when a friend logs on.

## Activities ranked by connection per build cost [D]

S = days to 2 weeks, M = weeks to 2 months, L = months (a team of one). What the map must hold follows each.

1. Table games (Ludo, Whot!, Ayò, draughts, chess). S each. Tables at joints, verandas, compounds, offices, schools.
2. The biro game. S-M. Desks: classrooms, offices, cyber cafés.
3. Police and thief. S-M. Blocks with walk-through alleys and compounds, a "station" spot; every block a loop.
4. Captains picking (the ritual). S. At every pitch.
5. Mineral cork ball / button football. S-M (the biro game's flick code). Tables, chalked ground.
6. The joint (hanging out). S-M. Beer parlour, buka and suya interiors with seats, one per district at least.
7. Five-a-side, arcade. L+. Pitches, school fields, Sunday street football, sideline spots.
8. Danfo crew: one drives, one conducts. M. Routes and named stops.
9. Table tennis. L. Tables at schools, estates, halls.
10. Keke/okada races and free driving. M. Race lines on real roads; the okada-ban map applies.
11. Your room in the compound. M. Player-home rooms.
12. Owambe: dance emotes, spraying play money, aso-ebi. M. Party streets and halls, an events calendar.
13. Viewing centre: replays of the group's own matches or a made-up Gidi league. S-M. Viewing-centre interiors.
14. Hustles together. M. Markets, mechanic yards, generator spots.
15. Water: canoe races between Makoro's stilt houses, a ferry ride, fishing off a jetty. M. Water you can enter.

Betting on friends' matches raises age-rating questions about simulated gambling: not researched, not proposed.

## Lagos moments in a shared world [D]

- The city makes the moment, never a player (a bottle bursts on the bus; "everybody come down"; a fight over ₦50
  change), so moments can't be used to grief.
- The host's game picks the moment, so the whole group sees the same thing.
- Players can join in (emotes, "abeg leave am", taking a side) but never lose progress, items or a result.
- About 2-4 an hour, none during a match.
- Laughing with Lagos, not at its people: sacred things are never jokes, real tragedies stay fictional, no mocking a
  tribe, a faith or someone being poor.

## What one person must not attempt early [D]

Public matchmaking; open voice with strangers; more than about 8 per session or dedicated servers (a listen server
hosted by one friend instead [F: Unreal docs]); real money, trading or a player economy; ranked play (it needs
anti-cheat); user-made content; story missions in co-op; and the city's own life must work first.

## The pilot [D]

Table games only send turns, which suits a listen server; physics sports need ball physics, AI and netcode that
agrees on both machines.

| Candidate | Players | Cost | Connection | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ludo | 2-4 | S (1-2 weeks) | High: luck lets anyone win, so it's all banter | Simplest to network and to watch |
| Whot! | 2-6 | S-M (2-3 weeks) | Very high: the most Nigerian | Suits called ball, angle, cross, carpet, star; house rules vary [F: Wikipedia, Pagat]; the host deals |
| Ayò | 2 | S | Medium | 2x6 pits, 48 seeds; capture rules vary, players pick first |
| Draughts | 2 | S | Medium | Same code as Ayò |
| Chess | 2 plus a crowd | S | Medium for the two, high for the crowd | See below |
| Biro game | 2 | S-M | High | Physics, worked out by the host each flick |
| Cork / button football | 2-4 | M | High | The biro game's flick code |
| Table tennis | 2 | L | High | Real-time physics and sync |
| Five-a-side | 4-10 | L+ | Highest | See football below |

**Pick:** Ludo first, Whot! straight after, at one table in the joint. The table, seats, turns and dice serve every
table game: all five for about a month.

**The first slice, "Friday night at the joint":** the owner, their brother and a friend online on PC from different
places; one block of Mùṣá with the joint (Ludo, then Whot!), the city's life around them, one danfo ride to a second
spot. It passes if they stay 60 minutes unprompted, ask to play again within the week, and talk afterwards about a
game or a moment, not a bug.

**Chess** [F + D]: street chess is a spectator game (Washington Square Park's ring of tables; an ethnography found the
scene "attracts caring individuals"). Two play, the crowd argues, the winner stays on. In Nigeria: Tunde Onakoya
founded Chess in Slums Africa in 2018 (Majidun, then Makoko and Oshodi); in April 2024 he played 60 hours in Times
Square for the Guinness record. No source counts players nationally. Makoro is Makoko's echo: a children's chess
club on a stilt veranda is a place the map can hold, Gidi's own invented club, never the real organisation or its
people, never pity.

## Football: Gameplay Football [F + D]

- Licences [F, read again by Claude]: Gameplay Football and its engine Blunted2, Unlicense (public domain); the
  vi3itor fork, Apache-2.0 (159 stars, last push 16 Jan 2024); Google Research Football, Apache-2.0, archived.
- **The art is the open question** [F]: the fork's `data/media` holds about 350 animation files, 43 player models,
  21 stadiums. A 2016 forum post says the data licence was unclear; the author's asset host returns 403. Nothing
  shows the author licensed the art.
- The code [F]: about 2.3 MB of C++ in 392 files on its own engine (SDL2, OpenGL, Boost, OpenAL); on the Mac it
  compiles but does not run. The author: use it "as inspiration... a badly tested and undocumented engine just slows
  down development".
- Reuse [D, estimates, untested]: it can't drop into Unreal; the match engine would be ported; the feel lives in the
  animations, usable only once their licence is cleared; online sync is new work. Months for a team of one. The
  cheaper use: read it, build Gidi's 2v2 arcade football from scratch.
- Before anything: ask the author about the data licence; run the fork on a PC; the owner judges the feel by eye.
  Downloads need the owner's OK.

## Your own face [F]

From UE 5.6, MetaHuman Creator, Mesh to MetaHuman and Animator are part of Unreal (gidi-game is on 5.8.3). A likeness
from three or more video frames plus iPhone 12+ depth (Live Link Face); the same phone drives facial animation. Free
under $1M revenue (Epic's licence page itself not reached: read it before relying on this). The brother's and
friend's faces need written consent.

## Online now, PS5 later [F + D]

A listen server plus free EOS lobbies and voice lets friends test on PC now; EOS crossplay has worked on PlayStation
since 2023. Steam Playtest is free. PS5: apply at partners.playstation.net (a legal entity; in 2022 EU sole traders
were accepted) with a project plan and ideally a working build; in 2022 new developers got dev and test kits on free
loan (current terms not reached). The friends' PC pilot is the playable piece the "Consoles later" decision waits for.
