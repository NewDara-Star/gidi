# 2. Union

*Claude's draft, 15 Oct 2026. It keeps the owner's Act 1 (the steal, the boss's rescue, the okada on hire purchase, the robbery), then gives the city its own clock: an election.*

## The idea
Every park, bus stop and ban line in Gidi is run by the Motor Park Union. Whoever chairs it collects from every danfo, every okada and every lorry: hundreds of collection points, every day. This year the chair is up for election. Two people want it, and both of them need a boy who can ride anywhere and whom nobody notices: Tunde.

The debt is still there. But the story isn't just "pay the boss". It's the whole city deciding who it pays, and Tunde carrying the envelopes.

## The people

**Driving the plot**
- **Tunde Ajayi, 19.** As in Act 1. In this story his gift is that he remembers every face and every route. Both sides find that useful, and dangerous.
- **Chairman Lateef Ajala.** Runs the Hub's motor park, Tunde's boss and lender, and the union chair for nine years. He wins by being loved and by paying.
  - *Voice:* "Election is not war. Election is shopping."
- **Alhaja Sikirat Bakare, "Iya Lorry."** She runs the Food Gate lorry park and the night market. She started as a pepper seller and owns forty lorries now. She's harder than Chairman and more honest about it.
  - *Wants:* the union chair, so that Food Gate's lorries stop paying the Hub's boys.
  - *Voice:* "Ajala gives you food so you will forget he took your plate."
- **Ify Okafor, a radio reporter.** She runs a late-night show on a small station in Saba. She's chasing the union story, and Tunde becomes her source.
  - *Her show is the game's radio.* What Tunde does turns up on air the next night, so the city talks about him without knowing his name.

**Giving it colour**
- **Small,** 17, a rider. In this story he's the riders' voice: loud and fearless. Late in the game he stands as the riders' own candidate.
- **Sergeant (retired),** an old soldier who keeps order at the Hub for Chairman. Polite, tired, and loyal until he isn't.
- **The agbero at the Long Bridge foot,** who collects from everything that moves. Whoever wins, he stays. He's the city's memory, and he talks to Tunde while taking his money.
- **Mama Bisi,** the Mama Put at Ladi Lane. Her stall is neutral ground where both camps eat.
- **Officer Dauda,** task force. The election is his busiest season: everyone pays him not to see.

## How it plays
**Act 1** is the owner's outline as written. In the background, the posters go up: Ajala's face on every danfo, Iya Lorry's on every lorry. The radio starts counting down: "Forty days to the union election."

**Act 2: The envelopes**
- After the robbery, Tunde is back on the ledger, and Chairman has a new kind of run. Envelopes, not oja: cash for park chairmen, cash for agberos, cash for Officer Dauda's boys. The map fills with the union's network as Tunde rides it, and every park he visits joins his map.
- **Iya Lorry** finds him at Mama Bisi's. She doesn't threaten him; she offers to pay his whole ledger the day she wins. All he has to do is tell her where the envelopes go.
- **The player is free all the way.** Ride for himself, work for one camp, work for both (the most money and the most danger), or ignore the election. Ignoring it is a choice too: the city changes around him either way.
- **Ify** wants proof, and her show plays whatever Tunde brings her. Give her the envelopes and the city turns on Chairman. Give her nothing and it plays his jingles.

**The danfo strike** is the big open-world event, in the middle of Act 2. Iya Lorry's lorries and a third of the danfos stop running to show her strength. For three game days:
- the roads are empty of buses
- everybody wants an okada; fares triple
- the banned zones fill with riders
- the task force can't cope, and Officer Dauda crushes bikes in the open to make a point

It's the richest time to ride and the most dangerous. Small gets caught; what happens next depends on whether Tunde is there.

**The click**, as in the owner's story, but the clue comes from the election. Tunde carries an envelope to the Alpha's back street, and the Alpha opens it in front of him: inside is Tunde's own robbed money, wrapped in the same nylon. Chairman paid for his own boy's robbery with his own boy's money.

**Act 3: The vote**
- **Tunde knows how the money moves now:**
  - Chairman's ledger
  - Iya Lorry's lorries
  - the envelopes
  - Officer Dauda's quota
- **He can back Chairman** and be paid off, the debt cleared and a park of his own. He'd become the next Ajala.
- **He can back Iya Lorry:** a new boss, possibly a fairer one, possibly not.
- **Or he can back Small.** The riders' own candidate has no money, only the boys Tunde has freed and every rider who owes a ledger.

Freeing riders works as in the owner's story. Here, every rider freed is also a vote.

## The ending: election day at the Hub
The vote is held in the old stadium beside the Hub, union members queueing in the heat.
- Chairman's boys at one gate, Iya Lorry's at the other.
- Ify broadcasting live from a danfo roof.
- The count is in a room Tunde knows, under the church calendar where he signed his first ledger.

**How it ends depends on how the player played:**
- **Chairman wins** (Tunde backed him, or did nothing): the city carries on. Tunde has his park, and a hungry boy comes to his office.
- **Iya Lorry wins:** the posters change, the agbero at the bridge collects for her now, and Tunde's ledger is "transferred". The player learns the system was never one man.
- **Small wins** (enough riders freed, Ify given the truth, the Sergeant turned): the boss's boys and Iya Lorry's boys face each other outside the stadium. Small's riders, Tunde's boys, stand between them. It's the owner's standoff, now about the whole city: nobody swings, the ballot boxes are carried out through a crowd of riders, and Chairman sits in his office with the radio on.

## Why it could bang
- **The city has a clock that isn't the player's.** The election comes whether Tunde is ready or not, and the posters, the radio, the strike and the parks change as it nears. An open world that is going somewhere.
- **There are two bosses and neither is clean,** so choosing is hard.
- **It's very Lagos.** Union politics, agbero tolls, envelopes and a strike that empties the roads are things Lagos players know in their bones, and things outsiders have never seen in a game.
