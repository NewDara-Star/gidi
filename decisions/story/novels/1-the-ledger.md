# 1. The Ledger

*Claude's draft, 15 Oct 2026. It builds on the owner's confirmed story (`../act-1-draft.md`, `../acts-2-3-draft.md`) and adds people.*

## The people

**Driving the plot**
- **Tunde Ajayi, 19.** He sleeps in a kiosk behind Ladi Lane Parts Market and hasn't eaten since yesterday. Quick hands, quick mouth, and too proud to beg until he has to.
  - *Wants:* to eat today, and one day to own one thing nobody can take.
  - *Hides:* he can read. He was in school until his aunt died, and he tells nobody, because a boy who can read gets asked why he's on the street.
- **Chairman Lateef Ajala, "Baba Park."** He runs the Hub's motor park and the okada park beside it. He pays school fees for six children in the area, feeds the boys at Christmas, and stands up when a woman enters the room. Everyone loves him, and everyone owes him.
  - *Wants:* the ledger always full.
  - *Hides:* the ledger is the business. The parks and the oja are just how it fills.
  - *Voice:* "Nobody owes me. Dem dey work for me. Na different thing."
- **The Alpha (Ganiyu).** He runs the boys on the Maze's back streets: charming, funny, generous with cigarettes. He calls Tunde "my guy" from the first meeting.
  - *Hides:* he owes Chairman too, from a long time ago, and every bike his boys take is a payment on it. He isn't the villain. He's the next thing Tunde could become.
  - *Voice:* "This your machine fine o. Take care of am. Lagos no dey forgive."
- **Mama Bisi.** She runs the Mama Put at the mouth of Ladi Lane market: rice, beans, fried plantain, a radio on the shelf. She's the one Tunde steals from in the first minute.
  - *Wants:* her son through university at Saba.
  - *What she gives Tunde,* once she forgives him: food on credit with no interest. She's the opposite of the boss, and the player feels the difference.

**Giving it colour**
- **Wale,** Mama Bisi's son, 20, a Lagoon University student. He's the one who swings at Tunde in the opening. Later he's Tunde's friend, and the one who explains, with a pen on a napkin, what compound interest is.
- **Kells,** Tunde's old friend from Jungle City. He works the internet café on the yahoo route, all chains and jokes. He's the one who says, "How you no know how your bike take go?" That's the click.
- **Small,** 17, the youngest rider at the park. He talks too much, crashes too often and owes more than anyone. He's the first boy Tunde frees.
- **Officer Dauda,** task force. He isn't a cartoon: he has a quota, a sick mother and a pension that never comes. He catches Tunde three times and starts calling him by name. He drinks with Chairman on Fridays.
- **Sister Ronke.** Tunde carries her home from the hotels by the Hub at 3 a.m. She owes a madam the same way Tunde owes Chairman, and she's the first person to tell him so. She never asks for pity; she asks for the fare.
- **The bike man at Railside,** who sells tokunbo bikes and asks no questions. He becomes the place Tunde buys his own freedom, one bike at a time.

## Act 1: Survive (the owner's outline, with the people in it)

Morning in the Maze. The player learns to walk by walking Tunde up Ladi Lane: past the wheelbarrow phone-case man with an earpiece singing along to a song only he can hear, past a fruit seller, past the smell of Mama Bisi's plantain. Tunde turns out his pocket: two coins. A man by the stall waves him off before he says a word.

The steal is one plate, one quick grab. Wale sees him and swings; the punch teaches the player to dodge. Then Mama Bisi's voice: "Ole! OLE!" And the market turns.

The chase runs the length of Ladi Lane: ducking under awnings, vaulting a crate of spark plugs, kicking past the one man brave enough to block the aisle. The crowd grows behind him, and it isn't a game any more. At the far mouth of the market they catch him. Somebody is shouting for a tyre.

Chairman steps out from under his canopy, and the whole crowd stops. He doesn't raise his voice. He puts his hand on Tunde's neck like a father, pays Mama Bisi double for the plate, and walks Tunde out.

*(On a replay, the player notices Chairman was standing at his canopy before the steal, watching.)*

In the motor park office, under a calendar from a church, Chairman listens to Tunde's story: no parents, no work, nothing. He says stealing will get him killed. He says a man should work. Then he gives him an okada on hire purchase, "because I see something in you." The ledger is a small exercise book, and Tunde signs it.

Leaving the park, the Alpha's boys surround him. The Alpha calls them off, laughs, admires the bike, and asks where Tunde sleeps.

**What the player does:** fares, and the open world from here. The first two are safe. Then the banned-zone fares appear, the Capital and Old Island, paying double and triple. The player chooses: the task force, Officer Dauda, fines, bikes crushed (up to three), and the conductor's path if all three go.

**The story comes to the player:**
- Chairman calls every evening for the remittance, warm at first.
- Mama Bisi calls once: "You fit carry my goods tomorrow?" It's the first job that isn't for the boss, and the first time someone pays Tunde fair.
- Sister Ronke's 3 a.m. rides.
- Kells in Jungle City, laughing at Tunde's helmet.

**The end of Act 1:** at 85% paid, Chairman says, "Bring the balance at once and the bike is yours." The player saves cash. One night, in a back street the player knows, the Alpha's boys take the money, the bike and two of Tunde's teeth. The last shot is the Alpha, not looking at him.

## Act 2: Hustle

Tunde goes back to beg. Chairman listens, sighs, and changes. He isn't cruel, just candid. "I can help you. But the way I will help you is that you will transport for me until my money is complete."

The new ledger lives on Tunde's phone, in an app the boss's nephew made. Interest on the old bike. Interest on the new bike. A daily charge when Tunde doesn't pay. The company bike gets handed over each morning and taken back each night: "If you like, make dem steal this one too."

The runs now carry oja: weed in sacks of pepper, on danfos and okadas, to drops all over Gidi. In Food Gate, the lorries. In Water Town, a canoe at a jetty under the Long Bridge. In the Heights, a gatehouse where a security man takes a bag without looking at it. The cargo has weight. One run goes wrong because a boy on another bike is caught, and the player watches what the task force does to him.

The player is free all through Act 2:
- Ride fares for himself; the money goes in his own pocket, not the ledger.
- Bet at the shops (wins and losses both have weight).
- Buy clothes, a better phone, his own tokunbo bike from the man at Railside.
- Never touch the ledger. But the ledger grows, and Chairman's boys start appearing at Tunde's room.

The scenes come between the runs:
- **"Abeg, this money is not going down."** Tunde complains to Chairman. Chairman shows him the book, line by line, and every line is true.
- **Wale's napkin.** Wale works the interest out on a napkin at Mama Bisi's stall: "Tunde. This number will never finish. Never."
- **Small's crash.** Small crashes a company bike, and Chairman adds the bike to Small's ledger in front of everyone.
- **Sister Ronke** tells Tunde about her madam's ledger, and it's his ledger, with different words.

**The click.** In Kells's café in Jungle City, Tunde tells the robbery story for the tenth time. Kells, half laughing: "How you no know how your bike take go? Who know say you dey carry the balance that night? Who you tell?"

The game replays the robbery, and now the player sees what they missed:
- the Alpha at Chairman's canopy in the opening
- the robbed bike, repainted, in the Hub park last week
- Officer Dauda and Chairman laughing on a Friday

**The end of Act 2:** Tunde follows the Alpha one night and watches him hand Chairman an envelope. The Alpha turns and sees him. Neither says anything.

## Act 3: Empire

This is where the owner's heart of the story lives. Tunde knows how he was made, and he can see it being done to every boy at the park.

**The new loop:**
- **His own pocket buys boys out.** Small first: Tunde walks into the office and pays Small's ledger in cash, in front of everyone. Chairman smiles and writes "PAID" with the same pen. It costs him nothing he can see, yet.
- **Every boy freed becomes Tunde's rider.** He runs fares off Tunde's own bikes, from a corner Mama Bisi lets him use by her stall. Each one takes a little income away from Chairman, and the map shows it: Tunde's corner, then two, then a stretch of Ladi Lane.
- **Chairman answers, as a businessman, not a gangster:**
  - he lowers his interest to keep his boys
  - he tells the task force where Tunde's riders park
  - he sends the Alpha

**The Alpha.** The Alpha comes to Tunde's corner alone at night. He tells his own story. He was Small once. He has been paying for twelve years. Taking bikes is the only way his number ever moves. The player chooses:
- buy the Alpha out, the biggest ledger in the game
- fight him
- send him away

Whatever the choice, the Alpha is at the standoff, on one side or the other.

**Other threads the player can take or leave:**
- **The land war in Water Town.** Chairman wants the stilt village cleared for a buyer from the Heights. Tunde's riders know the people there. Helping them costs money and buys loyalty.
- **Officer Dauda.** He can be paid, avoided, or turned. Turned, he tells Tunde where Chairman's oja moves. Tunde doesn't sell it; he lets it be seized. That hits Chairman's other business.
- **Sister Ronke's madam.** A side story: Tunde can buy out a ledger that isn't a rider's. It changes nothing in the war and everything in the story.

## The ending: the standoff
One night, Chairman's boys come for Tunde's corner at Ladi Lane, the same market where it started.

On one side are Chairman and the boys still in debt, with faces the player knows: a rider from a fare, a boy who carried oja with him, the man who blocked the aisle in the opening.

On the other side is Tunde, with exactly the boys he freed. Free three and three stand behind him; free twenty and it's an army.

Before anyone swings, Tunde talks to Chairman's boys. He doesn't make a speech. He holds up his phone with the old ledger on it and reads them his number, then asks one of them for his.

- **If the player played well** (boys freed, riders treated fairly, Mama Bisi's trust), the boys walk away one by one. Chairman is left standing in the market with his exercise book. The defeat is that nobody will fight for him.
- **If the player played badly,** few walk away. It's a real fight with real losses: faces the player recognises, on the ground, in the market where Tunde once nearly died.

**The last image:** morning in Ladi Lane. Mama Bisi frying plantain. A hungry boy at the mouth of the market, counting two coins. On a good playthrough, Tunde gives him a plate. On a dark one, Tunde offers him a bike "on hire purchase."

## Why it could bang
- **The villain is kind.** Chairman never shouts. That makes the betrayal hurt, and it makes him Lagos, not a cartoon.
- **The mechanic is the theme.** The ledger the player watches every day is the trap the story is about. The player feels it before anyone says it.
- **The ending is the player's own game,** visible in one line of people.
