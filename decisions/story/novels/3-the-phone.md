# 3. The Phone

*Claude's draft, 15 Oct 2026. It keeps the owner's Act 1 (the steal, the rescue, the okada on hire purchase), then turns the open world into a thriller: one object that keeps ringing.*

## The idea
Late one night, near the end of Act 1, Tunde carries a young woman from a hotel by the Airport to Saba. She's frightened and won't say why. At her gate she pays double and runs in. Her phone is still in the bike's front pouch.

The next morning it rings. A man's voice: "You carried my property last night. Bring the phone. We know your bike."

By evening, the woman is missing. Her face is on the radio, and Tunde has her phone.

From here the debt is still real, but it's no longer the only thing chasing him.

## The people

**Driving the plot**
- **Tunde Ajayi, 19.** As in Act 1. He took the fare because it paid double. Now he carries something that could get him killed or clear his name, and he keeps riding because the ledger doesn't stop for kidnappings.
- **Adaeze Nwosu, 22, the woman in the fare.** A student at Lagoon University in Saba, and smart. She found out who was using her uncle's account and ran.
  - *She's never a prize in the story.* She's a person who planned her own way out, and the phone is part of her plan.
  - *Voice, in the voice notes Tunde finds on the phone:* "If you're hearing this, I'm not stupid. I'm just late."
- **"Captain."** He runs a kidnap ring out of the creeks behind Water Town: a calm man who pays okada riders, without their knowing, to watch hotels, gates and the Airport road.
  - *Wants:* the phone. It holds the accounts the ransom money moves through.
  - *Voice:* "Everybody in Gidi works for somebody. You just don't know who pays you."
- **Inspector Halima Garba.** A real detective in a station with one working car. Honest and tired. She needs a witness, and she doesn't trust riders.
  - *Wants:* Captain, and for once a case that ends.
  - *What she can give Tunde:* nothing on paper. A favour with the task force, maybe.

**Giving it colour**
- **Chairman Ajala,** Tunde's boss and lender. His okada riders are Captain's watchers, and he knows it; Captain pays the park. He tells Tunde to give the phone back and "forget this thing."
- **Kells,** Tunde's friend at the internet café in Jungle City. He knows how the money on the phone moves, because it's his world, and he's scared of what he sees.
- **Adaeze's younger brother, Chidi, 15.** He stands at the hotel gate by the Airport every evening holding her picture. He becomes the reason the player can't just sell the phone.
- **Mama Bisi.** Her stall at Ladi Lane is where Tunde hides the phone in a pot of beans for a night.
- **Officer Dauda,** task force. He keeps catching Tunde in the banned zones. One night he asks why Tunde keeps riding to Water Town.

## How it plays
**The phone is the quest.** It lives in Tunde's pocket and in the game's phone menu. It rings with calls from Captain's men, Adaeze's mother, and once, from an unknown number, Adaeze herself.
- **Answering is a scene.**
- **Ignoring it is allowed,** but the open world answers back: riders start asking who carried the girl, and a car follows Tunde's bike through the Maze.

**The voice notes are the trail.** Adaeze recorded voice notes as she worked out her uncle's accounts. Each one names a place in Gidi:
- the hotel by the Airport
- a café in Jungle City
- a jetty under the Long Bridge
- a gatehouse in the Heights

The player visits them in any order, on fares or on purpose. Every district is open; the notes give it a meaning.

**Three people want the phone, and each can win:**
- **Sell it to Captain.** It's enough money to clear the ledger in one night. The player is free, and Chidi is still at the gate.
- **Give it to Inspector Garba.** No money, a lot of danger, and the right thing.
- **Give it to Chairman,** who asked nicely. He'll sell it himself, and he'll add the trouble to Tunde's ledger.

**The debt keeps running.** Chairman's evening call still comes. The middle of the game is a real squeeze between remittance and chasing. The player chooses which day goes to which.

## The turns
- **The first turn: Captain's watchers are riders.** Tunde sees a boy from his own park photographing the hotel gate. He's paid by Captain through the park and doesn't know what for. Every rider in Gidi could be watching every other.
- **The second turn: Chairman knows.** Kells traces one of the ransom payments to the Hub park's account. Chairman isn't Captain, but he rents him the city's eyes.
- **The third turn: Adaeze isn't in the creek camp.** She got out of it on the second night and has been hiding at the university, calling from a borrowed phone. She needs Tunde to carry her, and the phone, to Garba without passing a single rider.

That's the run the game has been teaching: every shortcut, every lane, every place the task force doesn't look, across a city where any okada could be watching.

## The ending: the Long Bridge at night
Captain sets a meeting at the middle of the Long Bridge: the phone for Tunde's life. Garba sets up at both ends with her one car and three borrowed officers. Adaeze is on the back of Tunde's bike. Okadas are banned on the bridge, of course.

**How it ends depends on the player's choices:**
- **Played straight** (the phone kept safe, Garba trusted, the riders warned): halfway along the bridge, Tunde's own riders, the boys he warned, block both lanes with their bikes. Captain's car has nowhere to go. It's the owner's standoff, on a bridge over the lagoon, with nobody swinging.
- **The phone sold:** the ledger is clear and Tunde owns his bike. The radio reports a body found in the creek behind Water Town. Chidi stops coming to the gate. The game carries on, and it's quieter.
- **Given to Chairman:** the phone disappears into the Hub, Captain goes free, and Tunde's ledger has a new line, "trouble". The last scene is Chairman laughing on a Friday with Officer Dauda.

## Why it could bang
- **One object makes the whole open world a story.** The phone rings while you ride, the notes send you everywhere, and the city's riders become a network the player can't trust.
- **It's a real Lagos fear.** Kidnapping, watchers and money moving through cafés, told from the seat of the bike that carries everyone, with weight: no glamour, and the victims are people.
- **It uses everything already built:** Saba and the university, the Airport, Water Town's creeks, the Long Bridge, the banned zones and Tunde's debt.

## Mix and match
- Story 1's people, the ledger and the standoff work inside this plot.
- Story 2's election could be the season it happens in.
- The phone could also be a single long side story inside Story 1's Act 2.
