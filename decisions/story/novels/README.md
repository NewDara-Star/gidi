# Three stories for Gidi (Claude's drafts, 15 Oct 2026, for the owner to pick from)

The owner asked for "two or three novels of the story" to read, pick from, and build their own. These are short stories, about 15 minutes' reading each, not full novels. Each one names the people who drive the plot and the side characters who give it colour, and it's told the way it would play in an open world.

None of this is decided. It's material. Take a character from one, a scene from another, throw the rest away.

## How a story lives in an open world (what all three use)
The owner's point: in an open world you can ride for ends on ends, never pay back, and go anywhere; nothing is locked. The task force will chase you in the banned zones, but there's no wall. So the story can't be a corridor. Four rules make it work:

1. **The story comes to you.** You never have to walk to a marker. The boss calls. A rider you know turns up at your fare. Your landlord knocks. If you ignore them, they call again, and then they come.
2. **The clock is the city, not the plot.** If you never pay back, that's a choice the story answers. The interest grows, the boss's boys start "checking on" you, and one day your bike is gone from where you parked it. Riding forever is allowed; it just isn't free.
3. **Every system is a scene.** Fares, the debt, heat, betting and the phone are the story, not breaks from it. A passenger's chatter can be a clue. A betting win can pay a boy's debt. A task force officer who keeps catching you becomes a character.
4. **Places open by meaning, not by locks.** Every district is reachable from minute one. The story gives you a reason to go somewhere, and the place changes once you know what happens there. The Heights are just big houses until you've carried something there for the boss.

## The Lagos feel (all three use it)
- **On the street:**
  - wheelbarrow phone-accessory men with earpieces and cases
  - fruit sellers and Mama Put stalls
  - danfo conductors hanging off the door
- **Places to go in:** betting shops on every corner, hotels by the hour, brothels behind a bar (off screen).
- **Who runs the street:** the agbero at every bus stop, the task force at every ban line.

These are the texture of every mission, not side content.

## Standing rules
- Jungle justice, drugs and kidnapping are shown with weight, never glamorised. Drugs are cargo, never a power-up.
- Churches, mosques, schools, hospitals and clinics are safe ground.
- Invented names, brands and people only. Sex work is off screen, and the women have their own lines and reasons.
- Place names are Gidi's (sound-alikes of the real ones, `docs/lagos-study/roads/NAMES.md`).
