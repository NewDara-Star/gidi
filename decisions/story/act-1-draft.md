# Gidi Act 1: draft (the owner's outline, 15 Oct 2026, with Claude's notes)

**Status:** a draft for the story pass, stage 3 of `gidi-roadmap.md` (not in this repo yet). The owner decides; the notes are suggestions. The flowchart, `gidi-act1-flow.mermaid`, isn't in this repo; the flow below is the same in words.

## The flow (owner, 15 Oct)
1. **The steal and the chase.** Tunde steals at Mama Put's in Ladi Lane Parts Market, and the "Ole!" chase runs down the aisle.
2. **The rescue.** The boss rescues him. In a cutscene the boss explains the hire-purchase deal; Tunde is grateful and gets the okada.
3. **The Alpha.** Leaving the boss's area, street boys harass Tunde. Their leader, the Alpha, calls them off. We see his face. He compliments the bike: he's casing it.
4. **The first two fares.** Safe routes, to learn riding in the city. The world is fully built and lived in, so the visuals and Easter eggs carry these.
5. **Free roam.** Pick fares from the map (pickup, destination, pay). Fares have a timer and a fast-time bonus. A daily remittance goes to the boss.
6. **The tempting banned-zone fares** appear after the first two fares. Take them or not:
   - **Never caught:** big payouts, and the heat rises.
   - **Caught, pay the fine:** the fine comes out of savings; carry on.
   - **Caught, don't pay:** the bike is impounded and crushed. Back to the boss for a new bike, and now Tunde is paying for two. The owner expects the double debt to make players protect bike 2, but plans for players who don't.
   - **Third bike crushed:** no more bikes. Tunde works as a conductor on the boss's danfo, chaperoned by a driver.
   - **Safe fares only:** it pays off, but slowly, so riding itself must be fun.
7. **Spending freely.** Clothes, sports betting, carrying passengers of all kinds, paying for sex (off screen), and more to think about. Each has a direct effect on money, good or bad.
8. **The ending, on every path.** Near the end of the debt, the Alpha's boys rob Tunde and beat him; a cutscene shows the Alpha.
   - **Bike path:** robbed of the money and the bike.
   - **Conductor path:** robbed of the money.
   - Back to the boss: the end of Act 1.
9. **The reveal, at the end of Act 2.** The Alpha is the boss's associate: the boss set the whole thing up. This betrayal, after an act of exhausting work for the boss, drives the rest of Act 2 and Act 3.

## The opening, in detail (owner, 15 Oct, later)
- **It opens close to the market.** Tunde walks up to the market thinking about food. The walk-up teaches the basic controls: movement, running, and so on.
- **The steal.** He steals something; the man he stole from tries to punch or hit him. Punching can be taught in a short mini-game.
- **The chase is a Subway Surfers-style runner.** People shout "Ole! Ole!"; Tunde runs through the market, ducking things and leaning his head aside. If one person blocks the way, he can kick them, taught as a mini-game.
- **The rescue.** At the end of the market, the boss pulls him out and rescues him. He says stealing isn't good, and that without the rescue Tunde could have died. Tunde tells his situation: an orphan, no work, nothing to eat, nothing to fall back on, and this is how he survives. The boss gives him an okada on hire purchase, to pay back. The journey starts there.
- **The owner's doubt:** "I don't know if that story is lovely."

**Claude's suggestions (15 Oct, for the owner):**
- **Show the hunger in the walk-up; don't tell it.** Coins counted in his hand, slowing at food smells, shooed from a stall. Then the orphan speech isn't needed.
- **Make the "Ole!" mob a real threat.** The crowd closes in at the end of the chase; someone fetches a tyre (jungle justice, shown with weight). It sets up "you could have died".
- **Let the rescue show the boss's power.** He steps out and the mob stops dead; nobody argues. Add one hidden hint that he was watching from the start (a look from his canopy, or "I saw you from the start"). It pays off on a replay after the Act 2 reveal: he was recruiting, not saving.
- **Settle who swings:** the draft says Mama Put (a woman), the owner said a man. Option: Mama Put shouts "Ole!" and her husband or son swings.

## Decisions (owner, 15 Oct)
- Cap at 3 bikes.
- Bribes and fines are easy to afford.
- The robbery is Act 1's one ending. The task force crush is a setback, not an ending.
- The robbers are the Alpha's gang. The betrayal is revealed at the end of Act 2.
- Paying for sex is allowed, off screen.

## Notes
### Who and what gets robbed: the lump-sum rule
- The 85% trigger counts money paid to the boss, not cash on hand; otherwise hoarding or spending breaks it.
- At 85%, the boss says "bring the balance at once and the bike is yours", so Tunde saves the final lump sum in cash.
- That cash is exactly what the Alpha's boys take, so the robbery lands on money the player has watched grow.

### The Alpha, layered
- **Act 1:** friendly (he stops his boys and admires the bike), then he robs Tunde. The player suspects the Alpha, not the boss.
- **Act 2:** the reveal that the Alpha works with the boss. Two layers of betrayal, and the first is on screen from minute ten.
- **Clues to plant for replays:**
  - the robbed bike, later, at the boss's motor park
  - the Alpha seen at the boss's post
  - a task force officer drinking with the boss

### Riding must be fun on its own
Safe fares are the slow path, so the ride itself has to carry them:
- **Handling:** a bike that feels good weaving through traffic, and shortcuts through lanes and markets.
- **Passenger chatter:** gossip, stories, jokes, Lagos life. Passengers can also deliver world lore and hints about the boss and the Alpha.
- **Fare variety:** market women with loads, students, a late-night drunk, urgent "go fast" fares, big-man fares in the Capital, oja pickups for the boss.

### Spending (money sinks)
- **Clothes:** fine as is.
- **Sports betting:** very Lagos (betting shops everywhere), and a good money sink. Show its pull with weight, not as a jackpot machine.
- **Passengers of all kinds,** including sex workers at night: realistic for Lagos nightlife and fine in an R-rated game, as passengers with their own lines and dignity.
- **Paying for sex** (owner, 15 Oct): allowed, kept off screen (fade to black or cut to morning, as in Red Dead 2 and Cyberpunk). The rules:
  - Implied only. Explicit content would push the rating from 18 to Adults Only, which most stores and consoles won't sell.
  - A choice with weight. It's money Tunde owes the boss, and the world can react: a friend's comment, the boss's boys noticing a short remittance.
  - The women are characters with their own lines and reasons, like the rest of Gidi's people.
  - Every character in these scenes is clearly an adult.
  - No "pay, then kill her and take the money back" mechanic (the widely criticised GTA trick).

### The banned-zone economy
- **Pay:** banned fares pay about 2–3× a normal fare. Rare "big man" fares in the Capital pay much more and need speed.
- **The safe path must still work:** legal fares alone can clear the debt, just slower.
- **Heat builds up.** Each banned-zone run raises task force attention, and heat starts low for a new rider. The task force itself is never softened.
- **The bribe balance:** affordable, but costing about what the banned fare earned extra. Getting caught means the risk earned nothing.

### The bike cap: 3 bikes
1. **First crush:** a new bike, and the debt grows. "Ehen. Task force. Okay."
2. **Second crush:** a new bike and more debt. The boss gets colder, and his boys start "checking on" Tunde.
3. **Third crush:** no more bikes ("Na you dey sponsor task force?"). Tunde becomes a conductor on the boss's danfo, chaperoned by a driver. Slower and humbling, but playable, and still paying down the debt.

Two more rules:
- **A debt cap:** past a point, each loss costs time and pride, not more money, so the game stays finishable.
- **Reactivity as reward:** the boss's lines escalate, task force officers recognise Tunde, someone at the impound yard greets him by name.

### Pacing
Act 1 is roughly 2–3 hours. The robbery lands at the 85% point.

### Build order
**The vertical slice:** the opening (the steal, the chase, the rescue, the Alpha), the first two safe fares, and about 15 minutes of free-roam fares in the Maze and the Hub. Build and polish that before the rest of Act 1.

## Standing rules that apply
- Jungle justice and the drug trade are shown with weight, never glamorised.
- Churches, mosques, schools, hospitals and clinics are safe ground, never crime locations.
- Invented brands, names and people only.
