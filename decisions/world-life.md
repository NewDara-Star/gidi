# Life in Gidi: what makes the world feel like it exists

The owner's thinking from 16 Oct 2026, and Claude's proposals. **Ideas, not decided**, unless marked as decided.

The owner's examples were examples, not the list:
- a preacher standing up in a bus
- betting
- conductors' calls like "Ketu-Ojota-Mile 12"
- hawkers' shouts
- the radio
- brands
- the currency

The goal is the whole texture of Lagos life.

## Immersion: four layers
1. **Rhythm.** The city runs on a clock, a week, a month and a year, whether the player is there or not.
2. **Systems.** Things go wrong for everyone, and everyone adapts. The player feels it too.
3. **Moments.** Small scenes that start on their own near the player.
4. **Texture.** Words on walls, sound, talk.

## 1. Rhythm
- **Day:**
  - Dawn: the call to prayer, bakeries, the first danfos.
  - Rush hour: go-slow, with hawkers working the traffic.
  - Noon: heat, business.
  - Evening: the rush home, buka smoke.
  - Night: generators, church vigils, clubs, the danger hours.
- **Week:**
  - Friday afternoon: Jumat, streets near mosques fill.
  - Saturday: owambe parties spill onto closed streets, with canopies, aso-ebi and money spraying.
  - Sunday morning: church traffic, Sunday best, quiet markets.
- **Month: Sanitation Saturday.** The roads are empty until 10 am, people sweep, and enforcement is out. Nothing says Lagos like it.
- **Year:**
  - The rainy season: flooded streets, the gutters overflow, okadas wade through.
  - Harmattan: dust haze, cold mornings.
  - December: everyone comes home, parties, prices up, traffic worse.

## 2. Systems that touch everyone
- **"NEPA has taken light."** Power cuts by area and by hour. The street goes dark, the generators roar, a cheer goes up when light comes back. Shops with generators stay open.
- **Fuel scarcity.** Queues at stations, black-market jerrycans, danfo fares go up.
- **Go-slow.** Traffic as a place where people live: hawkers, beggars, preachers, phone sellers, arguments.
- **Checkpoints.** Police "roger", LASTMA-style traffic wardens, task force raids on okadas and street traders, when traders pack up and run.
- **Prices move.** Inflation and scarcity change what things cost: bread, fuel, fares.
- **Network.** Signal drops in some places, and "abeg flash me" calls.

## 3. Moments (they start near the player and play out without them)
- **On the bus:**
  - A preacher stands up: "Praise the Lord! Jesus loves you!"
  - A medicine seller pitches one bottle that cures everything.
  - A fight over change: "conductor, my change!"
- **On the street:**
  - Two drivers quarrel and a crowd gathers to judge.
  - A "thief!" shout and a crowd chase.
  - A borehole driller.
  - Roadside football that stops for cars.
  - A generator repairman with a crowd watching.
  - A church crusade with loudspeakers.
  - A wedding convoy.
  - A burial procession, handled with respect.
  - A street blocked for a party.
- **At the market:** haggling, a load carrier shouting "make way!", a trader who calls Tunde "my customer".

The preacher, the crusade and the call to prayer are life, never jokes or crime. The safe-ground rule still holds: churches, mosques, schools, hospitals and clinics are never crime sites.

## 4. Texture
- **Walls:**
  - "THIS HOUSE IS NOT FOR SALE. BEWARE 419."
  - "Post no bills"
  - "Do not urinate here, by order"
  - Crusade posters, political faces, funeral posters, Nollywood posters, "Jesus is Lord" on buses.
- **Bus slogans:** "No food for lazy man", "God's time is the best", "Who knows tomorrow".
- **Sound:**
  - Conductors chanting their routes.
  - Hawkers' product shouts: "pure water!", "gala!", "cold zobo!".
  - Horns, generators, the call to prayer, church speakers at night, music from every shop.
- **Talk:** NPCs speak Pidgin, Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa and English, and switch between them mid-sentence. They react to the player: "Oga, how far?", "You dey craze?", "Shey you dey see road?"

## Places named the Lagos way (proposal)
- **The district names stay** as the map's names: The Maze, The Hub, Saba and so on.
- **The names people use are the bus stops,** named the way Lagos names them, after what is or was there:
  - a company: "Costain", "Berger"
  - a church: "Cele"
  - a distance: "Mile 2", "Mile 12"
  - a junction: "Iyana-…"
  - a thing: "Under Bridge", "Second Gate", "Two Pole", "Pako" (from "parking")
- **Conductors chain them into routes:** "Saba–Under Bridge–Ladi! Enter with your change!"
- **To do:** give every bus stop in the data a stop name in this spirit, invented, and a route call for each danfo route.

## Brands (invented; real brands are never used)
- **Every everyday thing gets an invented brand:**
  - noodles, sachet water, malt drink, soft drinks
  - the sausage roll in a wrapper
  - a telecom in yellow
  - banks, a betting company, a paint company that paints whole buildings in its colours
  - a generator brand, a cement brand
- **Bet Gidi** is the owner's name for the betting brand. A quick search found no company using it; check trademarks properly before any release.

## Betting (how it could play)
- **Bet Gidi shops** in every poor district: screens, slips, men arguing about football.
- **The player bets in-game money on in-game football:** a Gidi league, and virtual football every 3 minutes, as the real shops run it. Results come in on the radio and on the shop TVs.
- **It ties into story and life:** debts, a boss who runs fixed matches, a shop as a gang front.
- **Hard line: no real money, ever,** in or out of the betting, and no buying in-game currency to bet with. Loot-box and gambling rules (and age ratings) make that dangerous, and it's ethically wrong for a game about hustling to sell gambling to players.

## Currency
- **Recommendation: naira (₦), at Lagos prices.** GTA uses dollars; Gidi uses naira. That isn't copying a place: it's the life.
  - "Na 200 naira" and "5k" carry the feel.
  - The money itself is invented: notes with invented faces.
- **The alternatives** are "Gidi naira" or the owner's "Lyra" (naira with an L). Lyra reads as the lira (Turkey, Italy's old money). Owner to decide.

## The radio
- **Made for Gidi, not streamed from servers or APIs.** Streaming would bring licences that expire, and broken songs years later, as GTA's radio has lost tracks.
- **Music:** license songs from unsigned and rising Nigerian artists, as a buyout forever, or commission them. It's cheaper than stars, more authentic, and good for them.
- **Hosts:** Claude drafts the scripts; real Nigerian voice actors read them.
- **Stations, a starting list:**
  - Afrobeats and amapiano
  - fuji and highlife
  - street-pop
  - talk radio and call-ins
  - a gospel station
  - a news and traffic station that reports the city's real state: power cuts, flooding, betting results, the player's chaos

## After the story: what you do in the open world
- **Hustles:** drive a danfo, keke or okada on routes, sell in the market, run a POS stand, fix generators, deliver.
- **Owning things:**
  - **Rungs:** a kiosk, then a shop, then a motor park, then a filling station, then a building.
  - **What you get:** income, and trouble: rent, levies, rivals, raids.
- **Standing:** respect per district with the unions, the area boys, the police and the community. It opens doors and closes them.
- **Events:**
  - Owambes to crash.
  - Football on Saturdays.
  - December.
  - Danfo races at night.
  - Rainy-season rescues.
- **People:** strangers with small stories, met and met again.

## Online
- **Later, if ever.** GTA Online came after the single-player game, with a huge team, servers, anti-cheat and years of updates.
- **For now:** design the single-player world so it could go online. Gidi's life suits it: crews as unions, owned businesses, betting leagues, races.
- **Start with the world.**
