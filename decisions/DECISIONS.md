Compound walls moved into the data (`scripts/compounds.py`); balconies, canopies, trees and parked vehicles follow the same way.

- **Know when to stop generating; then hand-make Gidi** (16 Oct). "It's important to know when to stop generating and start hand making Gidi, including checking out what makes Lagos unmistakably Lagos and working with them on the map... so we are not in generator hell."

- **The hand pass covers infrastructure and labels** (16 Oct). Hand placement must place infrastructure properly and label it properly: bus stops, traffic lights, street lights, roads, streets, everything. The hand files already add, move and delete buildings and furniture. They need:
  - road and street edits (add, move, delete, with the width class)
  - names and labels on everything
  - checks that nothing stands on a carriageway
- **Roads have surfaces; poorer areas have unpaved and marshy ones** (16 Oct). Some Lagos roads barely look like roads: marshy ground, grime and mud, a truck driving through (the owner's photo). Every road and street carries a surface:
  - tarred
  - worn tar (potholes, broken edges)
  - unpaved laterite or sand
  - mud (waterlogged, standing water, grime)

  Main roads stay tarred, even when broken. Mud goes to the poorer, wetter places: low ground, near water, back streets, the open fabric types.

  **Data caution:** Overture tags most of Lagos's local streets "unpaved" where it tags them at all (98% even in Yaba, which is mostly tarred), so the tags are checked against Street View and the owner's photos before they set anything.
- **Hand-place, not tune.** The generator gives the base; then exact hand placement and clean-up through hand files (`web/hand/<id>.json`).
- **Freeze everything** once a district is built, so any spot can be quizzed (`scripts/whats_here.py`). The map must come out the same every time.
- **Account for every square metre:** carriageway, gutter, walkway, building line, declared encroachment. Furniture never stands on the road; decks are decks. Road widths: `scripts/cross_sections.py`.
- **Markets spill onto the road:** traders take the carriageway, with a lane kept clear.
- **Everything is in the data** (17 Oct: "even the grain of sand on the ground... if everything is in the data, we'll be able to better place stuff"). Anything that stands somewhere is an object in the data, with its own position (parked cars, stalls, water tanks, signs, people's spots). What is too fine to place one by one (sand, grass, litter, stains) is in the data as painted areas with a kind, a density and a seed, so it can be seen, checked and changed on the board. The kit and Unreal only dress what the data says; they invent nothing.
- **Every road end is something** (17 Oct): a junction, a close with a turning space, a gate or house, the water, or the edge of the map. A road never just stops (`scripts/roadends.py`; the board flags loose ends).

## How to work
- **Git:** never push to main; branches and PRs. Self-merge is fine when the checks or living test pass. Docs-only PRs can merge directly.
- **Writing:** plain words for a designer, honest critique, one recommendation. Look at what you make before showing it.
- **Hold-offs:** don't publish to the live artifact link (a different account).
- **Unreal (go, 17 Oct):** the game is its own repo, `~/gidi-game`. The gidi data is the master copy: the importer rebuilds the city from it every time and nothing it makes is committed. Hand-made assets go through Git LFS, each listed in `ASSETS.md`.
- **Cost:** use cheaper models where possible (Pro plan).

## Look and machine
- **The look target** (18 Oct, from two videos the owner sent): Gidi should look like the stilt-town Unreal game (every surface a real scanned material, every prop a proper model) in the places that matter, and feel like the Lagos street video (danfos three deep, crowds on the verge, stalls, wet dirty ground, haze) everywhere. The street video is AI-made: its photo-real people are not a game target. The whole city is generated from the data with Megascans materials and real models for what repeats (danfo, keke, okada, stall, drum, generator, shutter); the hero places (Ladi Lane, the markets, where the story is) are finished by hand to the higher level.
- **The laptop is the target machine** (18 Oct): the owner's M4 MacBook, 16 GB. At least 30 frames a second at 1080p on it, memory kept in check; every new piece fits the budget, and the high-end look is a higher setting, never the floor. Measure, don't guess (frame counters in the game). Textures at 2K in the project (8K downloads are capped in Unreal). Nothing runs beside the editor: bakes, Blender builds and Unreal take turns.
- **Build order: outward from the Maze, not story order** (19 Oct: "I don't even have a set story"). First one Maze street to the look target end to end (real models, the crowd, a walkable character, measured on the laptop); then the Maze's neighbours one at a time (the Hub, the Capital across Marigold Junction, Food Gate, then whatever borders the finished area), so the finished world is always one connected walkable piece. Each district: its Google Earth study (several real places), network and density, then kit and materials.
- **Consoles later:** a PS5 build needs Sony's PlayStation Partners programme (an application, an agreement, a dev kit). Build for Mac and PC first; apply when there's a playable piece of Gidi to show.

- **Places are built like the fonts and the danfo** (19 Oct): study a real Lagos spot on Google Earth and Street View in close detail, rebuild it one-for-one first (its layout, buildings, stalls, wires, clutter), then change it step by step until it is Gidi's own (invented names, our shops and brands, our twists). Street View is the main reference: more detail than any photo search. The game may take a year or two; places get their pass one at a time. Rules learned in one place improve the kit city-wide (e.g. balconies always have a door or opening).
- **Every street has a real twin** (19 Oct): each district gets its whole plan from Google Earth first (where the real place's buildings, schools, churches, markets and motor parks are, and how many), so the ecosystem is real; each Gidi street records the real street that inspires it (coordinates and pictures stay in `~/Real-world/research/`). Status on the pass map: **red** = twin known, the street built from the district plan with the default kit; **amber** = studied at street level (Street View); **green** = modelled in detail and passed by the owner. Streets then get built bit by bit, down to the detail.
- **District by district, matched to real life at plan level first** (19 Oct): the generator's guesses (road types, widths, building types) don't match Lagos, and fixing spots by hand doesn't stop the same mistakes elsewhere. So each district gets its real twin area studied from above on Google Earth, and its plan set from that (road network with the right types and widths, building types and heights, markets, motor parks, schools, churches, real counts) before details. Every street then starts red; details come street by street (Street View study, amber; one-for-one modelling and the owner's pass, green). Ìbùdó first. Lesson that prompted it: Market Road was entered as a "main", which the kit draws as a tarred highway with kerbs and lines; the type of a road decides how it looks.
- **Every type has a real twin too** (19 Oct): not only streets: every type of building, plot, street item, vehicle, deck, monument, road, road surface, ground and house detail records the real thing it comes from, where it was seen, and pictures (kept in `~/Real-world/research/`, never in the repo). The list is `docs/TWINS.md`, made by `scripts/twins.py` from the map and `web/twins.json`; new types appear there by themselves. Same colours as the streets: none (no twin yet), red (named, no picture), amber (pictures studied), green (modelled and passed by the owner). Owner: "so we know we have a real life twin". Getting the pictures is fine ("if you can get images it'll be fine").
- **A district is the child of its real inspirations, built only from real pieces** (owner, 19 Oct, night): "say these two people had a baby, and the baby was a functioning district". Ìbùdó is the child of Oshodi and Ojuelegba. Study both places in their entirety first (road structure from expressway to lane, markets, motor parks and how people park, kiosks and shop rows, schools, churches, mosques, civic centres, how the place functions socially), see what Ìbùdó's map can hold, then place every street and place from a real street or place in the two, mixed and matched ("you're not inventing any district from anything here"). Every Gidi street records its real source street, with pictures of its buildings, paths and ground (thousands of photos, outside the repo), so modelling works from them. The generated side streets go. Plan v2's big pieces (19 Oct) are not merged as such: only the rail through the Oshodi crossing stands; the rest is checked against the study.
- **Seams between child districts** (owner, 20 Oct: "remember that"): shared lines (arterials, the rail, district-edge mains) keep their crossing points at district edges; inside a district they may follow the real roads. A neighbour's street that ends on the shared edge carries on into the district (checks 5-7 pass for every area). A real street reaching the edge is run on to the edge road, never left floating. Neighbours stay frozen; only a decided line changes a neighbour, by hand edits. At the seams the real places don't match their Gidi neighbours, so seams are joined by hand at edge roads and gateways; when a neighbour is rebuilt from its own real places, its crossings line up with the built district.
- **Gidi stays Gidi, not a clone of Lagos** (owner, 20 Oct): Lagos is too big and much of it repetitive, so the 17-district frame stays. What changes is that every street, road, house and civic place in a district has its reference in real Lagos, taken from that district's inspirations (its real LGAs and neighbourhoods), with its twin recorded. Roads, streets and links must connect properly with the neighbouring districts (see the seams rule).
- **Take the interesting parts** (owner, 20 Oct: "Gidi has to be very interesting"): from each district's inspirations, take the pieces with the most life and character (junctions, markets, under-bridges, coach yards, night strips, canals, odd civic places), not the repetitive stretches. Example: Ìbùdó's factory belt became the Jibowu coach strip.
- **A livable world before the story** (owner, 20 Oct): before the story lands, Gidi must work as an open world you can live in: move around (on foot, danfo, okada, keke, cars), bet, gather wealth, transport people, transport goods. The map's places must carry what those activities need (betting shops, motor parks and ranks, markets and loading points, depots, fuel, banks), placed from the real inspirations like everything else.
- **The item register, once per finished district** (owner, 20 Oct): every item on the map (each building, street and place) gets its real data type (shop house, betting shop, church, kiosk, rooming house, depot...), storeys, a description and its game use (betting, pick-up, loading, money) read from its own twin photographs; buildings no camera sees get their type from size, height and neighbourhood, marked unseen. Done once a district is fully built (not area by area, so later areas don't make it redo), not as patches like extra pins. Order per district: study, child plan (owner checks), build area by area, twin photos alongside, then the register, then the owner's review.
- **Match passes** (19 Oct): a reference picture (Street View or a video frame) and the game rendered from the same spot, side by side, zoomed in, fixed element by element (ground, edges, buildings, people, overhead, light) until it holds up.

- **Fixes reach everything already built** (owner, 21 Oct: "if you fix a bug in the future... let's figure out how to get it retroactive"; "I can ask you this question again tomorrow, and you still find gaps... we can't keep doing that"). The areas built from real places are not kept as hand edits: `scripts/rebuild_areas.py` builds every area again from its source (the child plan, the real data, the builder) into `web/hand/gen/<district>.json`, and the bake's own clean-up (`scripts/autoclean.py`) runs every bake. A fix to the builder or the clean-up reaches every area on the next rebuild. `web/hand/<district>.json` keeps only fixes made by hand; one that no longer lands on anything fails the checks (check 17) instead of vanishing. Every rule here has a row in `decisions/CHECKS.md` saying what checks it (`scripts/check_rules.py`); rules nothing checks yet are listed as GAP in every bake report, so a gap shows up without anyone asking. How close an area is to its real place is checked too (`scripts/check_real.py`: buildings, streets, twins both ways, kinds of place, the livable things the twin photos show, real names).
- **Hand-made, checked by programs; the base is frozen** (owner, 22 Oct: "did we not agree to have the building be hand done and the checks be programmatic"; "you're making us work in circles"). Overrides the builder half of "Fixes reach everything already built" (21 Oct). Ìbùdó's generated areas (`web/hand/gen/hub.json`, as of #167) are frozen as the base: no more changes to the area builder (`scripts/hand_spots/ibudo_real_area.py`) or reruns of `scripts/rebuild_areas.py` (it refuses to run unless `GIDI_REBUILD_OK=owner`, set only when the owner says so). Every gap a check finds is fixed by a hand edit at that spot in `web/hand/<district>.json`. What stays retroactive: the checks (`check_district`, `check_real`, `check_rules`, the picture checker) and the bake's shared tools, which run on every bake and report gaps without fixing them; a hand edit that stops landing fails check 17.
- **Real Lagos roads, no ideal road template** (owner, 22 Oct: "don't keep the rule... place the roads correctly, and place the streets correctly. Make it reflect the real Lagos roads"; "Lagos is a little bit imperfect... that imperfection, we're seeing it as a problem when we view Gidi. But the problem is that Lagos itself is imperfect"; "if we're properly getting Lagos, we're good"). Replaces the building line in `scripts/cross_sections.py` (15 Oct) and the fixed road widths as rules. Roads, streets and lanes run on their real lines at their real widths; buildings stand where their real twins stand. No walkways or pavements unless the real road has them: people walk on the edge of the road and the street, as in the photos. A real building that lands on a Gidi road means the road is in the wrong place: move the road by hand, never drop the building. The checks measure how much real Lagos the map holds (buildings on their spots, roads on their lines, connections), not how well it fits a template; a car-blocking clash is reported for a hand fix, not cleaned away. The owner accepts the one-time city-wide cost ("if the entire city is properly fixed, it's a one-time cost"). With it: an audit of the whole city's flow at 1:4, whether districts need reshaping, moving or adding so Gidi flows like real Lagos (it revisits "Gidi stays Gidi" (20 Oct) only as far as the audit shows; the owner decides the frame).
- **Gidi is Lagos, miniaturised; the map starts again** (owner, 22 Oct: "making Lagos in a miniaturized way"; "you have to understand how the entire Lagos map works to begin with before knowing what we're going to cut as districts"; "maybe start the map from Gidi from scratch"). Replaces the 17-district frame of "Gidi stays Gidi" (20 Oct) and the far-away borrowing in "child district" (19 Oct). (1) Gidi takes the real Lagos map: its road network and where every place sits relative to the others, smaller. (2) Shrinking cuts the number of streets and connections, never the houses (a house stays house-size); which streets and places survive is chosen by hand per district from its defining features. (3) Districts come from the real map: study all of Lagos first (its 20 LGAs and 37 LCDAs, but by how places really work: estate belts, markets, industry, waterfronts, transport hubs), then cut districts: a rich place gets its own, neighbouring places that share a character merge into one district that feels like its own place. No set number of districts, councils or areas, and no set size yet. A district is inspired only by the places actually there and their neighbours, never by somewhere across the city. (4) Real houses are photographed as references, but never modelled as copies: models take inspiration (change colour, style, details); each Gidi street records the real streets it was inspired by as references. (5) Still built by hand, checked by programs; the research, twin photos and tools are kept; the sunk cost of the old frame is accepted.
- **The new map is 1:3, about 27 x 10.5 km** (owner, 22 Oct: "We can do 27 by 10.5 kilometers"). With houses at real size this keeps about 1 local street in 11 on average (docs/lagos-study/STUDY.md); the shrink is uneven: repetitive sprawl is squeezed hard (few of its streets kept), rich places keep more.
- **Roads first, districts by their borders** (owner, 22 Oct: "let's have the plan of Gidi first start from its roads"; "the roads are like the central point of the entire Lagos"; "divvy them less about their roads as more about like what their borders are... can this place connect to this place to make a district"). Replaces the seed-point units of the first similarity study (`docs/lagos-study/similar/`), which swallowed places with no seed and joined places that are not neighbours (Gbagada and Maryland, with Anthony Village between). (1) The plan starts from all of Lagos's real roads (expressways, trunk and main roads, bridges, with rail and water), shrunk to 1:3: the skeleton everything else hangs on. (2) The city is built around how real Lagos sits on those roads. (3) Places are cut by their real borders (a road, canal, lagoon, rail, an estate wall, or where the houses change), each place with its real neighbour across each border; two places join into one district only if they really touch and are alike enough, otherwise they stand apart. (4) What each district will be built from is known from the start, so districts are not designed one by one from nothing.
- **The plan is hand-made; the models come from kits** (owner, 22 Oct: "for the modeling of the places... kits is the way"; "for the actual districts, building the plan would require hand making. No generators, nothing. Proper hand making"). (1) The city's plan (roads, places, streets, every building's spot) is placed by hand on the road skeleton, no generators. (2) What stands on the plan is modelled from kits first: one kit per kind of Lagos place (house types and their parts, how often each appears, plots, streets, ground, clutter, people), each studied from a few hundred Street View pictures of the clearest real examples, its list checked by the owner, its parts built piece by piece in Blender (inspired, never copied), then a test street matched against Street View. (3) Hero places are modelled on top by hand. (4) Then the world is improved bit by bit: our own touch, place by place.
