# Decisions (the owner's standing rules)

What still guides the work. A decision that a later one replaced, or that held only for the old map, is in
`HISTORY.md`, with what replaced it. Where a later decision changed part of one, the change is noted at its end.
Each rule has a row in `CHECKS.md`. Ideas not decided yet are in `IDEAS.md`.

## The world
- **Open world first; narrative optional** (15 Oct 2026). "The entire world right now feels more placeholder and open world for me than having a set narrative." Districts are defined by what a player can do and meet there. The world comes first and is built to stand on its own; Tunde's story is layered on it. Don't push plot or characters beyond what the owner has confirmed.
- **Gidi is our city.** Inspired by Lagos but our own. Data is a starting point; the owner's vision and the game's needs win.
- **Invented names only.** No real Lagos names in the game. Every place gets an echo name: a sound-alike in the same language (owner, 22 Oct: "Make the names sound similar. So if something is English, it should be English. Something is Yoruba, it should be Yoruba"; Yaba → Saba, Computer Village → Gadget Village), never the real name, a real nearby place, rude or a cheap shot. Working names for all 51 districts: `docs/lagos-study/roads/NAMES.md`. Brands and businesses are always invented. *Changed 22-23 Oct:* "never traced" no longer holds for geometry: roads run on real lines ("Real Lagos roads") and houses are drafted from open footprints ("Houses: real footprints first"); Google's geometry stays out ("Google: study yes, lift no").
- **Safe ground:** churches, mosques, schools, hospitals, clinics and places flagged `safe` are never crime sites. Sacred traditions are never criminal.

## The map
- **If it's in the 3D, it's in the 2D** (owner, 16 Oct 2026). The map data describes everything that takes up ground: roads, plots, houses, walls, furniture. The 3D only dresses it: windows, roofs, paint, wear. It never places things of its own. Compound walls moved into the data (`scripts/compounds.py`); balconies, canopies, trees and parked vehicles follow the same way.
- **Hand-making happens on the drawing board** (16 Oct). A clean plan of the city to scale where everything can be checked, moved, resized, reshaped, added and deleted (`docs/BOARD.md`). Edits save as hand edits; "bake the board" makes them real.
- **The browser 3D is a quick preview; the full scene is for Unreal** (owner, 16 Oct): "the only reason I want a 3d view is to quickly see what the world will look like... speed is important." Light tiles only. *Now:* the browser tiles are the old map's; the new map is seen in Unreal.
- **The map is never final** (owner, 16 Oct). Gidi is tweaked until the game ships. Unreal will be one more view of the data, rebuilt by a re-runnable importer. *Now:* the game repo's builders build what the map's records say, every time (`gidi-game/tools/*_build.py`).
- **New districts:** *(15 Oct, old-map names; the new map has both on their real spots: 33 Airport, 17 Yaba and 18 UNILAG in NAMES.md)*
  - **The Airport,** where the real airport sits.
  - **Saba** (the owner's name), the Yaba/Akoka district, with **Lagoon University** on the lagoon.
- **Old Island is a true island,** with Gidi's versions of Third Mainland (the Long Bridge), Carter (Channel Bridge), Eko (Wharf Bridge) and the Lekki–Ikoyi Link (Cable Bridge, tolled). The Gold Island (after Banana Island) sits off the Heights on the Crescent Causeway. *Now:* the new map has the real island and its real bridges; the names here are old-map names, to be checked against the sound-alike rule ("Invented names only").
- **The research must be detailed:** "look at everything in the data". Every district has a deep study (`docs/DEEP-STUDY.md`, `docs/studies/`).
- **Monuments are placed by hand** (16 Oct). The kit's pieces (`legacy/blender/gidi_kit/monuments.py`, `landmarks.py`) are placed with the `add monument` hand edit. The plan is in `docs/hand/borrowed-lagos.md`. A district with any hand-placed monument keeps only those.
- **Claude decides when the owner says so** (16 Oct). "Decide. I trust your judgment." On 16 Oct Claude:
  - merged Gidi v2 (PR #54)
  - renamed **Pedro Print Row to Press Row** and **Daleko Grain Yard to Gari Yard** (both real Lagos names)
  - kept **Ladi Lane** (invented; a nod to Ladipo, not a copy) and **National Bowl**
  - accepted all 23 items of the Maze and Hub list (`docs/hand/maze-hub-lagos.md`)

  Decisions made this way are recorded here like any other, and the owner can reverse any of them.
- **The hand pass covers infrastructure and labels** (16 Oct). Hand placement must place infrastructure properly and label it properly: bus stops, traffic lights, street lights, roads, streets, everything. The hand files already add, move and delete buildings and furniture. They need:
  - road and street edits (add, move, delete, with the width class)
  - names and labels on everything
  - checks that nothing stands on a carriageway
- **Roads have surfaces; poorer areas have unpaved and marshy ones** (16 Oct). Some Lagos roads barely look like roads: marshy ground, grime and mud, a truck driving through (the owner's photo). Every road and street carries a surface:
  - tarred
  - worn tar (potholes, broken edges)
  - unpaved laterite or sand
  - mud (waterlogged, standing water, grime)

  Main roads stay tarred, even when broken. Mud goes to the poorer, wetter places: low ground, near water, back streets, the open fabric types.

  **Data caution:** Overture tags most of Lagos's local streets "unpaved" where it tags them at all (98% even in Yaba, which is mostly tarred), so the tags are checked against Street View and the owner's photos before they set anything.
- **Freeze everything** once a district is built, so any spot can be quizzed (`scripts/whats_here.py`). The map must come out the same every time.
- **Account for every square metre:** carriageway, gutter, walkway, building line, declared encroachment. Furniture never stands on the road; decks are decks. Road widths: `scripts/cross_sections.py`. *Changed 22 Oct:* the building line, the walkways and the fixed widths are replaced by "Real Lagos roads": real lines, real widths, people walking on the road's edge.
- **Markets spill onto the road:** traders take the carriageway, with a lane kept clear.
- **Everything is in the data** (17 Oct: "even the grain of sand on the ground... if everything is in the data, we'll be able to better place stuff"). Anything that stands somewhere is an object in the data, with its own position (parked cars, stalls, water tanks, signs, people's spots). What is too fine to place one by one (sand, grass, litter, stains) is in the data as painted areas with a kind, a density and a seed, so it can be seen, checked and changed on the board. The kit and Unreal only dress what the data says; they invent nothing. *Changed 22 Oct:* "a density and a seed" is replaced by "One ground truth": every fine thing is a stored record; brushes write them ("The map format's four calls").
- **Every road end is something** (17 Oct): a junction, a close with a turning space, a gate or house, the water, or the edge of the map. A road never just stops (`scripts/roadends.py`; the board flags loose ends).

## How to work
- **Git:** never push to main; branches and PRs. Self-merge is fine when the checks or living test pass. Docs-only PRs can merge directly.
- **Writing:** plain words for a designer, honest critique, one recommendation. Look at what you make before showing it.
- **Hold-offs:** don't publish to the live artifact link (a different account).
- **Unreal (go, 17 Oct):** the game is its own repo, `~/gidi-game`. The gidi data is the master copy: the importer rebuilds the city from it every time and nothing it makes is committed. Hand-made assets go through Git LFS, each listed in `ASSETS.md`.
- **Cost:** use cheaper models where possible (Pro plan).

## Look and machine
- **The look target** (18 Oct, from two videos the owner sent): Gidi should look like the stilt-town Unreal game (every surface a real scanned material, every prop a proper model) in the places that matter, and feel like the Lagos street video (danfos three deep, crowds on the verge, stalls, wet dirty ground, haze) everywhere. The street video is AI-made: its photo-real people are not a game target. The whole city is generated from the data with Megascans materials and real models for what repeats (danfo, keke, okada, stall, drum, generator, shutter); the hero places (Ladi Lane, the markets, where the story is) are finished by hand to the higher level.
- **The laptop is the target machine** (18 Oct): the owner's M4 MacBook, 16 GB. At least 30 frames a second at 1080p on it, memory kept in check; every new piece fits the budget, and the high-end look is a higher setting, never the floor. Measure, don't guess (frame counters in the game). Textures at 2K in the project (8K downloads are capped in Unreal). Nothing runs beside the editor: bakes, Blender builds and Unreal take turns.
- **Build order: outward from the Maze, not story order** (19 Oct: "I don't even have a set story"). First one Maze street to the look target end to end (real models, the crowd, a walkable character, measured on the laptop); then the Maze's neighbours one at a time (the Hub, the Capital across Marigold Junction, Food Gate, then whatever borders the finished area), so the finished world is always one connected walkable piece. Each district: its Google Earth study (several real places), network and density, then kit and materials. *Now:* old-map names; on the new map the work started from Mùṣá (15 Mushin), the old Maze's real place.

## Brand
- **Vehicles are built like the fonts** (19 Oct): the original as the base, in all its glory (the danfo is the T3-shape bus, measured from its real dimensions and the owner's references), then a short written list of Gidi's tweaks, distinct but not far off ("we are losing sauce" when it drifts into a generic design). No maker's badge (the owner's D instead). Built part by part in Blender (`gidi-game/tools/danfo_build.py`), so it comes apart for variants (rack, bull bar, hubcaps, mismatched panels) and wear levels. The same for the keke, okada, korope, molue and BRT.
- **The first goal: the street video's scene** (19 Oct): danfos three deep, kekes and okadas, crowds, stalls, wet dirty ground, haze, in one Àdàrú street, walked on the laptop and measured. Not the end goal; the first picture to prove, then the list gets crossed off (vehicles you can enter with their interiors and wear variants, characters and clothing, more streets, UI).
- **Meshy's place** (19 Oct): statues and monuments (nobody walks into The Reach); for anything people will walk into (the stadium, Crown Hall) Meshy designs the look and we build it ourselves in Blender, part by part. Not for general kit.
- **Matching Lagos** (19 Oct): the owner finds pictures of real Lagos places; we match each as closely as we can and let the data carry the look to the rest of the city (study yes, lift no). The Google Earth study stays for the layouts.
- **The kit session gets exact lists** (19 Oct): what kit to build, piece by piece, not open briefs.
- **Light by default on the laptop** (19 Oct): everyday building light (web tiles, one Blender job at a time); full-detail renders, full reimports and photo mode only when the owner asks or is away from the laptop.
- **People** (19 Oct; settled 24 Oct: the owner said yes to MakeHuman for the crowd, MetaHuman for people you interact with, once Claude confirmed crowd people can be punched, run over and react as in GTA): **MakeHuman for NPCs** (the crowd: hawkers, passengers, conductors in the background; built from a short spec with MPFB in Blender, `gidi-game/tools/person_build.py`, CC0 assets), **MetaHuman for characters you interact with** (story characters, close-ups, faces animated from the owner's phone video). Both animated in Unreal from retargeted motion. Crowd people are one mesh each (clothes merged into the body, one texture, near-to-far versions), because the measured cost of City Sample people is their hair, separate clothes and big textures (gidi-game `docs/PERFORMANCE.md`: 6 of them took high to about 2 fps). Being simple never makes them scenery: every crowd person can be talked to, pushed, punched, hit by a vehicle, and reacts (flees, fights back, falls as a ragdoll), on the same shared skeleton as the main characters.
- **Every street has a real twin** (19 Oct): each district gets its whole plan from Google Earth first (where the real place's buildings, schools, churches, markets and motor parks are, and how many), so the ecosystem is real; each Gidi street records the real street that inspires it (coordinates and pictures stay in `~/gidi-data/research/`). Status on the pass map: **red** = twin known, the street built from the district plan with the default kit; **amber** = studied at street level (Street View); **green** = modelled in detail and passed by the owner. Streets then get built bit by bit, down to the detail.
- **Take the interesting parts** (owner, 20 Oct: "Gidi has to be very interesting"): from each district's inspirations, take the pieces with the most life and character (junctions, markets, under-bridges, coach yards, night strips, canals, odd civic places), not the repetitive stretches. Example: Ìbùdó's factory belt became the Jibowu coach strip.
- **A livable world before the story** (owner, 20 Oct): before the story lands, Gidi must work as an open world you can live in: move around (on foot, danfo, okada, keke, cars), bet, gather wealth, transport people, transport goods. The map's places must carry what those activities need (betting shops, motor parks and ranks, markets and loading points, depots, fuel, banks), placed from the real inspirations like everything else.
- **The item register, once per finished district** (owner, 20 Oct): every item on the map (each building, street and place) gets its real data type (shop house, betting shop, church, kiosk, rooming house, depot...), storeys, a description and its game use (betting, pick-up, loading, money) read from its own twin photographs; buildings no camera sees get their type from size, height and neighbourhood, marked unseen. Done once a district is fully built (not area by area, so later areas don't make it redo), not as patches like extra pins. Order per district: study, child plan (owner checks), build area by area, twin photos alongside, then the register, then the owner's review.
- **Match passes** (19 Oct): a reference picture (Street View or a video frame) and the game rendered from the same spot, side by side, zoomed in, fixed element by element (ground, edges, buildings, people, overhead, light) until it holds up.

- **Hand-made, checked by programs; the base is frozen** (owner, 22 Oct: "did we not agree to have the building be hand done and the checks be programmatic"; "you're making us work in circles"). Overrides the builder half of "Fixes reach everything already built" (21 Oct). Ìbùdó's generated areas (`web/hand/gen/hub.json`, as of #167) are frozen as the base: no more changes to the area builder (`scripts/hand_spots/ibudo_real_area.py`) or reruns of `scripts/rebuild_areas.py` (it refuses to run unless `GIDI_REBUILD_OK=owner`, set only when the owner says so). Every gap a check finds is fixed by a hand edit at that spot in `web/hand/<district>.json`. What stays retroactive: the checks (`check_district`, `check_real`, `check_rules`, the picture checker) and the bake's shared tools, which run on every bake and report gaps without fixing them; a hand edit that stops landing fails check 17.
- **Real Lagos roads, no ideal road template** (owner, 22 Oct: "don't keep the rule... place the roads correctly, and place the streets correctly. Make it reflect the real Lagos roads"; "Lagos is a little bit imperfect... that imperfection, we're seeing it as a problem when we view Gidi. But the problem is that Lagos itself is imperfect"; "if we're properly getting Lagos, we're good"). Replaces the building line in `scripts/cross_sections.py` (15 Oct) and the fixed road widths as rules. Roads, streets and lanes run on their real lines at their real widths; buildings stand where their real twins stand. No walkways or pavements unless the real road has them: people walk on the edge of the road and the street, as in the photos. A real building that lands on a Gidi road means the road is in the wrong place: move the road by hand, never drop the building. The checks measure how much real Lagos the map holds (buildings on their spots, roads on their lines, connections), not how well it fits a template; a car-blocking clash is reported for a hand fix, not cleaned away. The owner accepts the one-time city-wide cost ("if the entire city is properly fixed, it's a one-time cost"). With it: an audit of the whole city's flow at 1:4, whether districts need reshaping, moving or adding so Gidi flows like real Lagos (it revisits "Gidi stays Gidi" (20 Oct) only as far as the audit shows; the owner decides the frame).
- **Gidi is Lagos, miniaturised; the map starts again** (owner, 22 Oct: "making Lagos in a miniaturized way"; "you have to understand how the entire Lagos map works to begin with before knowing what we're going to cut as districts"; "maybe start the map from Gidi from scratch"). Replaces the 17-district frame of "Gidi stays Gidi" (20 Oct) and the far-away borrowing in "child district" (19 Oct). (1) Gidi takes the real Lagos map: its road network and where every place sits relative to the others, smaller. (2) Shrinking cuts the number of streets and connections, never the houses (a house stays house-size); which streets and places survive is chosen by hand per district from its defining features. (3) Districts come from the real map: study all of Lagos first (its 20 LGAs and 37 LCDAs, but by how places really work: estate belts, markets, industry, waterfronts, transport hubs), then cut districts: a rich place gets its own, neighbouring places that share a character merge into one district that feels like its own place. No set number of districts, councils or areas, and no set size yet. A district is inspired only by the places actually there and their neighbours, never by somewhere across the city. (4) Real houses are photographed as references, but never modelled as copies: models take inspiration (change colour, style, details); each Gidi street records the real streets it was inspired by as references. (5) Still built by hand, checked by programs; the research, twin photos and tools are kept; the sunk cost of the old frame is accepted.
- **The new map is 1:3, about 27 x 10.5 km** (owner, 22 Oct: "We can do 27 by 10.5 kilometers"). With houses at real size this keeps about 1 local street in 11 on average (docs/lagos-study/STUDY.md); the shrink is uneven: repetitive sprawl is squeezed hard (few of its streets kept), rich places keep more.
- **Roads first, districts by their borders** (owner, 22 Oct: "let's have the plan of Gidi first start from its roads"; "the roads are like the central point of the entire Lagos"; "divvy them less about their roads as more about like what their borders are... can this place connect to this place to make a district"). Replaces the seed-point units of the first similarity study (`docs/lagos-study/similar/`), which swallowed places with no seed and joined places that are not neighbours (Gbagada and Maryland, with Anthony Village between). (1) The plan starts from all of Lagos's real roads (expressways, trunk and main roads, bridges, with rail and water), shrunk to 1:3: the skeleton everything else hangs on. (2) The city is built around how real Lagos sits on those roads. (3) Places are cut by their real borders (a road, canal, lagoon, rail, an estate wall, or where the houses change), each place with its real neighbour across each border; two places join into one district only if they really touch and are alike enough, otherwise they stand apart. (4) What each district will be built from is known from the start, so districts are not designed one by one from nothing.
- **The plan is hand-made; the models come from kits** (owner, 22 Oct: "for the modeling of the places... kits is the way"; "for the actual districts, building the plan would require hand making. No generators, nothing. Proper hand making"). (1) The city's plan (roads, places, streets, every building's spot) is placed by hand on the road skeleton, no generators. (2) What stands on the plan is modelled from kits first: one kit per kind of Lagos place (house types and their parts, how often each appears, plots, streets, ground, clutter, people), each studied from a few hundred Street View pictures of the clearest real examples, its list checked by the owner, its parts built piece by piece in Blender (inspired, never copied), then a test street matched against Street View. (3) Hero places are modelled on top by hand. (4) Then the world is improved bit by bit: our own touch, place by place.
- **A kit is proven by rebuilding real streets from it** (owner, 22 Oct: "take street views at random and try to recreate it from the kit... hundreds of times... it should look like a rendition of the same place, but just in Blender or on Unreal Engine"). Each kit is tested by picking real Street View spots at random and rebuilding each one from the kit's parts as close to one-for-one as possible, in Blender or Unreal, side by side with the picture. When a spot holds up, the parts that built it are good; when it doesn't, the missing or wrong part is fixed in the kit. Hundreds of spots across the kits, until the array of kits can build the whole city. Pieces too hard to make straight in Blender go the danfo way: Meshy makes a design (model and credits confirmed with the owner first), we study how it did it, then build our own part by part in Blender.
- **The map tells the kit what goes where** (owner, 22 Oct: "the 2D map, whenever we finish building it, informs and interfaces with what the kit gives it"). The hand-made 2D map carries everything a kit needs to know for each spot: the house type and storeys, what the ground floor is (shop, home, workshop), walls and gates, the street's surface, gutters and their edges, kerbs, what stands on the verge, the place's kind. The kit reads it and puts exactly that there; it never decides placement or invents things the map does not say. A kit part with no map field to drive it is a gap in the interface, fixed in the map's fields, not by letting the kit guess.
- **The Reach goes with Festac into the new map** (owner, 22 Oct: "we should have it in wherever we have like the Festac rendition of Gidi, because Festac is the inspiration for that piece of art"). Carries the 19 Oct rule into the new frame: wherever the new map's Festac-inspired place is, The Reach stands there.
- **Build light, look full: modes, near-detail and streaming** (owner, 22 Oct: "for us to build fast... the editor mode has to probably be on the low spectrum of things"; "when you want to see the game at its full glory, you can just like flip a switch"; "things that were closer were... detailed... things that were farther... not very detailed at all"; "the things on my back get offloaded off the memory, so the things at my front could get into the memory. But it has to be fast because people might like go back and forth very fast"). Adds to "The laptop is the target machine" (18 Oct) and "Light by default" (19 Oct). (1) Three modes, one switch: Work (the default for building: low settings, simple light, the view drawn smaller, no crowd, so the laptop stays smooth while things are moved), Play (Low / Medium / High per machine: an 8 GB MacBook Air is the floor on Low, the owner's laptop on High at 30 fps 1080p), Full (everything on, to see how it looks at its best on the owner's machine; only for looking and photos). (2) Near things carry detail, far things are simple stand-ins (for what Nanite draws, Nanite and HLOD do this: "One near model", 24 Oct) (levels of detail for buildings, people and props; far blocks merged into one cheap shape). (3) What the camera can't see is not drawn (off-screen and hidden behind buildings). (4) The world streams in pieces around the player: pieces ahead load before they are needed, pieces behind are let go only after a margin, so turning back is instant; memory stays under the machine's budget. (5) Every level has a budget (people, triangles, texture memory, frame time) and a check measures it in each mode; a level over budget is fixed before it grows. A 2015 MacBook Air cannot run Unreal 5 at any setting: reaching it would mean streaming the game from a stronger machine.
- **One near model; Nanite draws near to far** (owner, 24 Oct, on hearing Unreal's Nanite draws each thing in as much detail as the screen shows: "is there still a need for the [near], mid and [far]?"; then yes to this rule). Changes point (2) of "Build light, look full" for everything Nanite draws. Each builder makes one near model, within a triangle budget for its kind; fine detail (ribs, grain, stains, rust) is painted into image textures (colour, roughness and metal, normal) baked into its .glb, never Blender shader math, which does not reach Unreal. Nanite draws it from near to far and Unreal's HLOD makes the far city's stand-ins, so builders make no far versions of their own. Separate near and far versions are made only for what Nanite does not draw well: people (skinned), glass and other see-through things, and thin layered things (wires, foliage, sheets lying over each other: the zinc roof's ribs are modelled only at the eave for this, gidi-game #73). The budgets come from a street of these models measured in Unreal on the owner's laptop (frame time and memory).
- **The owner's eye passes a kit** (owner, 22 Oct: "our kit has to be something that I approve with my eyes"; "we generate the exact place and take a picture of it from the angle like it was taken on the street view, and I will try to spot the difference"; "they have to match the street view every time by my eyes"). Sharpens "A kit is proven by rebuilding real streets from it". Each test spot is rebuilt and shot from the Street View's own spot, heading, height and field of view, and shown beside the Street View picture. The owner looks and says pass or fail and what is different; a fail goes back to the part that caused it, which is made again and shown again. A kit is only approved by the owner's eye, never by a score; `KIT.md` lists ideas from the study until its parts have passed.
- **One ground truth: everything the kit shows is in the map data** (owner, 22 Oct: "everything that we do must be in map data. Every single thing"; "anytime we make a change to the map data, it just automatically changes in the kit"; "everything that is shown by the kit has to have a reference in map data... one ground truth"). Sharpens "The map tells the kit what goes where": the kit fills in nothing by itself, not even small detail. Every window, door, balcony run, sack, crate, bag of litter, bump and pothole in the road, poster and person spot is a record in the map, and the kit only draws what the records say. No seeds, random scatters or spacing rules inside the kit: where a test spot uses one today (window bays, goods arrangement, road relief, litter), it is turned into stored records. Change the record and the model changes on the next build; delete it and the thing is gone. Records may be copied by hand from a studied spot and then edited (a stamp), but what is stored is the full explicit record, never "copy of" or a rule. The map stays the single place to change anything.
- **The ultimate test: rebuild the real picture, then its other angles** (owner, 23 Oct: "the ultimate test… is trying to see if you can recreate that picture with the items you have modeled… recreate like multiple angles of that picture"). The list of things to model comes from real, undressed pictures of Lagos (Street View, Google Earth), never from a list made up in advance: zoom into each picture and list every thing that makes it up, until the list covers the whole image. For each thing, look at how others have already made it (kits, tools, tutorials), then model it clean to a standard look ("Model clean, then wear it"). The test: rebuild the picture from those modelled things alone. Then go further and rebuild the same place from other angles (nearby Street View steps and headings); a thing that only works from the first camera fails.
- **Model clean, then wear it** (owner, 23 Oct: "Model it clean first. Then we now start figuring out how to add dust… from new to almost dead"). Every object is first modelled clean and as modular as possible: whole parts, as they leave the maker. Wear (dust, grime, rust, fading, dents, missing bits) is a separate layer put on top, and it runs in stages from new to almost dead. How much wear, and any damage with a place (a dent, a missing door), comes from the record. The wear's own pattern may follow the object's shape (low edges rust first, dust settles on top faces, runs fall with gravity). Wear is never modelled into the clean part.
- **Know it up close, thin it far away** (owner, 23 Oct: "we know what it looks like in full focus… so we know how we're going to… reduce the polygons when we're not really up close"). Every object, from an umbrella to a kiosk, a lorry or a person, is first modelled at the detail it needs in full close-up and checked with close-ups of its details and where its parts join. Lighter versions for distance (fewer polygons, details baked into textures) are made from that full model, never instead of it.
- **Look how the pros do it, first** (owner's standing rule, said again 23 Oct; made a first step 24 Oct: "what solved our problem was that we researched... we will research how the pros do it, how it's done in the industry, or what ingenious way was done to achieve a particular thing"). Before building a new kind of thing or a new method, not only when stuck: find how studios, the engine's makers and expert artists already solve it (docs, talks, breakdowns, tutorials), adapt their solution, and say in the commit what was found and where. The case that set it: the street test of 375 houses could not load until research showed games share tiling textures and put fine detail in images, not triangles; the street went from killed while loading to 60 fps the same day. Also in CLAUDE.md. Downloads still need the owner's OK.
- **A robust kit, not a matched photo** (owner, 23 Oct, after zooming into the ot45 test: the lorry had no front tyres, the red truck was a box; "we're building to have like a robust kit for our world… as opposed to… particularly matching the image"; "whenever we need a whole, we can get a whole"). The kit tests' purpose is a kit that can build any Lagos scene whole, from any angle; photos test the kit and set the Lagos style, they are never targets to copy. Every part is a complete, reusable object (all its wheels, backs, undersides), its look set by record fields (size, colour, load, wear, damage states, parts present or missing), so one part covers many places; damage is a layer on a whole object, and a part is left out only where it is really gone. Method per spot: list every object and what it is in 3D (blurry photos are filled from other references, a Meshy study model, or how the thing is built); build each object alone and judge it on a turntable sheet (front, side, back, 3/4, top); place them from records; render a top view and a second street angle every pass (missing or hollow = fail); tone and texture last. A one-camera match by tricks is a fail.
- **A kit item grows; it splits only when it stops fitting** (owner, 23 Oct: "they work like objects… when we see a new street with a variation of an item we have, we add new properties in an existing item. If the property variant is too much we make a new item"; "the map load[s] the data as the ground truth… you can turn settings based off location"). Each kit item is one builder with a recipe (like `umbrella_build.py`, `danfo_build.py`). When a new street shows a variant of a thing we have, the first answer is a new recipe key or value on the existing item (a base, a state, a part present or missing), with the old recipes still building the same. A new item is made only when the variant no longer shares the item's parts or build (a market umbrella is one item open, half or closed; a tent canopy is another). The recipe keys are record fields, so a place's records set its items' settings: what an umbrella looks like at a spot is written in that spot's records, and the map stays the one ground truth.
- **The interface works both ways; the builders are the kit** (owner, 23 Oct, after the Emmanuel St layers: items are objects with properties, the map tells each placed item what it has, and the kits improve from photos, retroactively; "let the new builders become the kits going forward"). The game repo's builders (`gidi-game/tools/*_build.py`) are the kit; the old Blender kit is archived (`legacy/blender/`, frozen, never extended). Each builder declares every recipe key it reads (`FIELDS`: name, type, what it does when the key is absent), and a build that reads an undeclared key stops. A field a builder grows is added to the map schema in the same change, or it never reaches the world; a schema field no builder reads is flagged; records that do not set a newly added field are listed, so old places pick up the new property by hand.
- **Mùṣá's borders, edge roads, rail and service roads** (owner, 23 Oct, on PR #214: "give it to surulere"; Ladipo: "move the line so the whole market sits in Mùṣá"; the rest as recommended). (1) The south lobe below Itire Road (Randle, Akerele, Falolu, Karimu, Clegg) is Surulere's. (2) The whole Ladipo spare-parts market is Mùṣá's: the line moves north-west to take it from Isolo-Ladipo. Gidi is our city: a border can move for a place's sake. (3) An edge road belongs to the district on the side where its life is (shops, stops, markets); the neighbour joins onto it and never draws it again. (4) Rail and stations are map records (`rail`, `station`). (5) A motorway's service roads are their own streets (`service_road`), so they can carry their own stops, stalls and parked vehicles.
- **Houses: real footprints first, then by hand; inner land by lanes** (owner, 23 Oct, on the Mùṣá pilot block: chose "real footprints, then hand" and "lanes on dropped streets"). (1) A district's houses are first drafted from its real blocks' own houses: each Gidi block (its real block at 1:3) takes the real fabric at real size, moved, never scaled or turned, so frontages keep their real setbacks and angles (`scripts/draft_buildings.py`; Microsoft's open building footprints, ODbL, numbers only; never Google's geometry). (2) The draft writes footprints only; kind, storeys, roof, finish and ground floor are set by hand, block by block, from the shots, and corners and overlaps are cleaned by hand. A copied house that lands on a Gidi road is listed (`<district>/BUILDINGS.md`) and settled by hand: the road stays, because here the roads are on their real lines and the house is a copy from nearby fabric, not a real house on its own spot (the 22 Oct "a clash moves the road" was for real houses on their real spots). The house is set back on clear land beside where it fell, or left out where the frontage already holds its houses. The map is for the game only (owner, 23 Oct: "just going to be in game", the in-game map players put markers on), never shared as data; ODbL then asks only a credit (© Microsoft, © OpenStreetMap contributors / Overture) in the game's credits. (3) Where a big block leaves land far from any street, lanes and footpaths are drawn by hand on the lines of the real streets that were dropped (their gaps show in the copied houses), like Mushin's real closes and alleys, after studying the block's real twin on Google Maps and Earth (owner, 23 Oct: "always check Google Maps and earth to know about the houses and streets"). A block cut by hand-drawn lanes is redrafted with its twin's own houses turned to face the lanes (`--frontage`); a warehouse strip with its own sheds (`--sizes`). (4) A draft never moves what is already in the map: blocks that hold a building are skipped.
- **Rules draft, records rule; re-draft on request** (owner, 23 Oct: "that matches what I want"). What the kit tests learn (where litter clumps, what causes a stain, how a frontage fills) goes into the draft desk's rules and seeds (`gidi_kit/draft.py`, `legacy/blender/kit_draft.py`; *settled 24 Oct:* the draft desk stays in `legacy/blender/` (owner: "they should live in a legacy folder"); it is run as it is and its rules are not extended. What the old kit holds for the builders: `legacy/blender/WHAT-IT-HELD.md`). Those rules only write a first draft of a place's records; once written, the records are the truth and are edited by hand. Improving a rule never moves anything already in the map. When the owner asks, a district (or a smaller area) can be **re-drafted** with the improved rules: the new draft is shown side by side with the current one, hand-edited records are kept (never overwritten), and it replaces nothing until the owner says so.
- **The map format's four calls** (owner, 22 Oct: "What are you waiting for?", taking the recommendations in `docs/MAP-FORMAT.md`). (1) The board may have brushes that write many records in one stroke (litter, ground relief, a goods band); everything a brush writes is stored in full and editable one by one, so the map stays the one ground truth. (2) Small things (windows, stains, slabs, sacks, litter) are table rows inside their parent record, every value written out. (3) The dress layer moves to its own repo (`gidi-map-dress`, same paths) once it passes 1 GB. (4) All 51 districts get working echo names now (see IDEAS, "Place names"), changeable later.
- **People come from a kit too** (owner, 22 Oct: "do this kit situation for our people too"; "we're getting, like, real clothing... how the fashion of actual Lagos people are"). Lagos is a city of people: crowds are never cut for speed, they are made cheap (full detail near the camera, simpler in the middle, batched far away). (1) One shared skeleton for everyone: bodies vary (age, build, height, men, women, children), the bones do not, so every animation works on every person and nobody is rigged one by one. Start from what the project has: the City Sample (MetaHuman) skeleton and animations; crowd bodies from MakeHuman on that skeleton ("People" above), main characters' faces and bodies made in MetaHuman. (2) Clothing and what people carry are the kit: studied from a few hundred Street View shots plus the owner's pictures, listed by place, job and time (everyday wear, work clothes, uniforms, church and mosque, Sunday best, loads, bags, babies on backs), the list checked by the owner, pieces built in Blender (hard ones the danfo way through Meshy), each fitted to the shared skeleton. (3) Faces are never copied from real people (Street View blurs them anyway): made and mixed in MetaHuman, inspired, never a likeness. From the pictures only clothing, body types, stance and movement. (4) Passed by the owner's eye like every kit: a real Street View crowd rebuilt from the same camera, side by side. Order: after the old town building kit's first eye test.
- **Nothing is claimed without proof** (owner, 24 Oct: "we seem to be having a lot of problems with the scripts lying, or the agents lying to reach an outcome"; the process from the owner's stride-web repo). A lasting fix, not an instruction: hooks in `.claude/` run for every session in gidi and gidi-game. (1) The checks (`check_rules.py`, `check_map.py map`, a changed line naming a file that exists nowhere; in gidi-game `tools/kit_fields.py`) run before every commit and push, and before a turn ends with code changed; a failure blocks, and a check that can't run blocks too. (2) Every commit that changes code or the map says in a `Checked:` paragraph what was run or looked at and what it showed, in numbers; what was judged by eye says "by eye"; what wasn't checked says so. (3) Before every push a second model reads the rules, the messages and the diff, and blocks a claim with nothing behind it, a check that passes on missing input or was loosened to pass, a GAP turned PASS without a real check, and a rule broken that no script can check; a `Review:` paragraph answers a finding it got wrong. (4) A check never says FAIL on the expected state: what is still to fill is counted apart (check_map's TODO line), so FAIL always means something is wrong. (5) An agent's report is a claim like any other: checked before it is passed on.
- **The new map is the truth** (owner, 24 Oct: "the Gidi old map problem, let's clean up, the new map is the truth"). The generated city of 13-21 Oct (the old map) is history: new work never reads it, and it leaves the live surface of the repos (into `legacy/`, as `legacy/blender/` did) so nothing new is built on it by mistake. Its laptop test (gidi-game `tools/perf.sh`) is removed (gidi-game #70). Gidi's data store comes out of World Drive's repo into its own place, `~/gidi-data` (done 24 Oct).
- **Rebuild the old kit's things the new way** (owner, 24 Oct, after seeing the old kit's renders: "the kits did not do a very bad job to begin with. The only problem is it looks like toys... it probably does not have the object feeling that our current kits have"; "see the things that we have not built and try to improve them... on our current system of kit modeling... textures and all of that, the object process"; then: look at photos to see how close the kits are to the real objects and how to improve them, not just port the old kits). Everything the old Blender kit made that no builder makes yet is rebuilt as a builder in `gidi-game/tools/`, the current way. First the old render is set beside real photos of the thing (Google study shots, Street View, `research/refs/`) and the gaps are written down: what is missing, wrong in size or shape, too clean, too flat. Then study the real thing in the Google study shots and Street View, model it whole and clean as an object, judge it alone on a turntable sheet and in close-ups of its joints, then textures and the wear layer, driven by its map record. The old function is read only for its list of parts and its research note, never for its mesh: nothing is ported. The builder's sheet shows the new model beside the same photos, and each gap noted at the start says whether it is closed. The list and its order: `legacy/blender/WHAT-IT-HELD.md` (what the map's records need first, then the old kit's other things); a row is crossed off there when its builder passes the owner's eye.
